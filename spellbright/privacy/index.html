Spellbright privacy policy

Last updated 28 November 2026.

Spellbright is a word game for children aged 4 to 12, played in a browser at carrierpress.com/spellbright/app. This policy says what it keeps, where it keeps it, and how the ads in the free game work.

No account, no chat, no strangers

There is no sign in, no email address and no password. A child never types a name: the first screen asks only how old the player is, and the game gives them a made-up name such as Purple Comet 42. There is no chat and no way to message anyone. Every game is played against the game's own robots or with people on the same device, and Spellbright never connects one player to another.

What stays in your browser

These are kept in that browser's own storage for carrierpress.com, on that device, and nowhere else:

  • The player: the age picked (a number from 4 to 12, or Grown-up) and the made-up name. Never a birthday.
  • This week's spelling words, if a grown-up adds them: the words, the day the list last changed, and for each word how many times it was tried, how many times it was right and how many times in a row.
  • The adventure map: which stops have been cleared.
  • Spelling groups, if the device joins any (up to six): each group's name and code, so a child can switch between them, and the words and note of the group playing now.

None of it is sent to us or to anyone else (a group's code is the one thing a device sends, below). Another browser or device cannot see it. In a private window it lasts only until the window closes.

Reading aloud

Words and their meanings are read aloud from recorded clips: sound files the game fetches from carrierpress.com as it needs them, the way a page fetches a picture. Nothing about the player goes with the request. A word without a recording is read by a voice your browser provides, and the game picks the most natural English voice the browser has. In some browsers the most natural voices are online services run by the browser's maker, which then receives the text being read.

Say It: the microphone

Say It lets a child spell a word or read a sentence out loud and hear whether they got it right. It is off until a grown-up turns it on, on This week's words, and while it is off the game never asks for the microphone.

  • The voice never leaves the device. Say It never uses an online speech service. Where the browser has its own on-device speech recognition (Chrome on a computer or Chromebook), Say It uses that, which by its standard must run on the device itself; the game checks the browser offers it and never falls back to an online service. Everywhere else (Safari, iPhone, iPad, Firefox, Android) Say It uses its own small speech model, which runs inside the page on the device. Browsers that can do neither get no microphone, and Say It says so.
  • Nothing is recorded or kept. No audio is saved, no transcript is saved, and nothing heard is sent to us or to anyone else. The game keeps only what practice already keeps: for this week's words, whether the try was right.
  • On only when tapped. The microphone turns on when the child taps it and off after a pause, a right answer, another tap, 20 seconds, leaving the screen or switching tabs. A red Microphone on light shows the whole time.
  • The speech files. The first time, the device needs a one-time download. In Chrome, the browser fetches its English speech pack, about 60 MB, from Google, the way it fetches any update. In other browsers, a grown-up taps Get the speech model and the game downloads its own model, about 56 MB, from carrierpress.com (an open speech model, Whisper tiny, made by OpenAI and shared under the Apache 2.0 license). It is kept in the browser's storage on the device, like the game's other files, and can be removed by clearing this site's data. Nothing about the player goes with either download.

The switch itself (on or off) is kept in this browser's storage with the rest of the list above.

The ads in the free game

The free game is paid for by a short ad now and then, from Google (AdSense H5 Games Ads).

When. Only at a natural break, after a finished puzzle, round or game. Never during a turn, never in front of Help, never in spelling practice and never on a grown-ups screen. None in the first two minutes after the game opens, and never two within three minutes. If an ad does not load, the game carries on.

Marked for a child, and not personalised. Google's ad script is loaded with Google's tag for child treatment switched on, and the game asks Google for ads that are not personalised. Together these tell Google that the ad is shown in a game for children and must not be chosen from a profile of the person playing.

What Google receives. When ads are shown, the browser loads Google's script from Google's servers and asks Google for an ad. Like any request to any website, that shows Google the browser's address and the details a browser sends with every request, and Google handles them under its own privacy policy. The game gives Google nothing it keeps: not the age, the name, the spelling words or the adventure map.

No ads with a membership. Before Google's script is loaded, the game asks our own server one question: does this browser hold a live Spellbright key, or a Carrier Press Access key on the Apps or All Access tier? The answer is yes or no. If yes, Google's script is never loaded and nothing is asked of Google. If the membership ends, the ads come back and the game keeps working.

The key check

The membership's license key is entered on Spellbright's page, carrierpress.com/apps/spellbright. It goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. If it is, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that the ads come back when a membership ends or a purchase is refunded. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors. The page also keeps a copy of the key in your browser's storage, so it does not have to be pasted twice. Remove my key from this browser, on Spellbright's page, deletes the cookie; clearing the site's data, below, removes the rest.

Spelling groups

A teacher, homeschool parent or tutor with a group plan can make spelling groups. Each group has a name, up to 30 words and an 8-character code.

What a child's device sends. To join, the code the child types, and nothing else: no name, no age, no account and no email. Our server answers with the group's name and words, and the browser keeps the code in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, so it can fetch the words again when the grown-up changes them. Practice results never leave the device. The address a join comes from is used for one minute to limit wrong tries, and we keep no record of it.

Cheers. After practising, a child can send the group one of five emoji, once a day. Only the emoji goes: our server adds one to that group's count for the day. The cookie remembers which day this device last sent one, for each group, and nothing else.

What our server keeps. For each group: its code, its name, its words, the grown-up's note, when it was made and when the words last changed, how many devices joined it each day and how many of each cheer it got each day (both kept for 14 days, as numbers only). For each group plan: a fingerprint of its license key, an encrypted copy of the key so the server can ask Gumroad once a day whether the plan is still paid, and the answer. It also counts wrong codes per hour, from everyone together, to stop guessing. Nothing about a child is kept: not a name, an age, a score or anything that tells one device from another.

What the grown-up sees. Their groups, codes, words and notes, how many devices joined each group this week, and how many of each cheer it got. Never who joined, who sent a cheer, or how anyone did. A note can't contain a link, an email address or a phone number, and goes through the same word check as the spelling words.

Sending the code. "Send the code" opens the grown-up's own share sheet, email or messages app with a message they can read and change, and they choose who gets it. Spellbright sends nothing and never sees who it goes to. The printed code slips are made in the grown-up's own browser. The grown-up's browser keeps the plan key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read.

Removing it. Leaving a group, in Spellbright, deletes the code from the device and its cookie, and the device's own list comes back when no group is left. Deleting a group removes its code, name, words, note and counts from our server. To have a plan's record removed too, ask through Spellbright's support page.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. Spellbright never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

Nothing else

Spellbright has no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking software of its own. Apart from loading its own pages and files, the voice clips, the key question, a spelling group's code and words, Say It's speech files, and the ads described above, it makes no requests. The grown-ups screen has a link to Spellbright's page on carrierpress.com, and nothing is sent when it opens.

On the web, carrierpress.com, this app's own pages included, counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics. For each page it sees the page address, the page you came from, your browser and device type, and how long the page took to load. It sets no cookies, and it does not see anything you type or save in the app.

One exception: when some games and the certificate open, the page address carries the player's name and age as entered in the game, so the visit count can see them. A spelling group's join link also carries the group's code in its address.

Children

Spellbright is made for children, and it asks a child for nothing but an age, and in a spelling group, the group's code. With Say It on, a child's voice is heard on the device and never recorded, kept or sent. We never contact a child, nothing the game keeps about a child is sent to an advertiser, and none of it is sold to anybody.

Deleting it

A grown-up can remove a word from the spelling list, which also removes its results. To remove everything, clear this site's data in your browser's settings: that deletes the player, the spelling list, the adventure map and any group. It also clears what the other Carrier Press web apps keep here, because they share carrierpress.com. There is no account to close and nothing of yours on a server of ours to remove. Gumroad keeps its own record of a purchase.
