Sprout No. 1 · 20 November 2026

Two questions to your next book

Welcome to the first issue. Sprout comes out every Friday and covers what grew this week on both sides of the house: the books from Carrier Press, and the apps from Carrier Labs. It should take about five minutes.

This week: find your next book in two questions

There are more than seventy books on the shelves now, and a long shelf is hard to browse. So there is a new page that does the browsing for you. Find your next book asks two things: who the book is for, and what kind of story they like. Then it gives you one book to start with and two more in case that one is not quite it. No account, no quiz score. If it is a gift, the buy button goes straight to the hardcover where there is one.

From the Press

  • Watch before you read. Seven book pages now open with a short video explainer, made with Google NotebookLM from the book itself. The King in Yellow was first. They are free to watch until 30 November.
  • For members. Readers on Carrier Press Access can now hear all 79 episodes of the podcast series on Twelve Minutes on River Road, and watch the members' videos for it and for Ambrose Bierce's Can Such Things Be?
  • From the Journal. M. R. James and the ghost story at Christmas explains why the English ghost story belongs to December, with three of his stories to read aloud on Christmas Eve.
  • Dressed for the season. The site has put on its holiday look, and the gift guide groups the books by the reader you are buying for.

From the Labs

Nine Carrier Labs apps now run in a web browser, on any computer or phone. No download and no app store, just a page and a key.

  • Through the Gate shows what a resume parser actually keeps from your resume, and everything it throws away.
  • Vet Pocket keeps each pet's records in one place and helps you decide whether it is an emergency visit or can wait until Monday.
  • The Binder is the if-something-happens file: where the will is, which accounts exist, who to call. Locked with a passphrase only you know.
  • FirstDay turns the pile of school emails and PDFs into one calendar, one supply list and one form tracker.
  • Trade Desk is field service for one person with a van, with under sixty seconds of admin per job.
  • Pantry starts from what is already in the house and what runs out first, rather than from a recipe you have to shop for.
  • Fluent Hour is one spoken scenario a day, ten minutes. No streaks, no gems.
  • Homeroom measures a homeschool year against the family's own state rules and builds the portfolio as the year goes.
  • Stuck is homework help built so it cannot give the answer. It asks questions until the student finds it, and parents can read the whole session.

All of them are on the apps page with their prices and a sample you can try first.

On the bench

  • Next app on the web: Pill Proof, a shared log of who took which medicine and when, for the person keeping track of a household. It records. It does not advise.
  • A new series is coming. The Noise Floor opens The Concurrent, a new science fiction series. The page is up now; the book follows.
  • More Lovecraft. The second volume in the Lovecraft line, led by At the Mountains of Madness, is on its way to the stores. It carries the complete 1936 text of The Shadow over Innsmouth.

One to read

The King in Yellow by Robert W. Chambers. A play that drives its readers mad, a city that is not quite New York, and a yellow sign nobody should have seen. Written in 1895, it still reads like a dare. This restored, annotated edition is available on Amazon. The opening chapters are free if you want to try it first: send me the free opening.

That is the week. If something here would be useful to someone you know, send them the link. See you next Friday.
