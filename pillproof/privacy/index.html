PillProof privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026.

PillProof records that a dose was taken, and is usually paid for by an adult child on behalf of a parent. This policy explains what is kept and who can see it.

In this version a dose is confirmed in the app, not by photographing the pill. Each confirmation records the time and which account confirmed it, so a carer confirming on someone's behalf is recorded as the carer rather than as the person taking the medication. One reference photograph per medication is stored, so the person taking it can see what the pill should look like.

The person tracked is usually not the person who signed up

This is the part that matters most, so it is here rather than at the bottom. If you set PillProof up for a parent, you are creating a record of another adult's medication and daily behaviour, and they are the subject of it.

They are an adult and it is their health information. Set this up with them, not for them. A person old enough to take their own medication is old enough to be told that the times they took it are being sent to somebody else, and the app is designed to be visible on their device rather than hidden on it.

If the person cannot meaningfully agree to that, the decision you are making is a legal one about capacity and authority, and it is not one this app can make or verify for you.

What is stored

  • Your email address, and whether the subscription is active.
  • The household, its members, and who is the recipient.
  • The medications and schedules: names, doses and times.
  • Each dose: whether it was confirmed, and when, and who in the family recorded it.
  • Updates the family writes to each other about the person they care for, each marked as health, how they are, or day to day, with the name of whoever wrote it and whether somebody has done what it asked.
  • Appointments: what, when, where, any notes, and which family member said they would take them.
  • The name you choose to be shown to your family, if you set one. Your email address is never shown to other members of the household.
  • The device pairing between the two phones, and attempts to pair.

Photographs of pills

A photograph of each tablet is kept so whoever gives it can see what it looks like, and a dose can be recorded with a photograph, which is kept with that dose. Those images are of medication rather than of a person. They are not sent to a language model, are not used to identify anybody, and no face recognition of any kind exists in this app.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to an email address. No location and no movement tracking of any kind, which is deliberate: this app tells you a dose was taken, not where somebody is. No diagnosis, no clinical notes, no insurance details and no payment card details.

Who can see it

Members of the household you set up, and nobody else using the app. Every table checks the signed in account against the household before returning anything. Updates and appointments are visible to the members of the household; they are not shown on the phone of the person being cared for. There is no clinician view, no pharmacy access and no sharing outside the household. We do not sell any of it and we do not send it to advertisers.

Who else is involved

Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes. RevenueCat sits between the app and the app store and tracks whether your subscription is active, receiving the purchase events and an identifier for your account. Apple handles the payment itself; card details go to Apple and never to us.

Deleting it

Deleting the account removes the household, its members, the medications, schedules and every dose record, immediately and unrecoverably. The recipient can unpair their device at any time, which stops any further record being made.

The web version

PillProof in a browser, at carrierpress.com/pillproof/app, has no account, no database and no second device. Everything above about accounts, the phone of the person taking the medication, our storage provider, payments through Apple and deleting an account is about the phone app. In the browser there is one device, the carer's.

Your records stay in your browser. The people you look after, their medicines (name, dose and instructions as typed from the box) and the times and days each is taken, each dose that fell due and what happened to it (taken, with the time and the name chosen; skipped, with the reason and the name chosen; or not marked), the updates you write, the appointments, and the names offered when something is recorded are kept in that browser's storage for carrierpress.com, on that device. Nothing you record is sent to us or stored on any server.

Photos (a photo of each tablet, and any photo added when a dose is recorded) are made smaller in your browser and kept in its own storage on that device. They are pictures somebody chose to keep, not proof or verification of anything, and they are not sent anywhere.

A dose recorded in the browser says whose word it is by the name chosen there, such as "8:04am, Cal". It is never shown as coming from the phone of the person taking the medication, because on the web there is none.

The Updates feed is not shared live. Another browser or device cannot see anything here. The family see what the carer chooses to send them: Account offers a family summary (the medicines as typed, the last seven days of doses as recorded, anything still needing doing and the appointments coming up) to download as a text file or print. Once you send it to someone, it is theirs.

Your copy. Clearing the site's data in your browser erases everything, so Account offers Download a copy (everything, photos included, as one file saved to your device). Restore from a copy reads a downloaded copy in your browser and adds what is not already there; it never replaces anything, and the file is not sent anywhere.

No AI, no reminders. PillProof on the web makes no AI calls and sends no reminders or notifications of any kind. The only requests it makes are to carrierpress.com: to check your license key is still current, and, when you lock it, to forget the key.

Your license key. The web version opens only with a license key, from your Gumroad receipt or a Carrier Press Access key. When you paste it on this app's page, it goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. If it is, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that access ends when a purchase is refunded or a membership ends. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors.

If the subscription lapses on the web, PillProof still opens in that browser, today's list stays, and doses can still be marked taken or skipped, and undone. A lapse never stops either, and nothing you recorded is removed. Anything else that pauses until the key is current again says so in a single line.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. PillProof never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

To erase it, open Account, then Erase PillProof from this browser. That deletes everything PillProof kept in that browser, photos included. We cannot recover it, because we never had it. It does not cancel your Gumroad purchase; manage that from your Gumroad receipt. Lock PillProof in this browser forgets your key there and keeps everything for when you enter it again.

PillProof is not a medical device: it records, it does not advise. If something in it looks wrong, the box and the pharmacist are right.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
