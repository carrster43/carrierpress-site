Handoff privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026.

Handoff holds a co-parenting calendar, the expenses between two households, and a message log that can be exported. This policy explains what is kept and, above all, who else can see it.

The other parent sees the shared record. Assume everything is on the record.

This is the part that matters most, so it is here rather than at the bottom. When you join an arrangement, the other party sees the custody days, the expenses and every message in the log. That is the point of the app.

Messages cannot be edited or deleted once sent, by you or by them. That is deliberate, because a log that either side could quietly revise would be worthless in the situation it exists for. Write every message as though it will be read aloud in a courtroom, because it may be.

What the other parent sees of you

The display name you choose, beside your messages and expenses and in any export. Your email address is never shown to them, because an email address is a way to contact you and this app is not a way to get one. Until you set a display name, the other parent and any export show you as "Unnamed parent". Nothing else from your account, such as your subscription, is visible to them.

What an export contains

An export is the complete message log and expense record for the arrangement, with timestamps, in the order things happened. It is not filtered, and it is not your side only. Either party can produce one. We are not a party to your matter, we do not send anything to any court, and we take no position on how an export is used.

This is information about children

An arrangement names the children it concerns and records where they are on which days. We ask for as little as the app can work with: a name and, where a schedule needs it, a date of birth. There is no photograph, no school record and no location tracking of any kind. Children have no account here, we never contact them, and nothing about them is used for advertising in any form.

What is stored

  • Your email address, and whether your subscription is active.
  • The arrangement, the parties in it and the invites sent.
  • The children it concerns, and the custody days recorded.
  • Every message in the log, with the time it was sent.
  • Every expense: what it was for, the amount, and how it splits.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to your email address. No location, no phone number, no court file, no order document and no payment card details.

Who else is involved

Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes. RevenueCat sits between the app and the app store and tracks whether your subscription is active, receiving the purchase events and an identifier for your account. Apple handles the payment itself; card details go to Apple and never to us. Nothing in your arrangement is sent to a language model, and none of it reaches any of the three. We do not sell any of it and we do not send it to advertisers.

Deleting it

Deleting your account removes your own profile. It does not erase the shared arrangement, because the other party's copy of a joint record is not yours to delete, and a message log that vanished when one side left would defeat its purpose. Your messages remain in the log they were sent to, with your display name as it was when you wrote them. The expenses you raised remain too, labelled the same way, never with your email address.

Each of your messages also keeps the random account number it was sealed with. The log's tamper check is built over that number, and it is what lets the other parent prove the record was not altered. Once your account is deleted, it is not linked to any email address or account.

The web version

Handoff in a browser, at carrierpress.com/handoff/app, has no account, no email address and no sign in. Everything above about accounts, sign in codes, Supabase, RevenueCat, payments through Apple and receipt photographs is about the phone app. On the web, the shared record is held by a small server of ours on Cloudflare, one separate store for each arrangement, so that both parents see the same record and neither of them holds it.

What our server holds for each arrangement:

  • The arrangement's name, and when it was started.
  • Each parent's display name, the name the other parent sees, and when they joined. Each parent is known to the record by a random number, never an email address.
  • The children's first names, and a date of birth only if one is given.
  • The calendar: which parent each child is with on each day set, the reason when the usual pattern changes, and who set the day and when.
  • Every message, with the time, the name its author wrote it under, and the two hashes that link it to the message before.
  • Every expense: what it was for, the amount, the share asked, who raised it, and the other parent's answer and note.
  • Recovery codes and the key that started the arrangement, kept only as hashes. Neither can be read back from what is stored.
  • The invite code, which works once and for seven days. An unused one is kept until it is used or a new one replaces it; a used one is kept with the date it was used.
  • A count of how many changes the arrangement made each day, for the daily allowance, kept for seven days.

What it does not hold: no email address, no account, no password, no phone number, no location and no payment details. Receipt photographs are not kept on the web. Payments are processed by Gumroad, and Handoff never receives or stores your card.

Two cookies, both HttpOnly, so scripts on the page cannot read them. The key pass says this browser holds a current Handoff key: when you paste your license key on this app's page, our server asks Gumroad whether it is good, keeps the key in an encrypted cookie in your browser, and asks Gumroad again about once a day, so that it ends when a purchase is refunded or a membership ends. The party cookie says which parent you are in which arrangement, and it is what opens the record. The key pays; it never decides who you are.

Joining. The parent who starts the arrangement pays for it with their key. The other parent joins with the invite link, gives the name the other parent will see, and pays nothing to read the record or add to it. A key of their own is needed only to export it. Each parent is shown a recovery code once, when they start or join. It is the only way back into the record from another browser, and we cannot show it again: we keep only its hash.

Nothing in the record can be edited or deleted by either parent. No message, no expense and no answer to an expense can be changed or removed, by you, by the other parent, or by us on request. A calendar day can be set again, which replaces it and records who set it and when. Changing your display name changes only what you write after it: what you already wrote keeps the name it was sealed with.

An export is built in your browser from the whole record for the dates you choose, with no way to choose only some messages, and saved to your device as a text file. Once you send it to someone, it is theirs. Anyone can check one at carrierpress.com/handoff/verify/, which runs in their own browser and sends nothing anywhere.

No AI, no analytics, no advertising. Nothing either of you writes is sent to a language model, and there is no tracking script on any page of Handoff. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

If a subscription ends, both parents can still read the record and add to it. Exporting pauses until a key is current again.

Leaving. Account, then Leave this arrangement. Your place in it closes for good and your recovery code stops working. The record stays for the other parent, with what you wrote under the name you wrote it with, because the other parent's copy of a joint record is not yours to delete. An arrangement nobody is left in is erased, everything in it. Forget this browser only clears the party cookie there; your place and the record are untouched, and your recovery code brings you back.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
