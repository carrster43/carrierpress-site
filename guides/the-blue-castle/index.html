The Blue Castle: discussion questions

10 questions for book clubs, classrooms and solo readers, with a L. M. Montgomery timeline, from the Reader's Guide in the Carrier Press annotated edition of The Blue Castle by L. M. Montgomery.

This guide is printed at the back of the Carrier Press annotated edition.

Buy the hardcoverAbout this edition

Questions for reflection and discussion

  1. Valancy's Blue Castle is an imaginary refuge she has kept since childhood. What do we escape into when the real world offers us too little, and when does that kind of escape help us, and when does it hold us back?
  2. The novel opens with Valancy at twenty-nine, unmarried and treated as a failure. How much of the pressure she feels is particular to her time and place, and how much of it would a reader recognize today?
  3. Valancy's transformation begins with a single piece of news. Why do you think it takes something so drastic to free her, and what does that suggest about how she has been living?
  4. The Stirling clan rules Valancy through custom, gossip, and small daily corrections rather than open cruelty. Why is that kind of control so hard to resist? Which of the relatives did you find the most maddening, and why?
  5. John Foster's nature writing gives Valancy a language for what she wants before she can name it. Have you ever encountered a book, a place, or a voice that did that for you?
  6. The natural world of Muskoka is almost a character in the novel. What role does the wilderness play in Valancy's recovery, and why might Montgomery have set this particular story outside her beloved Prince Edward Island?
  7. Roaring Abel and Cissy Gay are the neighbors respectable Deerwood shuns. How does Valancy's treatment of them measure her change, and what is the novel saying about respectability?
  8. The book borrows the shape of a fairy tale, complete with a locked room and a mysterious stranger. Did that structure add to your pleasure, or did you find yourself resisting it? Did the ending feel earned?
  9. Montgomery is often remembered only for Anne of Green Gables. Having read this, how would you describe what she can do as a writer for adults?
  10. Valancy decides that a short life lived fully is worth more than a long one spent obeying. Do you agree? What would your own version of "living fully" require you to risk?

L. M. Montgomery: a brief timeline

1874
Born Lucy Maud Montgomery in Clifton (New London), Prince Edward Island; raised largely by her maternal grandparents in Cavendish.
1890s
Trains and works as a teacher; studies at Prince of Wales College and Dalhousie University; begins publishing poems and short stories.
1908
Publishes Anne of Green Gables to immediate and enduring international success.
1911
Marries the Reverend Ewan Macdonald; moves to Ontario, where she spends most of the rest of her life.
1922
Visits Bala, in the Muskoka lake district of Ontario, the landscape she will use for The Blue Castle.
1926
Publishes The Blue Castle, her fifteenth book and the only one set entirely outside Prince Edward Island.
1931
Publishes A Tangled Web, her second and last novel written for adults.
1942
Dies in Toronto. She leaves behind more than twenty novels, hundreds of stories and poems, and extensive private journals later published to wide acclaim.

If you loved The Blue Castle

Seek out Montgomery's other adult novel, A Tangled Web (1931), and her Emily trilogy, whose ambitious, writing-obsessed heroine is the closest thing in her work to a self-portrait.
