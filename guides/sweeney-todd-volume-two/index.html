Sweeney Todd: The String of Pearls: discussion questions

10 questions for book clubs, classrooms and solo readers, with a timeline, from the Reader's Guide in the Carrier Press annotated edition of Sweeney Todd: The String of Pearls by James Malcolm Rymer.

This guide is printed at the back of the Carrier Press annotated edition.

Buy the hardcoverAbout this edition

A short timeline and a set of discussion questions for readers and reading groups. The questions are spoiler-light but assume you have read, or are reading, the novel.

Questions for Discussion

  1. The story insists in its Preface that Sweeney Todd was a real man. Why do you think the author was so eager to have readers believe the tale was true, and why has that belief lasted so long?
  2. Todd's shop stands in the busiest, most respectable part of London, next to a church. How does the ordinariness of the setting make the horror more effective than a remote or gothic location would?
  3. Mrs. Lovett and her pies are as famous as the barber himself. What makes the idea of the pies so uniquely disturbing, and why has that particular horror proved so unforgettable?
  4. Tobias, the terrified apprentice, is one of the story's few sympathetic figures. How does the author use his fear and helplessness to build suspense?
  5. This is the long 1850 version, several times the length of the first serial. Where do you feel the story stretching or doubling back, and how much of that is a weakness and how much is simply the pleasure of a serial that never wants to end?
  6. The book was written for readers who bought it a penny part at a time. How does that shape the way each chapter is built, especially the way it ends? Compare it to a modern serial told an episode at a time.
  7. Johanna, who disguises herself to search for her lost sailor, is unusually active for a heroine of this kind. What do you make of her, and of the story's treatment of its women generally?
  8. The villains, Todd and Mrs. Lovett and Fogg, are drawn boldly and without much shading. Does the story lose anything by making them so purely wicked, or is that part of its power?
  9. The tale takes ordinary features of London life, a barber's chair, a pie shop, a church clock, an underground passage, and turns each into a source of dread. Which of these transformations works best for you, and why?
  10. Sweeney Todd has survived as melodrama, musical, and film long after his origins were forgotten. Having read the source, what do you think it is in this original story that has kept the Demon Barber alive for well over a century and a half?

A Sweeney Todd Timeline

1830s
Cheap steam printing and a newly literate urban public create a mass market for penny fiction. Edward Lloyd becomes the leading publisher of weekly "penny bloods."
1846-47
The String of Pearls: A Romance appears as a serial in Lloyd's The People's Periodical and Family Library, running across the winter and spring in eighteen weekly parts. This first, shorter version introduces Sweeney Todd, Tobias, Mrs. Lovett, and the secret of the pies.
1847
Even before the serial finishes, a stage version reaches the London theatre, beginning the character's long life on the boards.
1850
Lloyd issues the greatly expanded version reprinted in this edition, in penny weekly parts and then as a single long volume titled The String of Pearls: The Barber of Fleet Street. A Domestic Romance. It runs to well over a hundred and seventy short chapters.
Later nineteenth century
Sweeney Todd becomes a staple of cheap melodrama and touring theatre, and the phrase "the Demon Barber of Fleet Street" enters the language.
Twentieth century
The tale is filmed repeatedly, from silent versions onward, and treated as folklore as much as fiction.
1979
Stephen Sondheim and Hugh Wheeler's musical Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street opens on Broadway and wins the Tony Award for Best Musical, carrying the character to a vast new audience.
2007
Tim Burton's film adaptation of the Sondheim musical brings Todd to the cinema screen once more.
Today
Most people know Sweeney Todd through the musical and the films. The original penny-dreadful romance reprinted here is his true source.
