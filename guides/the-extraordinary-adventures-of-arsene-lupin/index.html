The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsene Lupin: discussion questions

10 questions for book clubs, classrooms and solo readers, with a Maurice Leblanc timeline, from the Reader's Guide in the Carrier Press annotated edition of The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsene Lupin by Maurice Leblanc.

This guide is printed at the back of the Carrier Press annotated edition.

Buy the hardcoverAbout this edition

Questions for reflection and discussion

  1. Lupin is a criminal, yet Leblanc makes us root for him from the first page. How does he do it? What lines does Lupin never cross, and how important are those limits to our sympathy?
  2. Several stories are told by a friend to whom Lupin later confides his exploits. How does that narrator change the way the tricks land? Did you enjoy being fooled, or did you find yourself trying to spot the disguise in advance?
  3. The official police, embodied by Inspector Ganimard, are almost always a step behind. What is Leblanc saying about authority, cleverness, and the law by letting his thief win so often?
  4. Lupin announces his crimes in advance and leaves his calling card behind. Why does he take the risk? What does that need for an audience reveal about him?
  5. These are detective stories told from the criminal's side. How does that inversion change the usual pleasures of the genre? Which did you prefer, the puzzle or the performance?
  6. The final story brings Sherlock Holmes to Paris to face Lupin. Whose methods did you find more appealing, the Englishman's cold logic or the Frenchman's improvisation, and did the contest feel fair?
  7. Lupin is a distinctly French hero, proud, gallant, theatrical. How much of his charm is bound up with his nationality and his era, and how much of it travels?
  8. Written for a popular magazine, the stories are models of economy and surprise. What techniques did you notice Leblanc using to hook you and to keep a twist in reserve?
  9. A hundred years of adaptations have kept Lupin alive far beyond the page. Having read the originals, what do you think it is about the character that each new generation keeps reaching for?
  10. Which of the nine adventures was your favorite, and why? What would a modern Lupin steal, and from whom?

Maurice Leblanc: a brief timeline

1864
Born Maurice Marie Émile Leblanc in Rouen, France.
1880s to 1890s
Works as a journalist and publishes literary novels and stories, admired by a few but read by few; hopes to be recognized as a serious author.
1905
At the request of publisher Pierre Lafitte, writes "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin" for the new magazine Je sais tout. It is an immediate sensation.
1907
The first stories are collected as Arsène Lupin, gentleman-cambrioleur, the book translated here.
1908 onward
Publishes further collections and novels, including full-length duels between Lupin and a lightly disguised Sherlock Holmes, after Conan Doyle's objection.
1900s to 1930s
Becomes one of France's most popular writers, producing some twenty Lupin volumes along with plays and other fiction, while remaining privately ambivalent about the fame his rogue had brought him.
1941
Dies in Perpignan, France. Arsène Lupin long outlives him, thriving in film, television, comics, and translation around the world.

If you loved The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsène Lupin

Seek out the further Lupin books, in which the thief matches wits with his disguised English rival and grows into a fuller, stranger figure. For the character's English cousin, try E. W. Hornung's Raffles stories, and for the detective on the other side of the Channel, the Sherlock Holmes canon that Leblanc so cheerfully raided.
