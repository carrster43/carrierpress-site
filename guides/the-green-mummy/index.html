The Green Mummy: discussion questions

10 questions for book clubs, classrooms and solo readers, with a Fergus Hume timeline, from the Reader's Guide in the Carrier Press annotated edition of The Green Mummy by Fergus Hume.

This guide is printed at the back of the Carrier Press annotated edition.

Buy on AmazonAbout this edition

Questions for reflection and discussion

  1. The Green Mummy was written by a man who had, briefly, been the best-selling mystery author in the world, and who then spent decades trying to repeat that success. Does the novel read like the work of a confident popular craftsman? What signs of that professionalism did you notice?
  2. Professor Braddock cares for nothing but "the civilization of the past" and is happy to overlook a great deal in pursuit of his mummy. Is he a figure of satire, of sympathy, or both? How does Hume use him to comment on obsession?
  3. The mummy at the center of the mystery is Peruvian, not Egyptian, yet the whole atmosphere of the book trades on the Edwardian craze for ancient Egypt. Why do you think Hume chose that twist, and how does it change the flavor of the story?
  4. Unlike many later mystery writers, Hume keeps the supernatural firmly offstage: the mummy's menace turns out to be entirely human. Did you find that more or less satisfying than a story with a genuine curse? Why?
  5. Money, or the lack of it, drives nearly every character, from the lovers kept apart by want of a fortune to the killer's motive. What is the novel saying about the power of money in Edwardian society?
  6. Hume assembles a large cast, a widow, a sea captain, a Peruvian claimant, a besotted suitor, an obsessed scholar. Which character held your attention most, and did any of them surprise you by the end?
  7. The book was written in 1908, before the "golden age" of the detective novel. Where did you feel it reaching toward the modern whodunit of clues and deduction, and where did it stay rooted in the older tradition of coincidence and sensation?
  8. The story carries the casual assumptions of its era about class, foreignness, and the exotic. How did those attitudes strike you as a modern reader, and how should a reissue like this one handle them?
  9. Hume was once outsold by no one and is now almost entirely forgotten, while writers who came after him are famous. What do you think allows some popular authors to last and others to vanish?
  10. If The Green Mummy were the first Fergus Hume novel you had ever read, would it make you want to seek out more? What did it do well enough to earn a second visit?

Fergus Hume: a brief timeline

1859
Born Fergusson Wright Hume on 8 July in Powick, Worcestershire, England.
1860s
Emigrates as a child with his family to Dunedin, New Zealand, where he is educated.
1880s
Studies law at the University of Otago; admitted to the New Zealand bar in 1885; moves to Melbourne, Australia, to work as a barristers' clerk while trying to write for the stage.
1886
Publishes The Mystery of a Hansom Cab at his own expense in Melbourne. It becomes the best-selling mystery novel of the Victorian era; Hume, having sold the rights outright for fifty pounds, sees almost none of the profit.
1888
Returns to England and settles at Thundersley in Essex, where he will live and write for the rest of his life.
1890s to 1900s
Produces a steady stream of detective novels and thrillers, never recapturing the sales of his first book but remaining a busy and popular author.
1908
Publishes The Green Mummy, one of his many Edwardian mysteries.
1932
Dies on 12 July at Thundersley, Essex, having written well over a hundred novels across his career.

If you enjoyed The Green Mummy

Seek out Hume's landmark first novel, The Mystery of a Hansom Cab (1886), the runaway bestseller that helped invent the modern detective thriller.
