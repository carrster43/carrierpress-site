The Secret of Chimneys: discussion questions

10 questions for book clubs, classrooms and solo readers, with a Agatha Christie timeline, from the Reader's Guide in the Carrier Press annotated edition of The Secret of Chimneys by Agatha Christie.

This guide is printed at the back of the Carrier Press annotated edition.

Buy the hardcoverAbout this edition

Questions for reflection and discussion

  1. The Secret of Chimneys is a thriller rather than a fair-play puzzle. How did reading it change your expectations of Agatha Christie? Did you miss the tight deductive structure of her Poirot novels, or enjoy the change of pace?
  2. Anthony Cade is a charming, capable adventurer who drifts into the plot almost by accident. What makes him an effective hero for this kind of story, and how does he compare with the brilliant detective figures Christie is better known for?
  3. The Herzoslovakian material is a knowing parody of the "Ruritanian romance." Where did you feel Christie sending up the conventions of the imaginary-kingdom adventure, and where did she seem to be playing them straight?
  4. Superintendent Battle, making his first appearance here, solves problems by patience and ordinariness rather than flamboyant genius. Why do you think Christie created such a deliberately unremarkable detective, and did he work for you?
  5. The novel is full of coincidence, disguise, and improbability. Did the sheer pace and wit carry you past the implausibilities, or did they strain your patience? What is the contract between this kind of thriller and its reader?
  6. Christie juggles a large cast, adventurers, diplomats, financiers, aristocrats, a master criminal, and a house full of guests. Which characters held your attention, and did any of them turn out to be more (or less) than they first appeared?
  7. The country-house house party would become one of Christie's signature settings. How does she use the great house and its assembled guests here, and what does the setting contribute to the mood of the book?
  8. The book carries the casual assumptions of nineteen-twenties popular fiction about foreigners, class, and money. How did those attitudes strike you as a modern reader, and how should a reissue handle them?
  9. Chimneys was written the year before The Murder of Roger Ackroyd, the novel that made Christie world-famous. Having read this lighter entertainment, what signs did you see of the writer capable of that landmark puzzle?
  10. If this were your first Agatha Christie, would it make you want to read more? What did it do well enough to earn a second visit, and what did it teach you about the range of a writer often reduced to a single formula?

Agatha Christie: a brief timeline (to 1925)

1890
Born Agatha Mary Clarissa Miller on 15 September in Torquay, Devon, England.
1914
Marries Archibald Christie; during the First World War works as a hospital dispenser, gaining the knowledge of poisons that would run through her fiction.
1920
Publishes her first novel, The Mysterious Affair at Styles, introducing the Belgian detective Hercule Poirot.
1922
Publishes The Secret Adversary, launching the adventuring couple Tommy and Tuppence and her lighter thriller vein; travels the world on a promotional tour for the British Empire Exhibition.
1923
The Murder on the Links, her second Poirot novel.
1924
The Man in the Brown Suit, another international thriller, and Poirot Investigates, a story collection.
1925
Publishes The Secret of Chimneys, introducing Superintendent Battle.
1926
Publishes The Murder of Roger Ackroyd, the novel that makes her internationally famous, in the same year as the marital crisis and famous eleven-day disappearance that made headlines around the world.

If you enjoyed The Secret of Chimneys

Seek out Christie's other light thrillers of the period, The Secret Adversary (1922) and The Man in the Brown Suit (1924), and the sequel that returns to this same great house, The Seven Dials Mystery (1929). Superintendent Battle goes on to star in Cards on the Table and Towards Zero.
