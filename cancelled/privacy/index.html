Canceled privacy policy

Last updated 5 November 2026.

Canceled finds the subscriptions you are still paying for and helps you end the ones you do not want. From the same statements it also shows where the rest of your money goes, where you could cut, and what an emergency fund would take. This policy explains what is kept and what is not, in the iPhone app and in the web version. The promise is the same in both. The web version keeps things in your browser instead of on a phone, and the section called "The web version, exactly" says everywhere that changes the details.

Your bank is not connected, and that is the design

Canceled does not link to your bank and never asks for banking credentials. It reads a CSV statement that you download from your bank yourself and choose to open in the app, and that file is parsed on your device. The recurring charges it finds are found by that parsing, not typed in by you. Nothing from the file is uploaded. That makes it less automatic than the alternatives and it is the reason the app can promise what the rest of this page promises.

There is no account

You do not sign in, because there is nothing to sign in to. This app has no database of ours, no cloud sync and no backend for your data. Everything it knows is written to your own device's storage, a phone's or a browser's, and stays there. The one exception is a license key on the web, described below.

Earlier versions of this app did have an account, reached by a code sent to your email address, and a hosted database behind it. Both were removed on 21 September 2026 along with the email address they needed. There is no longer anywhere for us to keep anything about you.

What is stored, on your device

  • Each charge you choose to track: the merchant as your statement names it, the name shown for it, how much it is, how often it bills, when it was last seen, and whatever status you have given it.
  • The charges you marked "Not a subscription": the merchant as your statement names it, the name shown for it and the amount, so they stay hidden in the statements you open later. Show again removes one.
  • The regular payments you counted as subscriptions: when you choose "Count it as a subscription" on a payment from your bank account, its name as your statement gives it, the name shown and the amount, so it is counted in the statements you open later. Stop counting it removes one.
  • A short summary of each import, so the next statement you open can be compared with the last one (what stopped billing, what that has saved, anything you canceled that is still charging, and your savings toward an emergency fund). Each summary holds three things: the recurring charges found (the name, the amount, how often it bills and when it last billed, the same details a tracked charge holds), the total moved to savings in each month, and your average essential spending a month. The app keeps at most 24 of these. It is written when you open a statement, without a separate tap, because comparing two months is what it is for.
  • The payments you marked as essential or not: when you switch a regular payment on or off under Emergency fund or under Left out as money moved, answer one under Charges to check ("A bill I need" or "A plan I could stop"), or mark one as a card bill or a transfer to leave out, the payee's name as the app keys it and your choice (count it, do not count it, or leave it out), so the fund is sized the same way next time.
  • Whether reminders are on: two switches, one for a reminder before a yearly charge renews and one for a monthly reminder to open your new statement.
  • Whether the app is unlocked, so it does not have to ask the App Store every time it opens.
  • When the app last asked for a rating, described below.

That is the whole list for the iPhone app, in your phone's own storage. The Account screen there lists the same eight things under "Kept on this phone, and nowhere else".

What is not stored

No email address, no name, no password and no account, because there is no account. No bank login, no account number and no card number, because the app never asks for any of them. No statement: the file you open is read and worked out on your device, and neither the file nor the transactions in it are kept. Your purchases at shops, restaurants and the like, your rent and your bills are used to show the Spending screen while the statement is open, and are never written anywhere. The only things kept from a statement are the import summary above (recurring charges, monthly savings totals and average essential spending), and the charges you choose to track, hide or count. (On iPhone, the Files picker hands the app a temporary copy of the file; the app deletes that copy the moment it has read it.)

Nothing above is ever uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.

Reminders

Reminders are in the iPhone app only. They are off until you switch one on, and iOS asks your permission the first time. They are local notifications: your phone schedules them for itself and shows them itself. There is no push server, no device token is created and nothing is registered with anyone. A renewal reminder says the name and amount of a yearly charge a week before it is due; the monthly reminder says it is time to open your new statement. Turning a switch off, or Erase everything, removes the reminders already scheduled.

Who else is involved

In the iPhone app, Apple and nobody else. The unlock is a one-time purchase made through the App Store, so Apple handles the payment and tells the app only that the purchase exists. Apple never receives anything you entered, including the charges you track.

In the web version, Gumroad, for the key. The unlock is bought from Gumroad, which sends you a receipt with a license key. When you paste the key in, your browser sends the key and Canceled's product ID to Gumroad to check it and, if that does not match, the key and the Carrier Press Access product ID, and nothing else: no statement, no charge and nothing you entered. Gumroad's answer is read for whether the key is good; nothing else from it is kept. Statements opened in the web version are read inside your browser tab in the same way, and nothing from them is sent anywhere.

Canceled is a one-time purchase and not a subscription, which is deliberate: an app about ending recurring charges should not add one. Carrier Press Access is a separate, optional monthly membership sold on Gumroad, and its key also opens the web version.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking software of any kind. There is no language model. The only server of ours is the key check on the web, described below, and it receives nothing but your key.

When you follow a link

When the app shows you where to cancel a subscription, that is a link to the merchant's own page. Tapping it opens your browser, and what happens then is between you and that company under their policy. The app sends nothing with you and is not told that you went. The same is true of the link to this page and of the support email address.

Deleting it

Everything is on your device, so you control all of it. The account screen has a button that erases every charge you were tracking, the list of charges you hid, the payments you counted, every import summary and your essential choices, turns off and removes any reminders, and closes any statement that is open, immediately and unrecoverably. Deleting the iPhone app removes the same data along with it. Neither needs to ask us, because we do not have a copy.

In the iPhone app, your unlock is held by Apple against your Apple ID rather than by us, so it survives both and can be restored from the account screen.

The web version, exactly

The web version at carrierpress.com/cancelled/app does everything above inside your browser. These are the places where it differs.

  • Loading the page. Your browser downloads the page's files from carrierpress.com, which is hosted on GitHub Pages, as it would for any website. The host sees what any web host sees, such as your IP address. That is over before you choose a file, and nothing you do in the app is part of it.
  • Reading a statement. The browser hands the file you choose to the page, inside your tab. It is read into memory, not uploaded and not written to disk by the app, and closing or reloading the tab forgets it. Only the import summary described above is kept.
  • How to check that. With the page loaded, turn off your Wi-Fi and mobile data, then open a statement: it reads the same with no connection at all. Or watch the Network tab of your browser's developer tools while you open one: nothing goes out to any site. At most you will see your file at a blob: address, which is the tab reading from its own memory.
  • Where things are kept. In your browser's local storage for carrierpress.com, not in an app on a phone. That means the first five items in the list above (the charges you track, hide and count, the import summaries and your essential choices), plus a note saying Canceled is unlocked (unlocked:cancelled, set to 1 once a key checks out). The Account screen lists them under "Kept in this browser, and nowhere else".
  • License keys. A one-time key is kept only in the encrypted cookie described under The key check on the web, and the app's own storage keeps only that note. A Carrier Press Access key is kept (carrierPressLicense: the key, its product, when Gumroad last said yes and when it was last asked), with a second note saying the membership opened Canceled (unlocked:cancelled:access). About once a day when you open the app, your browser sends that key and the membership's product ID to Gumroad to confirm it. If Gumroad cannot be reached, the membership stays good for 7 days from its last yes. If Gumroad says the membership ended, a renewal failed or the purchase went back, the key is dropped and Canceled locks again.
  • One address, one storage. All the Carrier Press web apps live at carrierpress.com, and a browser gives one address one storage. A Carrier Press Access key is shared there on purpose, so one key opens them all. No other Carrier Press web app reads Canceled's lists.
  • What the web version does not have. No reminders, no rating prompt and no rating note. In Contact support, the subject line says "web browser" where a phone's system version would be.
  • Buying. Buy and Join links open Gumroad's own page. What you enter there, including how you pay, goes to Gumroad under its policy and never to Canceled.
  • Links to other sites. Your browser may tell a site you open from Canceled that you came from carrierpress.com, as browsers do. Nothing else goes with you.
  • Erasing and losing it. Erase everything in this browser, on the Account screen, empties the lists and summaries and closes any open statement. The empty lists and two blank reminder switches stay in storage, holding nothing. It leaves the unlock note and any Access key. To remove those too, clear this site's data in your browser's settings, which also clears any other Carrier Press web app's data in that browser; you can paste your key again from the Gumroad receipt. A private window, or clearing that data, loses your lists, and nobody can bring them back, because we do not have a copy.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.

The key check on the web

The web version opens only with a license key. When you paste your key on this app's page, it goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. Nothing else of yours goes with it.

If the key is good, your browser keeps it in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, with the times Gumroad last answered. The cookie goes to the same server whenever you open the app, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad about the key again, so that access ends when a subscription does. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors. The app may also keep the key in your browser's own storage, as this page describes. Remove my key from this browser, on the app's page, deletes both.

carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.
