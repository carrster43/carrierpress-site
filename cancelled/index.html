Canceled support

Last updated 5 November 2026.

Canceled reads a bank statement you exported yourself, finds the charges that repeat, ranks them by what they cost you in a year, and tells you where the cancel button actually is.

Why it asks for a file instead of connecting to my bank

There is no bank connection, and there is nowhere for your spending to go. You download a CSV from your own bank and open it here. The file is read on your device, and the parsing, the merchant matching and the detection all happen there. The rows are held in memory while the results are open and are never saved: Close these statements, or closing the app or the browser tab, forgets them. You can open several files at once, which is how Apple Card, one month per file, adds up to a year; a row that appears in two files counts once.

Nothing from a statement leaves the device at all. There is no account and no server behind this app. The subscriptions you choose to keep track of are saved on your device, and nowhere else.

It found nothing, or it missed something

  • The window is too short. One month shows each charge once, so nothing can be confirmed as monthly yet: those charges are listed under Seen once, at what they charged and with no yearly figure, until you add the previous month. An annual charge appears once in twelve months, which is indistinguishable from a one off purchase. The app tells you what your period could and could not show, so export twelve months or more where you can.
  • Rows were skipped. Unreadable rows are counted and shown, never dropped quietly.
  • The columns were not recognised. The app looks for a date, a description and an amount, by their column names or, when a bank leaves the header out or labels them unusually, by what the columns hold. If it still cannot tell, it says so and refuses the file rather than half reading it.

Why two different amounts were grouped as one charge

Because real billing drifts. A charge "on the 3rd" lands on the 5th when the 3rd is a Saturday, so a monthly charge is matched within a few days of its usual date. A price rise six months in is still the same subscription, so the run survives it, prices at the latest amount, and flags that it went up, because people do not notice. Descriptors drift too, so reference codes are stripped before anything is grouped and four months of one merchant do not read as four different merchants. Two plans at one company, such as two Spotify plans on a joint account, stay two subscriptions.

It listed something that is not a subscription

Open it and tap Not a subscription, or touch and hold it in the list. It leaves the total and stays hidden in every statement you open later. Hidden charges are listed at the bottom of Found, where Show again brings one back. Hiding is never part of the unlock.

Why something says "might be annual"

Because one charge cannot prove it. A single large charge could be an annual subscription or a one off purchase, and calling it a subscription would cost somebody a cancellation they did not want. It is surfaced separately, with the reason, and left to you. If you know it is yearly, tap it and choose Track as yearly.

The dates came out wrong

03/04/2026 is 3 April in Britain and 4 March in America. The order is decided across the whole file, and any day above 12 settles it. A completely ambiguous file falls back to month first, and the app says so on screen.

What it does not do

  • PDF statements. CSV only. A PDF reader that quietly misreads a column is worse than not offering one.
  • Canceling for you. It tells you where the button is. You press it.
  • Every company. The cancellation routes are written by hand for a short list of companies. Where one has not been researched, the app says so rather than guessing.

Cancellation flows are redesigned often and deliberately made awkward, so an instruction can go out of date. If one is wrong, tell us. One route worth knowing in advance: a charge billed through Apple or Google is canceled in the store's own settings, and canceling inside that company's app does nothing.

What is stored, and the unlock

There is no sign in, no email address and no password. For each subscription you tap to track, the app keeps the merchant name, the amount, how often it bills, when it was last seen, and what you decided to do about it, on your device. It also keeps the names and amounts of charges you hid as not a subscription, so they stay hidden, and of regular payments from your bank account you counted as a subscription, so they stay counted. Deciding to keep something is a first class answer.

See it before you pay. The Canceled page shows what it finds on an invented statement. In a browser the app opens only with a license key. In the iPhone app, every statement you open shows the recurring charges it finds and what each costs you a year, where to cancel the most expensive one the app has a route for, and one charge on your tracking list. The unlock shows where to cancel every charge the app knows and keeps as many as you like. It is a one time purchase rather than a subscription, deliberately: an app about ending recurring charges should not add one. The current price is shown on the unlock button, which in the iPhone app reads it from the App Store for your country.

Restoring a purchase

In the iPhone app, on a new phone or after a reinstall, tap Restore a purchase on the Account tab. If it reports nothing to restore, check that you are signed in with the same Apple Account that bought it.

Refunds

In the iPhone app, Apple is the merchant of record, so refunds are requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com. We cannot issue one. The web version is bought on Gumroad, not from Apple: write to support@carrierpress.com about a web purchase. A refunded key stops unlocking Canceled.

In the web version

  • Your statement stays in the browser tab. You can test that: with the page loaded, turn off your Wi-Fi and mobile data, then open a statement. It reads the same with no connection at all.
  • Your key. Pay once on Gumroad and the receipt carries a license key. Paste it on the Canceled page, under Already have a key?, in each browser you use Canceled in. Keep that email: you need the key again in any other browser. A Carrier Press Access key on the Apps or All Access tier opens Canceled too, for as long as the membership lasts.
  • Coming back later. Your tracking list and a short summary of your last statements stay in that browser. The Found tab shows them under Where you left off, and the Tracking tab has the whole list. The statements themselves are not kept, so open newer ones to see what has changed.
  • What can lose your list. It lives only in that browser. Clearing this site's data, or a private window once it closes, loses it, and we cannot bring it back, because we never had a copy. It does not follow you to another browser or device.
  • Reminders. A browser cannot remind you, so renewal and monthly reminders are in the iPhone app only.

Erasing everything

From the Account tab, tap Erase everything on this phone (in the web version it says in this browser). It deletes every subscription you were tracking, the list of charges you hid and the regular payments you counted, and closes any open statement, immediately and unrecoverably. Your statements were never uploaded, so there is nothing else of yours to remove. In the iPhone app your unlock is tied to your Apple Account, so it survives. In the web version your unlock stays in the browser, and your key still works.

Privacy

The full policy is at carrierpress.com/cancelled/privacy. There is no server of ours, so your list never leaves your device. Apple handles the purchase in the iPhone app, and Gumroad on the web. Nothing is sold, and no part of your list goes to advertisers or to a model.
