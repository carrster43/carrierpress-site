The English habit of telling ghost stories at Christmas is older than M. R. James. He is
the writer most people now mean by it.

James was a medievalist, Provost of King's College, Cambridge, and one of the great
cataloguers of old manuscripts. The ghost stories were a hobby. He said where they were
first heard in his own note to the 1904 collection: most of them "were read to friends at
Christmas-time at King's College, Cambridge."

That is still the right way to meet them. They were written for one voice and a quiet room,
and they work best that way. Nothing in them is loud. A scholar opens a book, or buys a
picture, or picks something up from the ground, and the rest follows.

# Three to read aloud on Christmas Eve

**The Mezzotint.** Start here if the room has never heard James. A university museum
curator is sent an engraving of a country house. It is a dull picture. Then someone looks
at it again, and something on the lawn has changed. Every time anyone looks, it has moved a
little further. That is the whole story, and it is enough.

**"Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You, My Lad".** A professor on an out-of-season golfing
holiday finds an old whistle in the ruins of a Templar preceptory, and blows it. There is a
second bed in his hotel room. Nobody is sleeping in it. Read this one last, with the lamps
low.

**Casting the Runes.** A man rejects a paper on alchemy, and its author does not forgive
him. Some time later he is handed a slip of paper he never notices taking, and the only
way out is to give it back. The cinema made this one into Night of the Demon. The story is
better.

# Where to start

[**Ghost Stories of an Antiquary**](/books/ghost-stories-of-an-antiquary/) is his first
collection, all eight stories of 1904 in the order he chose, including The Mezzotint and
"Oh, Whistle". It is the one to start with, and the one to give.

[**More Ghost Stories of an Antiquary**](/books/more-ghost-stories-of-an-antiquary/) holds
the next two collections entire, More Ghost Stories (1911) and A Thin Ghost and Others
(1919): twelve stories, including Casting the Runes and The Stalls of Barchester Cathedral.

Between them, the two volumes hold every story from his first three books. Each edition
adds an introduction, a headnote before every story and a glossary of the antiquarian world
the plots turn on, and keeps James's own prefatory notes, which most selected editions drop.
The text itself is never altered.

Both are available on Amazon. For the rest of the shelf James belongs to, from Chambers and
Machen to Blackwood, see [classic weird fiction and cosmic horror](/weird-fiction/).
