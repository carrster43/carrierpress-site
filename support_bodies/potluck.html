Potluck is for the two things a few blocks actually do together: organizing something, and lending something. Everybody on a street here got a card through their door from whoever opened it.

Potluck in your browser

Potluck is on sale now in a browser, from the Potluck page. The iPhone app is not out yet, so this is the version people use today. Neighbors never pay: whoever opens a street buys a key on Gumroad, not from Apple, and every other household joins free with a door card.

  • Joining with a door card. On the You tab, under Join with a door card, type the sixteen letters and numbers on the card, the name your neighbors know you by, and a landmark if you like. Each card opens the street for one household, and it expires 120 days after it was made. If the code is not recognized, or the card has expired, ask whoever runs your street for a new one. If it says the card has already been used and that was you, use your recovery code instead.
  • Your recovery code. It is shown once, when you join or open a street. Write it down or keep it in your password manager: it is the only way back onto your street from another browser, or if this one forgets you. Type it on You, under Already on a street?, and press Open my street.
  • There is no account. No email address and no password. The street itself is kept on our server, so it is not only in your browser: its name, each household's name and landmark, what is posted, who said they are coming, the lending shelf and loans, reports and blocks. Your browser holds an encrypted cookie that says which place on the street is yours. Clearing this site's data, a private window once it closes, or Safari after about a week without a visit loses that cookie, not the street, and your recovery code brings you back. A happening you plan before your street is open stays in your browser until you post it.
  • Keep a copy. On You, Download a copy saves everything you can see as a text file. It is always free, even if the street's membership ends.
  • Opening a street. Subscribe on Gumroad, monthly or yearly (the price is on the Potluck page), and the receipt email carries a license key. Paste it on the Potluck page, under Already have a key?, or on You and press Use my key, then Open my street. One key opens one street. You look after it: the Your street tab makes and prints the door cards and this week's flyer, and holds the reports. Keep the receipt email: in another browser, the same key and Open my street again bring you back. A Carrier Press Access key on the Apps or All Access tier opens a street too.
  • Reminders. Potluck sends no notifications, and a browser cannot remind you when it is closed. The This week tab shows what is coming.
  • Cancelling. The membership renews until you cancel. Cancel in your Gumroad library, or write to support@carrierpress.com and we will cancel it for you. When it ends, nothing is taken away: the street stays readable, and saying you are coming, returning things, reporting, blocking, leaving and the downloaded copy all still work, while new posts, new things to lend, door cards and the flyer pause. Restart the same membership from your Gumroad library and they come back. The street stays tied to the key that opened it, so a different key opens a new, empty street.
  • Refunds. Apple has nothing to do with a web purchase, so write to support@carrierpress.com about one.
  • Help. Help and contact, on You, opens an email to support@carrierpress.com.
  • Leaving a shared computer. Forget this browser, on You, stops this browser opening your street. You stay on the street, and your recovery code brings you back.

We do not have your address

This is worth being exact about, because "address verified" normally means the opposite. A door card carries a code and a number, and nothing else. We keep a scrambled form of each code, so we can tell it has been used, and nothing about which house it went to. Whoever opened the street may keep their own note of which house got which card number; that note stays in their browser and never reaches us. Potluck in a browser never asks where you are.

What neighbors see is the name you chose and a landmark if you set one. Change them on You with Change them; the landmark can be blank, and things you already posted keep the name they went up under.

Lending, and why there is no reminder button

Put something on the shelf from the Lending tab. To borrow a thing, have a word with whoever owns it, and they mark it out to you with Lend it out, due back in two weeks. Both of you see the same date, and either can mark it back: It is back on one side, I have given it back on the other. Nobody else sees a loan.

There is no "send a reminder", on purpose. What kills an informal tool library is not losing the ladder, it is that nobody wants to be the person who asks. The borrower already sees the same date, and neither side's wording blames anybody.

Reporting something

Harassment and anything unsafe hide the post immediately, before anyone has reviewed it. Spam, somebody selling something, somebody who is not a neighbor, and anything else are queued instead: the post stays up until whoever looks after the street decides, hidden from you meanwhile.

Three separate people reporting the same thing also hides it.

Whoever looks after your street is the neighbor who opened it, not us, and whoever reported something is never named to anybody, them included. Their decision holds both ways: something taken down stays down, and something left up stays up even if more reports arrive.

It is not a place to report people

There is no suspicious-activity category anywhere in this app and there will not be one: the drift into being a crime feed is what turns neighbors into people watching each other. If somebody is in danger or a crime is happening, call 911. This is a few neighbors with a moderation queue and it cannot help with that.

Blocking

Blocking is separate from reporting and needs no reason: reporting says something should not be here, blocking says you do not want to deal with a person. The person you block is not told, because on a small street telling them is how a block becomes a doorstep conversation. You will not see their posts and they will not see yours, and Unblock on You undoes it whenever you like.

If you were removed from a street

Whoever looks after a street can remove a household. That browser stops opening the street, and the name stays on what was already posted. If that happened to you, or you cannot get back onto your street, write to support@carrierpress.com with the street's name and the name you used.

Leaving

Leave the street, on You, removes your place, the times you said you are coming, your blocks, and anything of yours on the lending shelf that is not out on loan. Rejoining needs a new door card. Whoever opened the street can come back with their key, and when the last household leaves, the whole street is erased.

Things you posted stay up with your name on them. A plan for Saturday's cleanup that vanished overnight leaves people turning up to nothing, and an anonymous one is worse. Take down anything you would rather remove before you go, with Remove this on a happening or Take it off on the lending shelf; the app says so when you press Leave.

In the iPhone app

The iPhone app is not out yet. In it, signing in will be a code emailed to you. There is no password, and no Facebook or Google sign-in: that would tie a real name and a face to a specific set of blocks, which is the join this app exists to avoid making.

Your data

In a browser, the street is kept on our server, which Cloudflare runs for us, as listed above, and your browser keeps the encrypted cookie for your place. Neighbors see a name and a landmark, never an address, and only the two sides of a loan see it. Payment is through Gumroad, by whoever opened the street only; we receive a license key, never card details, and we keep that key, encrypted, to check it with Gumroad about once a day. Nothing is sent to a language model. The app itself has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking software, and nothing is sold. The full policy, with the iPhone app's part, is at carrierpress.com/potluck/privacy.

On the web, carrierpress.com, this app's own pages included, counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics. For each page it sees the page address, the page you came from, your browser and device type, and how long the page took to load. It sets no cookies, and it does not see anything you type or save in the app.
