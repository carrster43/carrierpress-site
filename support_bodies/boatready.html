Boat Ready answers one question about one boat: does it carry what federal law requires? You describe the boat, and you get the carriage list for that exact boat with the rule behind every line.

Why an item appeared, or did not

Nothing in the list is general advice. Every line is produced by the boat you described: length overall, how many people are aboard, what drives it (an engine, sail or paddle, and which engine), whether you run inland or coastal, and the handful of toggles underneath. Change one of those and the list changes with it.

The toggles that move the most are enclosed engine or fuel space, fixed fire system, canoe or kayak, installed toilet and used after dark. If a line you expected is missing, that is usually the first place to look.

Why my boat needs no fire extinguisher

This is the commonest question. A boat with no engine at all, such as a kayak, a canoe or a sailboat with no motor, carries no federal extinguisher requirement. With an engine, the exemption is narrower than people remember: it needs under 26 feet, outboard power and no enclosed space where fuel or vapour can collect, all three at once. Miss any one and the requirement comes back.

Above that, the bands come from the federal table in 33 CFR 175.320: under 26 feet, 26 up to (not including) 40, and 40 to 65. A boat of exactly 26 feet sits in the second band and one of exactly 40 feet in the third. Over 65 feet the number goes by gross tonnage rather than length, and the app tells you how many 20-B units each tonnage needs. The ratings are the current 5-B and 20-B; extinguishers from model year 2017 and older may keep their B-I or B-II rating while they stay serviceable.

Why every answer ends with a line about your state

Boat Ready covers federal carriage requirements only. States routinely require more: an extinguisher where the federal rule does not, a different age for children's life jackets, a boating safety certificate. The app cannot know your state's rules, so it says so on every result rather than letting a short clean list read as "I am compliant". Check your state boating agency.

Where the rules come from

Every line carries the section of the Code of Federal Regulations it comes from, Titles 33 and 46. Where the federal boating safety manual, COMDTINST M16796.8A, still matches the current rule, its page is cited too. Nothing is written from memory and nothing is inferred, so you can check the source rather than taking the app's word for anything.

What Boat Ready is not

  • It is not an inspection and it is not legal advice. It is a reference tool.
  • It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the United States Coast Guard or the Coast Guard Auxiliary.
  • It covers recreational vessels and does not cover state rules. It cannot look at your gear: the expiry dates you enter are a reminder of the dates you typed, not a check that a flare or an extinguisher works.

It works with no signal

There is no account, no sign-in screen and no server of ours. Your boats are not uploaded and do not sync. Gear, Lights, Buoys and Sound signals answer with no connection, because the question gets asked at a dock or on the water. Conditions is the one part that talks to anyone: it asks NOAA for the stations you chose, and keeps what NOAA said so it still shows offshore.

What is free, and what the subscription adds

Free, with no card and no account: Gear with every item and its rule, Lights, Buoys, Sound signals, and NOAA's advisories and warnings on Conditions. Have a good look at those first. The web version has no timed trial of the paid parts.

The subscription adds the rest: My boats (saving the boats you look after, a checklist you tick off that keeps its place, and dates for flares and for each extinguisher, flagged when expired or due within three months), Practice for lights, buoys and sounds, and on Conditions the marine forecast, wind, tides, currents and sun times. Coming back after a while

Everything waits where you left it, in the browser or on the phone you used. The first screen is always Gear, ready for a new boat, so look under the My boats tab: each saved boat says how far through its checklist you got and what has expired since. Practice remembers what you have learned, and Conditions remembers your stations and fetches fresh forecasts when it opens. In a different browser you start empty, because nothing syncs.

Why Conditions shows no forecast zone

The advisories and the forecast come by marine forecast zone, which Boat Ready finds from your stations. If NOAA could not be reached when you saved them, Conditions says so and asks again each time it opens. If NOAA answers that no marine zone lies within 2 nautical miles of your tide station, as happens up rivers and well inland, Conditions says that instead and points you to VHF weather and channel 22A. Tides and currents still work there.
