GED Completion works out where you already stand, which subject to sit next, and keeps track of what is left. It is a completion tool rather than a question bank: most people who do not finish stopped, they did not fail.

What the diagnostic tells you, and what it cannot

The diagnostic sorts each of the four subjects into one of three actions: sit it now, nearly there, or work on this one first. Each answer saves as you give it, so losing signal part way through loses nothing.

It does not predict a score and never shows you a percentage. A short diagnostic cannot tell anybody what they would get on a two hour test, and a number would be believed. It also refuses to say anything at all about a subject you answered too few questions on, rather than guessing.

The bar for "you could sit this now" is deliberately high, because that is the recommendation that costs money if it is wrong: a test fee, a day off a shift, childcare.

Why the subjects are in that order

The Subjects tab lists them in the order worth doing them, not the order they are usually listed in. Subjects you could sit now come first, and within a band the shorter test comes first, because a seventy minute sitting fits around a shift in a way a hundred and fifty minute one does not.

It says the questions have not been loaded

The diagnostic draws its questions from the question set attached to your account. If none have been loaded yet, the screen says so plainly instead of showing you an empty test. Nothing you did caused it, and everything else still works: you can mark a subject as sat or passed yourself on the Subjects tab.

There is no streak, and coming back is fine

There is no streak, no days since, and no progress bar that can fall behind. Nothing counts how long you were away or says it back to you, and a long gap is answered by telling you what you still have banked. After a long enough break the app offers the diagnostic again, because a stale result anchors you to the person you were before the gap and redoing it is usually good news.

Who can see your progress

If you joined using an invite from a workforce programme, a corrections department or an employer, staff there can see which subjects you have passed, which one you are working on, and whether you have studied recently.

They cannot see your diagnostic answers, which questions you got wrong, or when you are free. That split is enforced in the database rather than promised in a page, and the app tells you about it before you connect. Joining an organisation is optional, and an individual account is visible to nobody but you.

Booking, fees and scores

This app is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to GED Testing Service, and GED is their registered trademark. It cannot book a test and cannot look up what you scored; everything it knows about your results is what you entered. Booking and scores live on GED.com.

Test fees vary by state and most states have vouchers or fee assistance. Ask your caseworker first, and check your state's page on GED.com. There is no fee lookup inside the app, and saying so is better than pointing you at a list that may be out of date.

Your data

Signing in is a code sent to your email address; there is no password. The app stores your email, the organisation you joined through if there is one, your diagnostic answers, your status per subject and your study sessions. It asks for no date of birth, no address, no case number, no offence history and nothing about benefits or immigration, and nothing you enter is sent to a language model.

Deleting your account removes your profile, your answers, your status and your sessions permanently, and ends any organisation's visibility of your progress. Subjects you have already passed with GED Testing Service are not affected: those are held by them, not by us. The full policy is at carrierpress.com/gedcompletion/privacy.
