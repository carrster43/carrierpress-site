# This week: a finale that rewards waiting

Most adventure stories for young readers end with a race. Somebody gets there first, and
getting there first is the win. The Glitch Blocks, a six-book run for ages 8 to 12, is built
the other way round.

The shelf picks up after Captain Cubemelon's crew has linked six worlds and earned six rules.
Then fourteen small glitches begin flickering between the doors, changing which one opens
where. They cannot be fought, only understood, and some of them turn out to be frightened.
In [The First Flicker](/books/the-first-flicker/), Captain learns that not every urgent signal
is the next right step. In [The Moving Line](/books/the-moving-line/), Moss Guard learns that a
shield can guard a path while the path itself moves.

[The Between Badge](/books/the-between-badge/) brings all of it together. The last four
glitches build one impossible bridge near a locked arch, and nobody crosses alone: each member
of the team holds a piece no one else can. The fourteen blocks are read instead of erased. And
the seventh badge, the one the whole series has been reaching for, appears only for the team
that protects the way home rather than racing to be first through it.

That is a rare thing to hand an eight-year-old: a story where patience is the brave choice,
and where the reward comes to the friend who held the door.

# From the Press

- **Start at the first flicker.** [The First Flicker](/books/the-first-flicker/) is where the
  glitches appear and a good door starts opening onto the wrong place. Teamwork and careful
  thinking solve the danger, and it never becomes a fight.
- **The whole shelf.** [The Glitch Blocks](/young-readers/#h-glitch-blocks) lists all six books
  in order, each one handing the hard lesson to a different friend.
- **Talk it over.** The [Carrier Press podcast](/podcast/) has twenty-minute conversations
  about the books, where each one came from and what the edition adds.

# From the Labs

- **[Threshold](/apps/threshold/)** lists every parental control that applies to one child's
  age, across phones, tablets, computers, consoles, six apps and home internet, in the order
  that matters. The birthday on the account comes first, because most other settings are worked
  out from it, and each control carries its steps and the date they were last checked against
  the platform's own instructions. For a teenager, an agreement you both say yes to comes after
  the setup. It records what you tell it you have done: it cannot see your child's devices, and
  it never promises that a child is safe online.

Every Carrier Labs app is on [the apps page](/apps/).

# One to read

**[The Between Badge](/books/the-between-badge/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The badge at the
heart of this book appears only when you protect the way home, instead of racing to be first
through it. It is the sixth and final book of The Glitch Blocks, for ages 8 to 12: one long-lost
message is finally made whole, and fourteen frightened blocks are understood rather than beaten.
It is warm, clever and triumphant, and it still leaves a mystery that has only just begun,
which makes it a good one to finish together before summer, and it is available on Amazon.
