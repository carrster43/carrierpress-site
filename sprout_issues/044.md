# This week: three languages, one notebook

The Keystone Cycle sends five Greek diaspora teenagers to share a converted sponge merchant's house on Kalymnos, then on to the places their families left: Tarpon Springs, Darwin, Sfax, Alexandria, Broome. Each of them turns up a document there first. Each book stands alone, and they read richer as a cycle.

In [The Sfax Letters](/books/the-sfax-letters/), Lina Belhadj-Kosma is the only fully trilingual student in the house. She speaks Greek in a Sfax-Greek register her grandmother taught her, French at university level, and Arabic at her father's family-dinner level, and she carries a small notebook with three columns. In October she opens a storage room on a chore errand and finds a stack of letters labeled in Sfax-Greek. The top one is from 1903, written by an eleven-year-old girl sent from Sfax to a household in Pothia. The girl is family.

The cycle opens with [The Drosos Ledger](/books/the-drosos-ledger/), where Athena Markou of Tarpon Springs arrives expecting Greece to confirm who she is, and learns her Greek is a hundred years out of date. In [The Hudson Creek Record](/books/the-hudson-creek-record/), Niko Lekkas from Darwin finds a parish marriage record from 1908 with his name on it and the wrong town.

Why is one house so full of paper? Because a sponge merchant was a banker whether he wanted to be or not, and the paper stayed when the boats were sold.

# From the Labs

- **[Endorsed](/apps/endorsed/)** is a study app for the CDL and the transport credentials around it, from
  air brakes and HazMat to drone, vessel and rail tests. Every answer names the federal rule, FEMA course
  or state law it rests on, and every pack was checked item by item by a reviewer who did not write it.
  The CDL packs include a pre-trip examiner: you say each item out loud, as you would to the examiner, and
  tick it off.

# One to read

**[The Sfax Letters](/books/the-sfax-letters/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. There are one hundred sixty-two
letters, and the girl who wrote the first of them is the sister of Lina's great-grandmother's mother.
Book 3 of The Keystone Cycle is a quiet, trilingual, archive-minded novel about the women whose hands
wrote what their daughters could not afterward read, and about forty afternoons at a grandmother's
painted kitchen table on Rue de l'Algerie. It is a good book for anyone with a box of family letters
they have never quite opened, and it is available on Amazon.
