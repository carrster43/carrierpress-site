# This week: repairing a thing, or reading it

Patch Bolt is the team mechanic in The Glitch Blocks, a six-book series for ages 8 to 12, and
his hands have never wanted anything more than to fix the save-point stuttering in the Mech
Workshop. The trouble is that in this place every quick repair erases something true. Fix the
break, and you delete the message it was holding.

So [The Broken Save](/books/the-broken-save/) asks Patch for a different kind of courage:
document it first, label it, translate it, and only then decide. A little archive robot named
Bit Bracket helps the team learn the difference between repairing a thing and reading it.

The idea runs right through the shelf. The glitches in these books cannot be fought, only
understood. In [The Loop That Spoke](/books/the-loop-that-spoke/), Pip Pop stops and listens to
a phrase everyone else wants shut down. In [Static Orchard](/books/static-orchard/), the
orchard keeps saving the team back to the same tree with one small detail changed, and the
scariest changes turn out not to be broken at all. Some of them are just scared.

For a child who likes taking things apart, that is a useful thought to carry around: the odd
noise, the wrong result, the thing that will not behave, might be telling you something before
you make it stop.

# From the Press

- **Stop and listen.** [The Loop That Spoke](/books/the-loop-that-spoke/) follows Pip as a
  repeating phrase, reversed and re-heard, turns out to be carrying a message home.
- **Memory and marks.** [Static Orchard](/books/static-orchard/) gives the lead to Brick Bloom,
  the team's mark-maker, and asks what a sign means when a glitch can eat any drawing.
- **Not sure which book?** [Find your next book](/pick/) asks two questions, who it is for and
  what kind of story they like, and gives you one book to start with.

# From the Labs

- **[Who Serves](/apps/whoserves/)** puts everyone who governs your address on one screen: the
  districts, the offices, the names, and when each of them is next up for election. Every name
  shows where it was read and when it was last checked, and when a check is too old the app
  says so rather than vouching for it. There is no rating, score, grade or endorsement of
  anyone: it shows the record and leaves the judging to you.

Every Carrier Labs app is on [the apps page](/apps/).

# One to read

**[The Broken Save](/books/the-broken-save/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Under the oldest broken
save of all, Patch uncovers a mark that fits Captain's own wrench exactly, which cannot possibly
be a coincidence. It is the fourth book of The Glitch Blocks, a hands-on adventure for ages 8 to
12 about patience, problem-solving and the things worth protecting. It suits any reader who
loves robots and gadgets, and it makes a good case that the bravest move is sometimes to keep
your hands still. It is available on Amazon.
