# This week: German classics, complete at last

Some famous books have reached English readers only in pieces. Two on the classics shelf were translated afresh for these editions, so that the whole of each could be read.

[Dr. Mabuse the Gambler](/books/dr-mabuse-the-gambler/) is set in Germany in the ruined years after the Great War. Into the gambling clubs and seance parlors of a society coming apart walks a man with no fixed face: physician, hypnotist, forger, market-rigger and murderer, who wants to rule everyone within his reach for the pleasure of power alone. Norbert Jacques published the novel in 1921, and Fritz Lang turned it into the silent film that made Mabuse a legend. The English most readers could find was a loose 1923 translation that dropped the book's real title. This edition restores the title and all twenty-one chapters, including the violence and sexuality earlier English editions softened.

[The Golem](/books/the-golem/) by Gustav Meyrink appeared in 1915, and it is not the folk tale. There is no clay servant taking orders. There is a man who cannot be certain whose memories he is having, a ghetto being demolished around him while he works, and a figure that returns to Prague every thirty three years wearing whatever face the city needs. This edition is translated from the 1915 text, all nineteen chapters.

If it is the confidence trick you enjoy, the same shelf has [The Man Who Robbed the Bank of England](/books/the-man-who-robbed-the-bank-of-england/): in 1873 four young Americans nearly walked off with the Bank's fortune, using good tailoring, a false name and forged bills of exchange.

# One to read

**[Dr. Mabuse the Gambler](/books/dr-mabuse-the-gambler/)** by Norbert Jacques. Most English readers
met this novel under the wrong title, in a translation that softened what Jacques
actually wrote. This edition is a translation from the German with an introduction on Jacques, Weimar
paranoia and Fritz Lang's films, and a translator's note on the title, the names and the card game. The
mastermind is a mirror of his age, which is reason enough to read him now, and the book is available on
Amazon.
