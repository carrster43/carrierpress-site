# This week: a story that waits for spring

Some books take place over a weekend. [The Music of the Spheres](/books/the-music-of-the-spheres/)
takes a winter. Between December and April, seven children go down into a chamber under the
south lawn of Atherton Manor, across the south park to a glass dome that answers only to a
song thrush, and a mile and a quarter over wet rock into a grotto that is older than the
manor. And in April, for ninety seconds, the thing works.

The Atherton Manor Mysteries are built this way, around a house and its grounds and the
long patience of the people who made them. [The Starlight Secret Society](/books/the-starlight-secret-society/)
begins it, with three siblings and a story their mother once told them about a stone hidden
at the top of the highest hill. [The Tower on the Highest Hill](/books/the-tower-on-the-highest-hill/)
moves the calendar on again, between May and midsummer, when the children take a hundred
foot cord and a curtain weight up a hill and discover that the outside of a building is
bigger than the inside.

One more thing sets Book 2 apart, and it is the adults. The grown-ups in this house
are careful, fallible and worth listening to, and they do not take the work out of the
children's hands. It is one reason the book suits confident readers of nine and up, and the adults who end up reading over
their shoulder.

# One to read

**[The Music of the Spheres](/books/the-music-of-the-spheres/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. For a
hundred years, four black panels have hung on a ballroom wall being called mirrors. The
children find them, along with twenty journal volumes with three gone missing, a list of
nine places on page two hundred and four, and a postscript in the hand of a woman who died in
eighteen eighty-one asking that this family not be the ones to open it. It is Book 2 of the
Atherton Manor Mysteries, a quiet, literary middle grade adventure about seven children
building something out of a drawing, and about what it costs the person who has to answer
when you finally learn how to ask. It is available on Amazon.
