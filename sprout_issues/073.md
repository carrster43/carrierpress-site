# This week: thieves, real and invented

The gentleman thief is a strange kind of hero. He breaks the law in a good suit, and we
like him for it, partly because he picks his targets and partly because he is so plainly
enjoying himself. The Classics Line has the original, and the real thing, and the version
with all the charm taken out.

The original is Arsène Lupin, who arrived in 1907. Maurice Leblanc made him a burglar, an
escape artist and France's answer to Sherlock Holmes, and the rivalry was friendly enough
that the notes in this edition include an entry on Herlock Sholmes.

The real thing is [The Man Who Robbed the Bank of England](/books/the-man-who-robbed-the-bank-of-england/).
In 1873 four young Americans nearly walked off with the Bank's fortune, with no tunnel and no
pistol, just good tailoring, a false name and a devastating understanding of how Victorian
banking ran on trust. A single undated slip of paper brought the whole scheme down, and
Austin Bidwell's own account, first published in 1897, follows him through the manhunt, the
trial and the twenty years in prison that came after.

And the version without the charm is [Dr. Mabuse the Gambler](/books/dr-mabuse-the-gambler/),
from 1921: a man with no fixed face who sits down at your table a stranger and by morning
owns your fortune, your will and your voice. Read the three together and you can see how
thin the line is between a rogue you root for and one you fear.

# One to read

**[The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsene Lupin](/books/the-extraordinary-adventures-of-arsene-lupin/)**
by Maurice Leblanc. Lupin robs only those who deserve it, walks out of prison on his own
schedule and signs his crimes with a flourish, and in French these first adventures have
never gone out of print. This annotated edition has the complete original English
translation, an introduction on Leblanc, his friendly rivalry with Conan Doyle and Lupin's
long cultural footprint, and notes on the French references for modern readers. It is the
place to meet the gentleman thief before a century of imitators, and it is available on
Amazon.
