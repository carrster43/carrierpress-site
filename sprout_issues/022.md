# This week: the moment somebody stops looking away

The Standalone Novels are the books that do not belong to a series, and at first glance
they do not belong to each other either. A missing sister in a mountain town. A wealth firm
with six months to name a successor. A night photographer who only ever watched. An
encrypted ledger going back four centuries. What they share is a single moment: the one
where somebody decides not to look away.

In [The Last Adviser](/books/the-last-adviser/), Adam Halsey built one of Boston's most
trusted wealth firms out of two rooms over a bank and a phone call he made in the worst
week of 1987. Forty years later he has six months to choose who runs it next.
[The Sterling Conspiracy](/books/the-sterling-conspiracy/) belongs to Elias Thorne, a
meticulous financial analyst at a London investment firm who trusts data above all else,
until an encrypted ledger and a four-century-old secret society test that trust.

And in [Concrete Mirrors](/books/concrete-mirrors/), Rory Vance lives five stories above a
Manhattan alley and documents the city after dark with a camera, a recorder, a flask and a
creed: do not judge, only witness. His whole life is built on not stepping into the frame,
which makes him the most interesting test of the shelf's one rule. What happens to a man
whose alibi is observation, once he can no longer stay outside the picture?

- **[Potluck](/apps/potluck/)** is neighbourhood organising and lending, kept to a few
  blocks, with each address verified without the address being stored. Organise a thing,
  lend a thing, keep a regular gathering going, and in October map the doors giving out
  treats. Moderation is built in from the start rather than added later.

# One to read

**[Concrete Mirrors](/books/concrete-mirrors/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Rory Vance tells
himself the lens keeps him clean. Then the night begins to look back. A cryptic mentor
presses him to photograph not the decay but the mending, while a colder figure draws him
into underground social experiments designed to prove that everyone, the watcher included,
has a price. It is a noir-tinged character study of voyeurism and moral erosion, for
readers who like the psychological unease of Patricia Highsmith and a city that plays a
character of its own. It is available on Amazon.
