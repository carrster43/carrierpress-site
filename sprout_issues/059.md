# This week: a year on the shelves

Sprout's year followed the seasons. January brought the money books, for the month when
everybody resolves to sort things out. Spring and summer belonged to the young readers' series
and the Keystone Cycle. October went to the Gothic, ending at Halloween with the original
Sweeney Todd, and December went to archives, island mysteries and long winter puzzles.

So here is a classic to carry over the line. In 1904 G. K. Chesterton walked across a
Yorkshire moor with a small, shabby Catholic curate named John O'Connor, and came off it
shaken. The priest looked to the world like a man who knew nothing. He turned out to
understand human evil better than the famous journalist did, for the plain reason that people
had knelt down in the dark and told him. Seven years later,
[Father Brown](/books/father-brown/) walked into print.

If Chesterton suits you, [The Man Who Was Thursday](/books/the-man-who-was-thursday/) is his
1908 spy thriller about a council of anarchists named for the days of the week, and it turns
into a philosophical fever dream.

# One to read

**[Father Brown](/books/father-brown/)** by G. K. Chesterton. Everybody in every story
underrates him, which is exactly how he works: he goes inside the murderer, thinks the man's
thoughts, and then he knows. This edition prints The Innocence of Father Brown (1911) and The
Wisdom of Father Brown (1914) complete and in order, twenty-four stories with a headnote for
each. A story a night would carry you well into January. It is available on Amazon.
