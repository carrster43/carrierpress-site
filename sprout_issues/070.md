# This week: a house made of clues

The best treasure hunts in fiction are not really about the treasure. They are about the
place, and about the person who hid things there and trusted somebody, someday, to come
looking. [The Starlight Secret Society](/books/the-starlight-secret-society/) is built that
way from top to bottom.

Atherton Manor sits at the end of a long drive, its west wing closed off and its grounds
vast and partly overgrown. The trail begins when a globe in the library yields a parchment
map. After that, the conservatory sundial gives up a serpent stone, a walk across the south
park leads to an aviary half a mile away, and a brass cylinder carries the words WHERE LIGHT
IS NOT WHAT IT SEEMS. There is a maze of mirrors in the west wing ballroom, and a hedge
labyrinth built over seven summers by a great-aunt who was disinherited from the family.
Every object is a door to the next.

The same house keeps opening in the books that follow. In [The Music of the
Spheres](/books/the-music-of-the-spheres/), the children find a chamber under the south lawn
that was cut to be an ear, tuned to one note. In [The Tower on the Highest
Hill](/books/the-tower-on-the-highest-hill/), they climb ninety one steps to a room fourteen
feet square, with a stool lying on its side and a shallow copper dish that nobody alive can
explain.

It is a series for readers who like to solve along, and for the grown-up who will be asked,
at bedtime, to read just one more chapter.

# From the Labs

- **[Fluent Hour](/apps/fluenthour/)** is one spoken conversation a day, about ten minutes,
  with someone patient. Choose a language and a situation, such as a bakery that has run out
  of what you wanted, and the other person speaks first; you answer out loud. There is at
  most one correction a turn, and only when a listener would not have understood, with no
  streaks, no points and no gems.

# One to read

**[The Starlight Secret Society](/books/the-starlight-secret-society/)** by Jeffrey L.
Carrier. The stone at the heart of this story does not give brilliance: in the family tale,
it reveals the brilliance one already has. Eleven year old Leo, ten year old Clara and eight
year old Finn follow a chain of clues across their own home toward it, while a rival group
of children, paid by a man named Mr. Pemberton, tries to get there first. It is a quiet,
literary middle grade adventure for readers from nine to thirteen, and Book 1 of the
Atherton Manor Mysteries. It is available on Amazon.
