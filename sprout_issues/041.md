Welcome to Sprout. September is close, and this issue is about a shelf of novels that keep their eyes on ordinary people rather than on the event that found them.

# This week: the ordinary days before

The American Firsts are novels about the first time America did something it now does every week, told from inside the lives that were standing there when it happened. Each book stands alone, and each carries an Author's Note separating the documented record from invention.

[Twelve Minutes on River Road](/books/twelve-minutes-on-river-road/) is set on the 3200 block of River Road in East Camden, New Jersey, an ordinary working street: a shoe repair, a barber with a wooden horse for the children, a corner drugstore, and a path across the back yards that everybody uses and nobody owns. On the morning of Tuesday, the sixth of September, 1949, thirteen people were killed on that pavement in about twelve minutes. Two thirds of the novel takes place before anything happens, and every ordinary Tuesday in it is read twice at once: as what it is, and as what you already know it to be.

[One Minute Past Noon](/books/one-minute-past-noon/) goes back to Wall Street on the sixteenth of September, 1920, when a horse drawn wagon outside the Assay Office came apart. Thirty three people died, one of them never named, and the case was never solved. [Mrs. Kehoe](/books/mrs-kehoe/) is set in Bath, Michigan, and almost all of it happens before the eighteenth of May, 1927, at a kitchen table, with a farm wife who notices everything and concludes almost nothing.

The Camden and Wall Street novels take the names of the dead from the death certificates and newspapers of that week. Where the record is silent, the books are silent.

# One to read

**[Twelve Minutes on River Road](/books/twelve-minutes-on-river-road/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The man
who did the killing is named three times in sixty eight chapters and is never once given a thought of
his own. What the novel follows instead is the street, and the sixty years the people on it had to go
on living afterward. Six of the thirteen were not from that block; they were passing through, stopped
at the light, or in to have a watch looked at. With early September ahead, it is a patient, humane way
to remember them, and it is available on Amazon.
