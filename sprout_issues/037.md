Welcome to Sprout. This week a five-book cycle comes to its last page, and it ends at dawn.

# This week: how a cycle closes

The Keystone Cycle follows five Greek diaspora teenagers who share a house in Pothia, on
Kalymnos, for two years, and then go on to the places their families left. Athena goes to
Tarpon Springs, Niko to Darwin, Lina to Sfax, Phaedon to Alexandria and Tia to Broome. Each of
them turns up a document first, and each book follows one of them.

That is a hard shape to end. Five lives, five cities, five different families learning that the
story they inherited was one of several. The final book,
[The Roebuck Bay Record](/books/the-roebuck-bay-record/), does it by bringing the whole cohort
together in time rather than in place. The cycle closes on a single moment: a Tuesday in late
June, at five forty-two in the morning local time, in five cities at once.

If you are starting fresh, begin with [The Drosos Ledger](/books/the-drosos-ledger/), where
Athena finds her great-great-grandfather under outstanding accounts in a museum ledger. Then
[The Hudson Creek Record](/books/the-hudson-creek-record/), where Niko finds a 1908 marriage
record with his own name on it. Each book stands alone, so you can also read them in any order
and let the cohort assemble around you.

What links them is less a plot than a habit of mind: patience with old paper, and the idea that
a family record can hold more than one true line.

# One to read

**[The Roebuck Bay Record](/books/the-roebuck-bay-record/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Working
through the ledgers one row at a time, Tia Theodorakis-Coffin finds her great-great-grandmother
Eirini, age seventeen, departing Pothia for Broome on the eighth of September 1908. Tia's family
holds three lines at once, the Kalymnos divers, the Yawuru country of her father's mother's
people and her mother's pearl-shed lineage, and the book asks how you record a line without
flattening it. It is the fifth and concluding volume of the cycle, place-rooted and quietly told,
and it is available on Amazon.
