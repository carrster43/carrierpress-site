# This week: what a family carries in music

Phaedon Trakas is eighteen when he arrives in Pothia, on Kalymnos, the first of five students
sent to a converted sponge merchant's house. In the common room, under a tarp, he finds a piano
from 1953. In the museum archive he finds two letters written in 1957 that name a Trakas of
Alexandria, addressed to the great-grandfather his grandmother never finished telling him about.

[The Pencil Under the Bench](/books/the-pencil-under-the-bench/), the fourth book of The
Keystone Cycle, is built on that distance. Two years in Pothia teach him what the letters say.
Two years in Alexandria, in a family apartment on Cleopatra Street kept in the Trakas line since
1933, teach him what they mean. There is another 1953 piano there, and the piece he is writing
for graduation begins to write itself.

Music is one way a family keeps a record. Language is another. In
[The Sfax Letters](/books/the-sfax-letters/), Lina Belhadj-Kosma is the only fully trilingual
student in the house, speaking Greek, French and Arabic, and she carries a small notebook with
three columns. When she finds a stack of letters, the top one written in 1903 by an eleven-year-old
girl, she begins to translate.

Both books are about what a family hands down when it cannot hand down the whole story. Each
stands alone, and they read richer side by side.

# From the Press

- **Three columns.** [The Sfax Letters](/books/the-sfax-letters/) is a quiet, archive-minded
  novel about the women whose hands wrote what their daughters could not afterward read.

# From the Labs

- **[FirstDay](/apps/firstday/)** turns the school emails, letters and PDFs into one list of
  every date a parent has to act on, every form and every supply request, each with the letter
  it came from. Every date carries its own confidence, and one it was not sure it read
  correctly is not scheduled: it waits at the top of the list for you to confirm or dismiss. A
  slip is tracked as signed, and then as sent back.

# One to read

**[The Pencil Under the Bench](/books/the-pencil-under-the-bench/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. On a
Saturday afternoon in Alexandria, Phaedon finds a great-great-uncle's hand in pencil on the
underside of a piano bench, addressed to the future descendant who would find it. It is a
literary coming-of-age novel about inheritance and return, set between a Greek island and an
Egyptian city, in Greek, French and Italian. Readers who love music in fiction will find a lot to
hold onto here, and it is available on Amazon.
