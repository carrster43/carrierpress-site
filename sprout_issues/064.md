Welcome back. This week is about paper, and the families who are left holding it.

# This week: the paper a failed experiment leaves

A sponge is one of the easiest animals in the world to propagate and one of the hardest to
farm. Cut a living sponge into pieces, keep them in clean moving water, and each piece will
close its own wound and carry on. The trouble is time. A cutting takes years to reach a
size a buyer will pay for, fixed to one spot in open water where a storm or a thief or a
warm season can end it.

That is the starting point of [a piece in the Journal](/blog/the-sponge-that-would-not-grow.html)
about the sponge trade of Kalymnos and Tarpon Springs, where men went down in canvas suits
and lead boots long after everyone knew what the work cost. Farming was discussed the whole
time and kept not happening, until the beds took a disease and it became the only promise
left. When a trade is dying, everybody writes: applications, surveys, plot claims, letters
to ministries and to cousins.

A successful business leaves a building. A failed experiment leaves paper, and the paper
outlives everyone who understood it. That is the engine of the Things Left Open novels, five
heirs across the Greek diaspora, each holding a document that is either an accident or a
decision. [The Sponge Cache](/books/the-sponge-cache/) begins with a ledger.
[The Pearl Ledger](/books/the-pearl-ledger/) follows a maritime lawyer in Sydney who reads
old ship registries the way other people read weather, and finds that the order of four
vessel names in a 1907 pearling registry is not an order at all. It is a set of coordinates.

# From the Press

- **The shelf.** [Things Left Open](/fiction/#h-things-left-open) is five standalone but
  connected novels. Read any one alone; they are richest in order.
- **A song that is a cipher.** In [The Sixth Cache](/books/the-sixth-cache/), a researcher
  in Sfax listens back to his grandmother singing for the ninth time and finds the saints in
  her verse fall in an order no Orthodox calendar has ever kept.
- **Why a merchant's house is full of paper.** [The dorm and the
  drachma](/blog/the-dorm-and-the-drachma.html) explains why a sponge merchant was a banker
  whether he wanted to be or not, and why so many of these books turn up a cache.

# One to read

**[The Sponge Cache](/books/the-sponge-cache/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The book opens with
a granddaughter counting the mourners by their accents. A grandmother has died and left
Marina a ledger that does not balance, and a stranger is already willing to pay far too
much for it. It is Book 1 of Things Left Open, literary suspense about a document that is
either an accident or a decision, and the natural door into the series. It
is available on Amazon.
