Valentine's weekend. A short issue, and not quite the love story you might expect.

# This week: the other kinds of love

The cards in the shops this week mostly picture one kind of love. Novels have always been
better at the others: the sister you lost, the parent who kept a secret, the town that loved
its own peace more than the truth.

[Beneath the Surface](/books/beneath-the-surface/) is built on one of those. Nora Sutton
left her mountain town at eighteen, the summer her sister Lily vanished from the shores of a
lake. Two decades later a drought drains the lake, and a sunken car comes back into the
light. Nora goes home certain it is Lily. It is not.

On the same shelf, [The Last Adviser](/books/the-last-adviser/) is a quieter kind of love
story: a man with six months to choose who runs the firm he built, a diagnosis he has told
no one, and a wife who has been keeping a quiet record since the winter.

  the problem in their own words, and Stuck asks one question at a time until they get there
  themselves; it will not give the answer or any step of it. You can read every session,
  including how many times they asked outright for the answer.

# One to read

**[Beneath the Surface](/books/beneath-the-surface/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The body in the
car belongs to Danny Keller, a young man the town spent two decades insisting had skipped
his debts and left, and who died the same week Lily disappeared. It is a propulsive,
atmospheric thriller about grief, complicity, and what a community will drown to keep the
peace. For a reader who likes their love stories with a storm rolling down into the basin, it is
available on Amazon.
