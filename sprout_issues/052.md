# This week: the first thing you ever sold

Most people remember the first thing they sold: a cup of lemonade, a drawing, a lawn mowed
for a neighbour. The Money Skills and Making Things shelf starts from that memory and takes it
seriously. Its books are practical books about earning, building and handling money, written
for readers who are starting early.

[Side Hustle Junior](/books/side-hustle-junior/) opens with Maya, who draws twelve cats with
tiny crowns on a Saturday afternoon, packages them as a digital sticker set, and goes to
sleep. By Sunday night, eleven strangers have bought it. The rest of the book lays out seven
earning paths, from sticker packs and print on demand to the flashcards and worksheets that
teachers and students actually need. Every chapter ends with a TRY THIS exercise meant to get
the reader from reading to doing in under an hour.

What makes the shelf worth a look is that it does not pretend. The book is honest about the
effort, and it covers the dull, protective parts too: online safety, copyright basics, a
digital footprint, and what to do when things go wrong.
[Safe Online Projects for Kids](/books/safe-online-projects-for-kids/) comes at the same
ground from the other side. Most online safety books for kids are built on fear. This one is
built on creativity.

# One to read

**[Side Hustle Junior](/books/side-hustle-junior/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Maya made her
first sale from a device she already owned, with an app she had downloaded to doodle. That is
the whole premise: no startup money, no business license, no expensive software, just a skill
and the specific steps to turn it into something someone will pay for. It also carries a
Spend, Save, Reinvest framework for first earnings, the age rules for each platform, and a
guide for parents and educators. A good one for a long winter weekend with time to try
something, and it is available on Amazon.
