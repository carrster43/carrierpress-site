# This week: a story for every month

Some books are meant to be finished in a weekend. Others are meant to be lived alongside.
[The Surprise Book](/books/the-surprise-book/) is the second kind, and it says so in its
premise: a girl named Dotty spends an entire year secretly collecting a story for every
month, pasting each one into a scrapbook so it can go under the Christmas tree for Marjorie.
Everything in the book is what Marjorie read.

That makes it a fine book to pick up in late March, because the year inside it is just
getting going. March has a boy nicknamed Buttinski, who makes peace by interfering. May
brings a paper May basket, left on a door by a giver who runs. Further on there is a blackberry adventure in August, a shipwrecked Betty Crusoe in
September, and at the end a soldierly Christmas, because this was 1918.

Patten Beard's stories turn on kindness done quietly and at some cost, and Alice Beard's
illustrations carry them. Read one at bedtime, or read one a month and let the book run
alongside the real year, which is very close to what its author seems to have had in mind.

The same shelf has another small book that children took over. [Artful
Anticks](/books/artful-anticks/), gathered in 1894, is Oliver Herford's light verse and
drawings, where a violet arrives late and an elf borrows a sleeping dormouse's toadstool and
never gives it back, which is why a mushroom is an elf's umbrella.

# One to read

**[The Surprise Book](/books/the-surprise-book/)** by Patten Beard. In one of its stories a
little girl telephones Santa Claus and is asked, instead of giving her list, to find children
he ought to know about. It is a year of fifteen stories about small kindnesses, made to be
read aloud, and its world of arctics, penny banks and the safe and sane Fourth is a century
gone. This annotated edition keeps all of Alice Beard's original illustrations and adds an
introduction, A Year of Small Kindnesses, notes on 1918 household life and a reader's guide
for families and classrooms. Start it now and it will see you all the way to December; it is
available on Amazon.
