# This week: kids who make things online

Most online safety books for children are built on fear: a list of warnings to memorise.
[Safe Online Projects for Kids](/books/safe-online-projects-for-kids/) starts somewhere
else, from the plain fact that children are not only browsing the internet. They are
making things on it.

So the book gives them things to make. Across seven hands-on chapters, readers aged 8 to
14 set up their own digital workspace and a Digital Rules Poster, make an original digital
drawing and plan an illustrated story, code an animated character from scratch in
Scratch, build a maze game with keyboard controls, scoring and sound, and publish a real
webpage they designed themselves. Safety is woven into every project as it goes: strong
passwords, privacy settings, smart sharing, kind behaviour online, and what to do when
something feels wrong.

The same idea runs through the rest of its shelf, Money Skills and Making Things:
practical books about earning, building and handling money, for readers starting early.
[Side Hustle Junior](/books/side-hustle-junior/) opens with Maya drawing twelve cats with
tiny crowns on a Saturday afternoon and turning them into a digital sticker set. In
[The Digital Founders](/books/the-digital-founders/), it is past midnight, the laptop fan is
humming, and Alex is writing the first line of code for a company that does not exist yet.

# One to read

**[Safe Online Projects for Kids](/books/safe-online-projects-for-kids/)** by Jeffrey L.
Carrier. It is built for the grown-ups too: a complete Facilitator Guide that turns every chapter into a ready-to-teach unit, with learning
objectives, discussion questions, extension activities and safety conversation starters.
That means a parent, homeschooler, librarian or classroom teacher can run a full
digital-literacy unit with no prep, and a 25-term glossary makes every idea easy to look
up. It is clear enough for an eight year old to follow alone and never talks down to a
fourteen year old, and it is available on Amazon.
