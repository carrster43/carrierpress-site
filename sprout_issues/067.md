# This week: one question, five books

On a six mile barrier island in North Carolina's Outer Banks, the Ginger Mae Coffee Shop
opens at seven fifteen every morning, the way it has for forty years. When the woman the
island called Grandma Knapp dies, she leaves the shop to Victoria, Olivia and Solei, three
friends in their late twenties who only meant to make coffee. What she also leaves them,
scattered through cedar boxes and fireproof safes, is a trail.

That is the whole engine of the Marjorie Corey Files. Each book is a short mystery that
stands on its own, and each one opens one more drawer in the life of the woman who left the
shop behind. Underneath them all runs a single question, asked from the very first page:
who was she, really?

It starts with [The Second Shooter](/books/the-second-shooter/), a quiet death that pulls a
thread running far past the island, to a sixty year old conspiracy and a photograph from
Dallas. It ends with [The Morning Table](/books/the-morning-table/), five years on, when a
historian arrives with the careful manner of someone who knows what she is about to say will
change things: the woman the island knew as Grandma Knapp does not appear anywhere before a
certain year. What the women find in the end is not a deception but a life chosen and built
from nothing, entirely her own.

You can read them in any order. The last answer lands hardest if you start at the
beginning.

# One to read

**[The Second Shooter](/books/the-second-shooter/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. It is a cozy
mystery set in a coffee shop, and its thread runs back to the Kennedy assassination: a
secret the island kept for sixty years. It is Book 1 of the Marjorie Corey Files, and the
place where Victoria, Olivia and Solei first find that Grandma Knapp left them a trail as
well as a shop. It is available on Amazon.
