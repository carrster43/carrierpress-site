# This week: a horror sold in penny parts

Everyone knows Sweeney Todd from the stage and the screen. Almost nobody has read him. The
Demon Barber of Fleet Street began as a penny-part romance in 1850, a story that reached its
readers a piece at a time and ran past one hundred and seventy chapters before it was done.
It is long because it was built to bring people back for the next part.

That is why it divides so naturally in two. [Volume One](/books/sweeney-todd-volume-one/)
opens on the small shop by St. Dunstan's church, where a stranger walks in and is never seen
to walk out. [Volume Two](/books/sweeney-todd-volume-two/) is where suspicion finally gathers
around that shop, and the machinery of Todd's dark fortune begins to turn against him.

If the penny dreadful leaves you wanting older shadows, go back half a century.
[The Monk](/books/the-monk/) is the Gothic novel Parliament denounced, published in 1796
when Matthew Lewis was twenty, and its own author was forced to censor it.

# One to read

**[Sweeney Todd, Volume Two](/books/sweeney-todd-volume-two/)** by James Malcolm Rymer. The
story that gave the world Sweeney Todd runs to 177 chapters, and this volume carries the last
ninety-three of them, whole and unabridged. The string of pearls, the missing sailor and the
celebrated pies of Mrs. Lovett all draw toward the light here. The edition adds notes on
Fleet Street and the period references, and a Sweeney Todd timeline from the 1830s to today.
Halloween is the right weekend to finish it, and it is available on Amazon.
