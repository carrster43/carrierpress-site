# This week: baseball, faith and fakery

Between 1997 and 2005, an FBI investigation called Operation Bullpen broke open a national racket and produced more than sixty convictions. By the Bureau's own estimate, most of the autographed baseballs that changed hands in those years, balls signed by Ruth, by Mantle, by Williams, were fakes.

[Stealing First Base](/books/stealing-first-base/) puts a family at the center of that crime. It begins in 1920, on a train platform in southern Italy, where a nineteen-year-old porter named Russo Massena throws one punch too many and has to run. He runs all the way to Yankee Stadium, carrying a love for a game he has never seen played. That love passes down the generations, bending a little further each time, until it reaches his grandson Gavin, who was supposed to hit home runs for the Yankees and instead builds the most prolific signature-forgery operation in the country from a warehouse outside Bakersfield.

The detail is what makes it: paper aged in shellac baths and sealed bags of dog food, and a quiet prodigy named Gregory Marino mixing his own iron gall ink from a chemistry textbook. The characters are invented. The crimes are not.

If you like a novel about money that hides in plain sight, the same shelf holds [The Sterling Conspiracy](/books/the-sterling-conspiracy/), in which a London financial analyst who trusts data above all else opens a misfiled document: a ledger of transactions spanning four centuries that should not exist.

# One to read

**[Stealing First Base](/books/stealing-first-base/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The finest forger in the
country learned his trade at eight years old, at a Long Island kitchen table, when Gavin handed him a
pen and a shoebox of signatures and told him to try the Mantle. Moving from a Pullman card game in
1952 to a Las Vegas card show to a federal courtroom in San Diego, this is a warm, slyly funny saga
about fathers and sons and the men who loved baseball in the dark. Read it while the pennant races
are still close enough to argue about. It is available on Amazon.
