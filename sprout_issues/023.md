# This week: a case lost in the filing cabinets

At one minute past noon on the sixteenth of September, 1920, a horse drawn wagon standing
outside the Assay Office at the corner of Wall and Broad came apart. Thirty three people
died. One of them was never named. The case was never solved, and nobody was ever charged.

Most novels about an unsolved case try to solve it. [One Minute Past Noon](/books/one-minute-past-noon/)
does something harder. It follows the hour and the six years after it, and it lets you
watch the case go quiet, not in a single moment but slowly, in filing cabinets, among
men who are doing their jobs correctly. A Bureau of Investigation agent comes to
understand that the case will be lost inside its own paperwork, and the book makes you
feel why.

It holds itself to a strict rule while it does. Every person who dies in the book died in
fact, under their own name, age, address and trade, taken from the death certificates of
the City of New York and the newspapers of that week. Where the record has nothing to say,
neither does the novel.

Its shelf, The American Firsts, has two more books built on that kind of record.
[Twelve Minutes on River Road](/books/twelve-minutes-on-river-road/) begins on an ordinary
working street in East Camden in the most prosperous decade it will ever have.
[Mrs. Kehoe](/books/mrs-kehoe/) sets a coroner's inquest beside the kitchen-table days of
the woman it never asked about.

# One to read

**[One Minute Past Noon](/books/one-minute-past-noon/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Five
hundred pounds of cast iron window sash weights went out in every direction at the height
of a man, and the city that lost thirty three people that noon never found out who was
responsible. The novel follows a sixteen year old runner who counted his own wages that morning, a
lobby porter of fifty eight who was proud of his brass, the Bureau agent, and the men in
Boston and Romagna the Bureau was never quite able to reach. It is a novel about the
particular way a country loses something, and it is available on Amazon.
