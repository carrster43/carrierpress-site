# This week: a love story inside a firm

[The Last Adviser](/books/the-last-adviser/) is not a romance, and it is full of love all
the same. Adam Halsey has a diagnosis he has told no one. He also has a wife who has been
keeping a quiet record since the winter, and a daughter who runs the firm's conscience two
floors down and never wanted the chair. He has good hours inside bad days.

The novel calls itself a book about what a family is when the money is the wound. It is
also about the people who notice first, and what they do with what they notice. On a
weekend given to grand gestures, it is a good book about the small, faithful ones.

# From the Labs

- **[Vet Pocket](/apps/vetpocket/)** keeps each pet's weights and every vaccination, worming
  and flea treatment with when the next one is due. When something is wrong, describe it in
  your own words and it answers the 2am question: go now, be seen today, book an
  appointment, or watch closely, with the questions your vet will ask. It is not a vet and
  does not diagnose; if you are worried enough to ask, ringing your vet is always right.

# One to read

**[The Last Adviser](/books/the-last-adviser/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Adam Halsey built
one of Boston's most trusted wealth firms out of two rooms over a bank and a phone call he
made in the worst week of 1987. Forty years later he has six months to choose who runs it
next. It is a succession told in the room where it happens, and the late night work of
writing down everything one man knows before he loses the words for it. It is available on
Amazon.
