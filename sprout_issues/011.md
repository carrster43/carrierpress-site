# This week: the shiny object problem

There is a pattern most people recognise once it has a name. You start one side project,
read about a better one, switch, read about a third, and by the end of the month you have
three beginnings and nothing finished. [Passive Income Blueprint](/books/passive-income-blueprint/)
calls it shiny object syndrome, and lists it among the five most common pitfalls.

The book's answer is not more motivation. It is a narrower plan. It starts with the income
trap that keeps capable people stuck, which is trading every waking hour for money, and
then works through one idea: build once, earn repeatedly. You choose one vehicle, from
digital products and courses to affiliate sites, and turn a one-time effort into a system
that needs little upkeep. Then there is a thirty day plan with one concrete task per day.

It is written for freelancers, side-hustlers and small founders, and it is careful about
what it is: general education, not financial advice, with examples that are illustrative
and results that vary.

The other books on the shelf come at the same problem from different sides.
[Automation Income Systems](/books/automation-income-systems/) is about the systems that keep
working when you step away, and [The Passive Income Dividend
System](/books/the-passive-income-dividend-system/) is about a second income stream paid by
established companies you partly own.

# From the Labs

- **[Homeroom](/apps/homeroom/)** keeps one family's homeschool records against its own
  state's rules. Pick your state and it lays out the year's requirements, each with the
  statute it comes from and the date it was last checked, then logs attendance, a portfolio
  of work samples and a reading log as the year goes. It sums up the law for a parent and is
  not legal advice; the statute is the authority.

Every Carrier Labs app is on [the apps page](/apps/).

# One to read

**[Passive Income Blueprint](/books/passive-income-blueprint/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. Its
first argument is that most advice about passive income is built to sell you something, and
it sets out to do the opposite. Its premise is that you do not need a secret, you need a
system, and what follows is a clear, step-by-step guide for people tired of trading every hour
for money, ending in a plan with one task per day. If January's plans have stalled, it is a
calm place to start again in February, and it is available on Amazon.
