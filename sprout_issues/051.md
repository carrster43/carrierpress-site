Welcome to November. This week is about endings, and about the paper people leave behind.

# This week: five heirs and the question they share

Things Left Open is five novels about heirs scattered across the Greek diaspora, and each one
turns on a document that is either an accident or a decision. In
[The Sponge Cache](/books/the-sponge-cache/), a grandmother dies and leaves Marina a ledger
that does not balance. In [The Pearl Ledger](/books/the-pearl-ledger/), a maritime lawyer in
Sydney is asked to clear a 1907 pearling registry and finds that the order of the vessel
names is a set of coordinates.

You can read any one of them alone, but they are richest in order. What links them is less
the mystery than the question each heir is left holding. Not what the document proves, but
what they owe it.

[The Seven Plates](/books/the-seven-plates/) is where the series ends, and it ends by putting
that question to a vote. Four heirs, each carrying a decision from their own city, meet in an
eighty-two-year-old woman's flat in Athens. The fortune has waited fifteen years, untouched.
The families' rule says nobody reaches it without consent. A disinherited rival is circling,
a regulator is asking, and the vote in front of them is not about how to take the money.

If you want the history under the fiction, the Journal piece
[The sponge that would not grow](/blog/the-sponge-that-would-not-grow.html) explains why a
failed experiment leaves paper behind, and why that paper outlives everyone who understood it.

# From the Labs

- **[Paper Trail](/apps/papertrail/)** reads a receipt, a warranty or an insurance policy and
  pulls out the date that matters: when cover ends, when a policy renews and charges you, or
  the last day to return something. Where it could not read a date clearly, it asks you to
  confirm it rather than remind you about a guess. On the web, each reminder goes into your
  own calendar on the day to act, and a Home section keeps the house's equipment and its
  upkeep beside the documents they came from.

# One to read

**[The Seven Plates](/books/the-seven-plates/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. It begins with a
corrupted scan. Andreas Charalambou, a Greek-Cypriot security engineer in London who recovers
dead men's wallets and fathers' last passwords for a living, repairs an archive image for his
aunt in Limassol and finds a seed phrase hidden in it, split across seven engraved steel
plates. It is the finale of Things Left Open and complete in itself. Read it if you like a
thriller where the hard part is not the lock but the conscience. It is available on Amazon.
