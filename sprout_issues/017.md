# This week: glitches you cannot fight

Most adventure stories for young readers hand the heroes something to defeat. The Glitch
Blocks do something gentler and stranger. Fourteen small glitches start flickering between
the doors of the six worlds, changing which one opens where, and they cannot be fought,
only understood. Some of them turn out to be frightened.

In [Static Orchard](/books/static-orchard/), the second book, the glitch is hiding in the
trees of the sunlit green hills. Every time the team thinks it is safe, the orchard saves
them back to the very same tree, with one tiny detail changed each time: a different
branch, a wrong shadow, a path that was not there before.

Brick Bloom is the team's mark-maker, and he has always believed a sign you draw once will
hold forever. The orchard teaches him otherwise. A glitch that rewrites memory can eat any
drawing, unless the whole team remembers, out loud and together, exactly what the mark
means. It is a lovely idea to hand an eight year old: that agreeing on what matters is a
kind of strength.

The series begins with [The First Flicker](/books/the-first-flicker/): six worlds, six
rules, fourteen little glitches about to change everything. Book three,
[The Loop That Spoke](/books/the-loop-that-spoke/), turns the whole idea around.
Everyone wants to shut the glitch down. Only Pip wants to listen to it.

# From the Labs

- **[PillProof](/apps/pillproof/)** is a medication log for the person you look after.
  Each dose is marked taken or skipped, with the time and the name of whoever recorded it,
  and the rest of the family gets a summary to download or print. It records what was
  marked as taken, by whom and when. It does not advise on doses, and if something in it looks wrong, the
  box and the pharmacist are right.

Every Carrier Labs app is on [the apps page](/apps/).

# One to read

**[Static Orchard](/books/static-orchard/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The scariest changes in
the orchard are not broken at all: some saves were made to keep someone safe, and some were
made to keep someone out. With help from a tiny tree-sprout named Sprig Signal, Brick and
Pip Pop work out which is which. It is a gentle, clever adventure about memory, friendship
and the power of agreeing on what matters, and it reads aloud well a chapter at a time.
Book 2 of The Glitch Blocks, for ages 8 to 12, available on Amazon.
