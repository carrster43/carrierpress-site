# This week: time as the head start

Late summer is when families think in school years: new teachers, new supplies, a child a grade older. It is also a good moment to think in decades, because that is the one thing a child has that no adult can buy back.

That is the starting point of [Seeded for Success](/books/seeded-for-success/). It argues that the most valuable thing you can give a child is time for money to grow, and then shows parents, grandparents and anyone who loves a kid how small, early contributions can become a real financial head start by adulthood. It walks through why the first decade decides the trajectory, the arithmetic of compound growth year by year, and how the common kinds of account differ. It also lays out a giving model in which a single early gift outlasts ordinary charity. The book says plainly that it is general education, not financial advice.

If the grown-ups read that one, hand the young reader [The Growing Fortune](/books/the-growing-fortune/). On the morning Alex turns thirteen, the birthday gift is a single sheet of paper: the paperwork for an investment account opened the day Alex was born, quietly growing for thirteen years. What follows is a coming-of-age story told in the language of money, with a first real mistake, a rival who takes shortcuts, and a market storm that tests whether the lesson stuck.

The two books cover the same idea from opposite ends of the kitchen table.

# From the Labs

- **[Stuck](/apps/stuck/)** is homework help that asks the next question. Your child types the problem
  in their own words, and it asks one question at a time until they get there themselves; it will not
  give the answer or any step of it, even when told the work is already handed in. It never scolds and
  never mentions cheating, and you can read every session, including how many times they asked outright
  for the answer.

# One to read

**[Seeded for Success](/books/seeded-for-success/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. A child born today has one
advantage no adult can ever buy back, and it is not money: it is decades. This is a plain-language
guide, with no jargon and no hype, to turning that time into a fair starting line, from the year by
year math to choosing an account. Read it before the school year swallows the calendar, while the
long view is still easy to see. It is available on Amazon.
