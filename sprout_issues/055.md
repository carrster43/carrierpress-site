# This week: the notebook in the kitchen drawer

Most families have one: a notebook of recipes in a hand nobody else writes in, with stains on
the pages that get used. It is a cookbook, but it is also a record. The quantities say what
the house could afford. The margins say who liked what. Nobody thinks of it as an archive
until the person who wrote it is gone.

The Marjorie Corey Files are built on that kind of inheritance. Three women co-inherit the
Ginger Mae Coffee Shop on Patchogu Island, off the North Carolina Outer Banks, and with it
sixty years of what the town agreed not to say out loud.
[The Second Shooter](/books/the-second-shooter/) opens the series with a thread back to the
JFK assassination, and a secret the island kept for sixty years.

In [The Recipe Notebooks](/books/the-recipe-notebooks/), Book 2, Victoria has set out Grandma
Knapp's old notebooks under glass behind the counter as a small memorial. Then a visiting
pharmaceutical scientist stops cold at the display, quietly photographs three pages, and
hurries out the door. When the women finally open the old fireproof safe, three of the
notebooks are in Grandma's hand but hold no recipe anyone could cook. The quantities are in
milligrams. The instructions are a code.

Each book in the series stands on its own, and each opens one more drawer in the life of the
woman who left the shop behind.

# One to read

**[The Recipe Notebooks](/books/the-recipe-notebooks/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. A retired
patent lawyer checks into the island bed and breakfast, asks whether the Ginger Mae is still
open, and turns up dead in the south dunes on a Thursday morning. His laptop is gone, and his
briefcase has been repacked by someone who was not him. It falls to Solei, with her
scientist's eye, to read what an old chemist hid inside a grandmother's kitchen. A short,
warm, quietly serious island mystery, the right length for a December evening, and it is
available on Amazon.
