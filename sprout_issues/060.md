# This week: building something with friends

Every company starts as an annoyance most people would grumble about and forget. The ones worth
reading about start with someone who does not forget. And almost always, the idea is the easy
part. The hard part is the people you build it with.

[The Digital Founders](/books/the-digital-founders/) opens past midnight, the laptop fan
humming, as Alex writes the first line of code for a company that does not exist yet. By
morning it has a name: Innovate Solutions. Building it will take a team, and a team will take
trust. The novel follows a group of teenage builders as they turn a late-night idea into a real
product, and a friendship into a company, through the cofounder argument that nearly ends it, a
technical failure with no clean fix, money decisions that feel impossible at sixteen, and a
rival who moves faster and plays dirtier.

[The Startup Club](/books/the-startup-club/) covers the same ground with four high school
students and one company: Maya writes the code, Liam runs the strategy, Chloe designs the
experience and Noah tells the story. The equity split almost ends the partnership. Both books
know that a founder's worst day is usually a conversation, not a crash.

# From the Labs

- **[Canceled](/apps/cancelled/)** finds every recurring charge in your bank statement, on
  your own device, ranks them by what they cost you a year, and shows you where to cancel. It
  does not connect to your bank, and your statement is read in your browser and never uploaded.
  It is one payment, never a subscription, which would rather defeat the point.

Every Carrier Labs app is on [the apps page](/apps/).

# One to read

**[The Digital Founders](/books/the-digital-founders/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The idea is
real, a daily struggle every student knows, and it is thrilling and impossibly large at the
same time. What the story asks is what Alex and the team are willing to build, and what they
are not willing to become to build it, when the fastest path and the right path stop being the
same road. It is a coming-of-age novel for readers who love creators and competitors, and the
right one for a month of fresh starts. It is available on Amazon.
