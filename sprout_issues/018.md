# This week: why a merchant's house is full of paper

The Keystone Cycle is built around one building: a converted sponge merchant's house in
Pothia, on Kalymnos, where five Greek diaspora teenagers live for two years before each of
them goes on to a place their family left: Tarpon Springs, Darwin, Sfax, Alexandria,
Broome.

Why should a merchant's house be full of paper? Because a sponge merchant was a banker
whether he wanted to be or not. The trade ran on advances. A boat went out on money that
did not exist yet, against a catch nobody had seen, and the merchant who fronted it kept
the account. Multiply that by every boat in a harbour across sixty years and the building
fills up with the only record anybody kept of who owed what to whom. When the trade ended,
the boats were sold and the paper stayed.

That paper is where each book begins. In
[The Drosos Ledger](/books/the-drosos-ledger/) it is a ledger page. In
[The Hudson Creek Record](/books/the-hudson-creek-record/) it is a 1908 marriage record,
and two grandmothers' sentences, sixty years apart, that no longer agree.
[The Pencil Under the Bench](/books/the-pencil-under-the-bench/) runs from a piano under a
tarp on a Greek island to a pencil under a bench in Alexandria, seventy years later. Each
book stands alone. They read richer as a cycle.

# One to read

**[The Drosos Ledger](/books/the-drosos-ledger/)** by Jeffrey L. Carrier. The heroic 1905
family migration Athena Markou grew up celebrating was not a migration. It was a
debt-clearing, and she finds it in a back-room museum ledger on Kalymnos, under her
great-great-grandfather's name, listed with the outstanding accounts. Her grandmother, the
keeper of every recipe and every saint's day, has known the whole time. The novel follows
Athena across four years, two in the salt-damaged dorm in Pothia and two back home in
Tarpon Springs, watching her own city for the first time. It is a quietly devastating
coming-of-age novel about inheritance and silence, Book 1 of The Keystone Cycle, and it is
available on Amazon.
