FirstDay support

Last updated 24 November 2026.

FirstDay takes the emails and letters a school sends and turns them into one list of what is due, one supply list, and one form tracker, so nothing arrives as a surprise on the morning it is due.

FirstDay in your browser

FirstDay is on sale now in a browser, from the FirstDay page. The iPhone app is not out yet, so this is the version you can buy today. It is bought on Gumroad, not from Apple.

  • Your key. Subscribe on Gumroad, monthly or yearly, or buy the school-year pass once (the prices are on the FirstDay page). The receipt email carries a license key. Paste it on the FirstDay page, under Already have a key?, in each browser you use. Keep that email: you need the key again in any other browser. A FirstDay Founding access key opens it for its first year, and a Carrier Press Access key on a tier that includes the apps opens it for as long as that membership lasts.
  • There is no account. No password and no sign-in code. Your children, the dates read out of each letter and the supply list are kept in that browser, on that device, and not on our server. Another browser or device cannot see them.
  • Keep a copy. Clearing this site's data in your browser, or a private window once it closes, erases your records, and Safari on an iPhone or iPad clears them after about a week unopened. We cannot bring them back, because we never had a copy. Under Your copy on Account, Download a copy now and then. Restore from a copy adds what is missing and never replaces what is there, which is also how you move to another browser.
  • Reminders. A browser cannot remind you when it is closed, so FirstDay hands the dates to your own calendar. On Account, Add every date to my calendar makes each confirmed date an event on the day it is due, with an alarm at the hour you choose, as many days ahead as you chose. Each date under Watching on Coming up also has its own Add to my calendar.
  • Cancelling. A monthly or yearly membership renews until you cancel. Use Manage membership in your Gumroad receipt email or your Gumroad library, or write to support@carrierpress.com and we will cancel it for you. The period you paid for runs out first. The school-year pass never renews: it ends after the first June 30 that follows the day you bought it. Either way, once it ends your dates stay readable in that browser, and what stops is reading new letters.
  • Refunds. Apple has nothing to do with a web purchase, so write to support@carrierpress.com about one. A refunded key stops opening FirstDay.
  • Leaving a shared computer. Lock FirstDay in this browser forgets your key here and keeps your records. A key that has ended cannot open FirstDay again after that, so download a copy first. Erase FirstDay from this browser removes your records for good. Events already in your own calendar stay there, and neither one cancels your Gumroad purchase.

Why a date is sitting in "Check these"

Every date FirstDay reads out of a letter carries its own confidence, and anything it was not sure of is not scheduled. It goes to the "Check these" block at the top of Coming up instead, showing the date as it was read and the letter it came from. Confirm it and FirstDay starts watching it; tap "Not a thing" and it stops.

This is the whole design rather than a hedge. A date the app guessed and then reminded you about is worse than no reminder at all, because after one wrong buzz you stop believing the ones that were right.

Nothing is quietly dropped, either. A date FirstDay could not read confidently is still written down, still shown, and still attributed to the letter it came from, because a deadline that vanished would be the same failure as one that was invented.

Why a reminder did not arrive

In a browser, the reminder comes from your own calendar, so it arrives only for a date that is in it. Three things keep a date out, and they are all deliberate:

  • The date is still unconfirmed. A date waiting in "Check these" is left out of the calendar until you confirm it.
  • It came after you added your dates. Your calendar holds the dates as they were when you added them. For a date confirmed or read since, use Add to my calendar on it, or add every date again: most calendars update the same event rather than doubling it.
  • The thing is already done, or past. A slip sent back, an event marked done, or a date already gone is not added. One that was added before you marked it stays in your calendar until you delete it there.

In the iPhone app, which is not out yet, an unconfirmed deadline is never emailed and never buzzes your phone; both channels read the same rule, so they cannot disagree with each other. It asks to turn on phone reminders the first time you confirm a date, with a "Turn on phone reminders" link on the Account screen if you said no, and marking a slip sent back, or an event done, cancels its notification.

Either way, reminders come at the hour you pick on Account. Forms and payments use the notice period you choose; events keep their own shorter notice, because being somewhere takes less arranging than getting a signature back to school.

Why a deadline is against the wrong child, or against none

You do not pick a child before adding a letter. A newsletter routinely covers a whole year group and names children inside itself, so FirstDay assigns each item as it reads it. Anything it cannot place lands unassigned rather than against whoever happened to be selected, because a deadline filed against the wrong child is worse than one filed against none. Names are only used to work out which child a letter is about.

Forms have two steps

A form is not done when it is signed. Signing and returning happen on different days and the second is the one that counts, so a form tracks both: mark it signed, then mark it sent back.

What FirstDay does not do

  • It does not replace reading the letter. It reads dates out of school mail and it can misread one.
  • It never contacts a school and holds no school portal login.
  • Information reaches it two ways only: what you paste in, and a photo or PDF you add.

In the iPhone app

The iPhone app is not out yet. Its subscription will be billed through your Apple ID, and its Account screen shows whether it is active. Managing or cancelling happens on your device at Settings › your name › Subscriptions. Cancelling there stops the renewal and leaves the current period running to its end. We cannot cancel an Apple subscription for you and cannot see your payment details. Apple is the merchant of record, so refunds are requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Your records, and your children's

In a browser there is no account: your records stay in that browser, as above. A letter you paste, or a photo or PDF you add, goes through carrierpress.com to a model run by Anthropic to be read, and the photo or PDF itself is not kept anywhere. The children's names you entered are not sent with it; matching a letter to a child happens in your browser.

In the iPhone app, signing in is a code sent to your email address; there is no password. It stores the letters you add, the dates, supplies and form statuses read out of them, and each child by the name you gave, with no date of birth, no photograph and no school login.

In the iPhone app you can delete your account from the Account screen. That erases your children's details, every letter you have added and every deadline being tracked, permanently. The full policy is at carrierpress.com/firstday/privacy, and it names the services involved, including the model that reads a document you add.
