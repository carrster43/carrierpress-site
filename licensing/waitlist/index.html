Waitlist for clinics and counseling centers

Between a referral and a first appointment there are often months, and most people spend them doing nothing because nothing was offered. Waitlist gives that time a shape.

Four short self-guided CBT courses, a PHQ-9 and GAD-7 check-in every two weeks, and crisis resources one tap away on every screen. It is free to the person waiting. The clinic pays.

What it does

  • Four short CBT coursesBehavioural activation, thought records and worry time, five to fifteen minutes each, written for someone who has never done CBT.
  • A check-in every two weeksPHQ-9 and GAD-7 on their own two-week window, never more often, with change under 5 points reported as about the same.
  • Crisis resources that cannot be missedA permanent Help now tab in a colour used nowhere else, and item 9 handled before the score.
  • Honest about what it isIt tells people it is not therapy on sign-in, on the courses, on the crisis screen and in their account, and tells them to keep the appointment.
  • Private by designEach person's records are theirs alone: no clinician or staff account can read them, and nothing ranks or triages them.

The check-in every two weeks

The PHQ-9 and GAD-7 both ask about the last two weeks, so the check-in is offered every 14 days and not before. No chart is drawn until there are three check-ins, and a change of under 5 points is reported as what it is. This is the app's line for it, word for word:

“About the same as last time. Scores move around a few points on their own, so that is not a step backwards.”

When someone answers item 9

Any answer above zero on PHQ-9 item 9, about thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm, puts crisis resources on screen above the score, whatever the total says. That rule is part of the scoring itself and is tested at every value. A permanent Help now tab is on every screen, works with no signal, and does not record that it was opened. It lists: 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, 988 by text, Crisis Text Line (text 741741), Veterans Crisis Line (988, then 1), Emergency services (911).

Everywhere the app is used, it says: “If things get bad, you do not have to wait for your appointment. Call or text 988, any time.”

What it is, and what it is not

“Waitlist is not therapy and it is not a therapist. It is structured self-help for the months before your first appointment, so you are not doing nothing while you wait. Keep the appointment. Nothing here replaces it, and nothing here is a reason to stop or change any medicine you take.”

That is the app's own wording, shown to every person who uses it. Waitlist records answers. It does not diagnose, does not rank anyone's urgency and does not alert anyone, so no one should be referred to it in place of care. The crisis lines in it are United States lines, and it says so.

Waitlist is structured self-help made by Carrier Labs LLC. It is not therapy, not a diagnosis and not a crisis service. If someone is in danger, call or text 988, or call 911.

Questions

Who pays?

The clinic, the university or the employer. It is free to every person using it.

Can our clinicians see what patients enter?

Not in this version. Each person's records are theirs alone, which is what keeps it self-help. A summary the person chooses to share is the next stage, built with a partner clinic under its clinical governance.

Does it triage or alert anyone?

No. It records answers and shows crisis resources. Deciding someone is in danger is a clinical act, and the app does not make it.

Is it only for the United States?

For now, yes. The crisis numbers are US lines and the app says so.

How is it priced?

Per organisation, by the number of people you refer. Tell us roughly how many are on your waiting list and we send a written quote.
