GED Completion for adult education programs

Most adults who stall on the GED do not stall on the teaching. They stall between tests, when a shift changes, a child is sick, or the next subject looks like a wall. GED Completion is built for that gap.

It starts with a short diagnostic, tells each learner which subject they could sit now, orders the rest so the next win comes early, and gives your staff a roster of who has gone quiet so someone can call.

What it does

  • Diagnostic firstA short diagnostic in each subject sorts it into sit it now, nearly there, or study first. It refuses to guess on too few answers.
  • The next win comes earlySubjects they can pass now come first, and shorter tests first within each group.
  • No streaks, no shameNo streak, no days-since counter, nothing that resets. Coming back is welcomed, every time.
  • A roster for your staffWho has passed what, what they are working on, and when they last studied, so a caseworker knows who to ring this week.
  • A line staff cannot crossStaff never see a learner's diagnostic answers or their shift and childcare pattern. The learner is told exactly what staff can see, on the screen where they connect.
  • Joining is the learner's choiceConnecting to a program is optional, and the app says so.

See it working

An invented learner answers twelve diagnostic questions in each subject. This is what the app says back, word for word. It never shows them a percentage: every line ends in something to do.

You can sit Social Studies now, without studying for it.

  1. Social Studies (70 minute test)
    You could sit Social Studies now. Book it before you talk yourself out of it.
  2. Science (90 minute test)
    Science is close. A few sessions on the parts that tripped you up, then book it.
  3. Math (115 minute test)
    Math needs some work first. That is normal, and it is the one to give real time to.
  4. Language Arts (150 minute test)
    Language Arts needs some work first. That is normal, and it is the one to give real time to.

The order is the point. The subject they can already pass comes first, and shorter tests come first within each group, because a 70 minute sitting fits around a shift and a passed subject is still passed when life gets in the way in month three.

When someone comes back

The moment that decides whether a learner finishes is the day they open the app after weeks away. The app never counts the days back to them, never mentions what they missed, and has no streak to break. A test checks every greeting it can produce for those words.

  • Back after five days, one subject passed“1 down.” Next session on Science.
  • Back after five weeks, one subject passed“Welcome back. Still 1 passed.” Pick up on Science. One session, then see how you feel.
  • Back after four months, two subjects passed“Welcome back. You still have 2 subjects passed, and they do not expire.” Let us redo the diagnostic before anything else. It is twenty minutes and it is usually good news.
  • Back after four months, nothing passed yet“Welcome back. Nothing you did is lost and nothing has expired.” Let us redo the diagnostic before anything else. It is twenty minutes and it is usually good news.

What it is, and what it is not

GED Completion keeps learners moving toward the test. It does not teach the subjects, does not book tests and does not predict scores: booking happens at GED.com, and the app says so. It pairs well with the instruction your program already gives.

Questions

Who pays?

The program, the board, the facility or the employer. It is free to every learner.

Does it replace our instruction?

No. It works alongside it. It tells a learner what to sit next and keeps them coming back; your instructors still teach.

What can our staff see?

Each learner's subject status (passed, working on, diagnosed) and when and how long they studied. Not their diagnostic answers, and not their availability or childcare.

Does it work offline?

The first version needs a connection. An offline build for facilities is the next stage, planned with a corrections partner.

How is it priced?

Per learner seat per year, quoted in writing for your program. Tell us roughly how many learners in the form.
