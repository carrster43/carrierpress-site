How it works

  1. Send an inquiry. Tell us about the book and what you would like done. It takes about five minutes.
  2. We read your sample. You get a personal reply within 30 days, yes or no, with any questions.
  3. You get a written quote. Scope, price, schedule, rights and royalties are set out in writing before any work begins. Read the standard publishing agreement (PDF).
  4. We make the book. You approve the edit, the typeset pages and the listing before anything is released.

Questions authors ask

Do I keep the rights to my book?

Yes. You keep the copyright. The contract licenses Carrier Press to publish the formats you choose, and it includes a reversion clause, so the rights come back to you under the terms we both sign.

How are royalties paid?

You receive 60% of net receipts on print books and 70% on ebooks. Net receipts are what the store pays Carrier Press for a copy, after the store's discount and the printing cost. Royalties are paid within 150 days of the sale, once your earned balance is over $100. A smaller balance carries forward to the next payment and is never lost. The contract states your royalty and defines net receipts in plain words, and royalties are never held back to recover the package fee.

Why 150 days?

Amazon pays publishers about two months after the end of the month a book sells, and IngramSpark about three. 150 days lets every store's payment arrive before yours is due, so you are paid on a date we can always keep.

Will my book be in bookstores?

Through IngramSpark your book is listed in the Ingram catalog, which bookstores and libraries order from, so any shop or library can order it. We do not promise shelf placement: that is each store's choice.

Do I have to buy copies?

No. Author copies are available at print cost if you want them for events or gifts, and never required.

Can I bring my own editor or finished files?

Yes. Untick anything you already have in the form and the quote covers only what is left.

Why does each format need its own ISBN?

An ISBN names one edition of one book. The paperback, hardcover, large print and ebook are each a separate edition, so each needs its own number for stores and libraries to order the right one. That is why ISBNs are priced per format, with a discount when you take them all.

Who owns the ISBNs?

Carrier Press, as the publisher of record. An ISBN identifies the publisher as well as the edition, which is what puts the Carrier Press name on your book in every catalog. If the rights come back to you, the next edition takes new ISBNs under its new publisher, as it would leaving any press.

Do you use AI?

Audiobook, podcast and video editions are produced with AI narration and AI production tools, and the quote says so. If AI tools are used in any other part of the work, the quote names them before anything starts, and you can decline them.

Can I see a sample contract?

Yes. Read the standard publishing agreement (PDF). It is the same agreement every author signs, with Schedule A filled in for your book. Take whatever time you need to read it before you sign.

What if something goes wrong?

Write to publish@carrierpress.com and the publisher answers personally. Every concern gets a written reply.
