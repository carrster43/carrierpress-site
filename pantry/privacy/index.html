Pantry privacy policy

Last updated 19 November 2026.

Pantry starts from what you already have and what expires first, rather than from a menu. This policy explains what is kept.

Your item list is sent to Anthropic to suggest a meal

This is the part you are least likely to assume, so it is here rather than at the bottom. When you ask for a suggestion, the items you have on hand are sent to a large language model run by Anthropic, which proposes what to cook. If you photograph a receipt, that image is sent to be read as well.

Nothing is sent unless you ask for a suggestion or photograph something. Adding an item by hand stays on our database.

What a food list can reveal

Worth saying plainly: what somebody eats can imply a religion, a health condition or a household size. We do not analyse your list for any of that, do not segment or profile you on it, and never sell or share it. It exists to answer one question, which is what to cook tonight.

What is stored

  • Your email address, and whether your subscription is active.
  • Every item you have, with quantity and any expiry date.
  • The suggestions made, so you are not offered the same thing repeatedly.
  • Receipts you photograph and the items read out of them.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to your email address. No grocery store account, no loyalty card, no purchase history from any retailer, and no payment details.

Who else is involved

  • Anthropic receives your item list when you ask for a suggestion, and any receipt you photograph.
  • Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes.
  • RevenueCat sits between the app and the app store and tracks whether your subscription is active. It receives the purchase events and an identifier for your account.
  • Apple handles the payment itself. Card details go to Apple and never to us.

Deleting it

Deleting your account removes your profile, every item, suggestion and receipt, immediately and unrecoverably.

The web version

Pantry in a browser has no account and no database. Everything above about your account, Supabase, RevenueCat, Apple, stored receipt images and deleting an account is about the phone app.

What is in your kitchen stays in your browser. The things you add, where they are kept and their dates, what you used and what you threw away, what was read off each receipt, the dinner ideas you were given, and what you wrote in "what you cannot eat", "what you would rather not" and how many people you cook for are kept in that browser's storage for carrierpress.com, on that device. They are not sent to us and not stored on any server. Another browser or device cannot see them, and clearing the site's data in your browser erases them, so Account offers Download a copy. Restore from a copy reads a downloaded copy in your browser and adds what is not already there; it never replaces anything, and the file is not sent anywhere.

Reading a receipt. When you photograph or choose a receipt, or paste the text of one or of an online order email, that photo, PDF or text is sent through a small server of ours on Cloudflare to Anthropic to be read, and what was read (the shop, the date, the total and the food on it) comes back to your browser. A grocery receipt names a shop, a date, what you bought and sometimes the last digits of a payment card. The photo, PDF or text itself is not kept, in your browser or anywhere else.

Dinner ideas. When you ask what to cook, your browser sends, through the same server to Anthropic: the name of each thing still in your pantry, where it is kept and how many days it has left (under a number your browser makes up); what you wrote in "what you cannot eat" and "what you would rather not"; how many people you cook for; and the titles of recent ideas, so the same dinners do not come round. The first of those is usually health information about someone in your household, and it is sent for exactly one reason: an idea cannot keep a food out without being told what it is. No name, email address or account is sent, because there is none. The ideas come back to your browser and are kept there.

Anthropic processes what it is sent to return the result and does not train on it.

No reminders. The web version sends no notifications and no email.

Your license key. The web version opens only with a license key, from your Gumroad receipt or a Carrier Press Access key. When you paste it on this app's page, it goes to that same server, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. If it is, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that access ends when a purchase is refunded or a membership ends. The cookie also counts the receipts read and the ideas asked for that day, so that one key cannot be used without limit. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. Pantry never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

To delete it, open Account, then Erase Pantry from this browser. Nothing about your kitchen is held anywhere else to delete.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
