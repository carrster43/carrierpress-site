Last updated 24 November 2026.

Pantry starts from what you already have and what goes off first, and answers one question on one screen: what is for dinner tonight.

Pantry in your browser

Pantry is on sale now in a browser, from the Pantry page. The iPhone app is not out yet, so this is the version you can buy today. It is bought on Gumroad, not from Apple.

  • Your key. Subscribe on Gumroad, monthly or yearly (the price is on the Pantry page), and the receipt email carries a license key. Paste it on the Pantry page, under Already have a key?, in each browser you use. Keep that email: you need the key again in any other browser. A Carrier Press Access key on the Apps or All Access tier opens Pantry too, for as long as that membership lasts.
  • There is no account. No password and no sign-in code. What is in your pantry, your dinner ideas, what was read off your receipts and what you cannot eat are kept in that browser, on that device, and not on our server. Another browser or device cannot see them. The receipt photo, PDF or pasted text itself is not kept anywhere once it has been read.
  • Keep a copy. Clearing this site's data in your browser, or a private window once it closes, erases everything, and Safari erases what a site saves on its own after about a week without a visit. We cannot bring it back, because we never had a copy. On Account, under Your copy, Download a copy now and then. Restore from a copy adds what is not already here and never replaces anything, which is also how you move to another browser.
  • Reminders. A browser cannot remind you when it is closed, so there is no evening nudge in the browser version. Open Tonight when you are deciding.
  • How much it reads. Up to 15 receipts and dinner ideas a day, the two counted together. Typing things in has no limit.
  • Cancelling. The membership renews until you cancel. Use Manage membership in your Gumroad receipt email, or write to support@carrierpress.com and we will cancel it for you. The period you paid for runs out first. After that, everything in your pantry stays in the browser, and what pauses is reading receipts and asking for dinner ideas.
  • Refunds. Apple has nothing to do with a web purchase, so write to support@carrierpress.com about one.
  • Leaving a shared computer. Lock Pantry in this browser, on Account, forgets your key here and keeps everything. Erase Pantry from this browser removes it for good. Neither one cancels your Gumroad purchase.

Adding things, and why there is almost no form

The only thing an item needs is a name. There is no quantity field, no unit picker, no category and no date picker, because every one of those is a decision somebody makes at seven in the evening by closing the app.

Quick-add reads one line. Type chicken thighs fridge 3 and you get chicken thighs, in the fridge, due in three days. Type rice and you get rice. The field stays put after each line, so a whole shop goes in one line after another.

Two things are read off the end of the line: a location word such as fridge, freezer or cupboard, and a bare number, read as days from today. Anything else stays in the name, and nothing is ever rejected. A line that did not parse becomes an item called exactly that, which is untidy and far better than an error. Larger numbers stay in the name, so a weight stays a weight.

Why some items have no date

Because most of a real cupboard has no date on it. Undated items sort to the bottom and are still shown: an app displaying only the dated third would be describing a kitchen nobody owns.

Dates that came off a receipt

On Add, Photograph a receipt or Choose a photo or PDF, or paste an online order email or a receipt's text and press Read the pasted text. A whole shop goes in at once, with till abbreviations turned back into food, and the same receipt read twice adds nothing twice.

The dates are typical shelf life for that kind of thing added to the purchase date, not a date read off the packet. That is a guess and the app says so, but an approximate date that sorts milk above canned tomatoes beats no date at all. To change one, tap the thing under In the house and press Change. Added by mistake takes it off without counting it as used or binned.

What you cannot eat

Whatever you write under What you cannot eat, on Account, goes with every request for a dinner idea, and every idea is told to leave it out, including as a hidden ingredient: no sesame means no tahini, no dairy means no butter.

Pantry can still get one wrong, and it cannot read labels. Each idea carries the line Made to leave out, with your list, and Check the labels. Check each idea, and everything under Assumes you also have, against your list before you cook.

It is free text on purpose. A checklist covers the allergens its designer thought of and fails silently on the ninth, so write it however you like.

The separate box for what you would rather not eat holds dislikes and diets. Those are preferences rather than rules, and a suggestion will break one if the food is genuinely better for it.

Why the suggestion came back empty

When it cannot build a dinner from what you have without touching your list, it is told to say so and suggest nothing, rather than something close. An empty suggestion is a mildly annoying evening; the alternative is not.

Food past its date is never used to build a dinner. If everything in the house is past its date, Tonight says so, and you can check it or bin it from In the house.

The titles of your most recent ideas are sent along only so the same dinner is not offered again.

Pantry cannot see your kitchen

It suggests from what you told it you have. It does not know how anything was stored, and it is not a food-safety device. A date here is a sorting aid, not a verdict on whether something is good. Check the food, not the app.

Used, or binned

Open a thing under In the house and press Used it or Binned it; either answer removes it. The difference feeds the figure for the last month on Account.

What Pantry does not do

  • No shopping list. The point is starting from what you already have, and a list is the first step back toward being every other meal planner.
  • No store account, loyalty card or purchase history.
  • Nothing learns from you beyond avoiding a repeated suggestion title.

An internet connection is required.

In the iPhone app

The iPhone app is not out yet. It will be sold as a subscription through your Apple ID. The current price and billing period are shown on the purchase screen inside the app, which reads them from the App Store for your country rather than repeating a figure here.

Manage it at Settings › your name › Subscriptions. Cancelling stops the renewal and leaves the current period running to its end; we cannot cancel it for you. After a reinstall or a new device use Restore Purchases, and if it finds nothing, check you are signed in with the Apple ID that bought it. Apple is the merchant of record, so refunds are requested at reportaproblem.apple.com.

In the iPhone app there is one nudge a day, at dinner time, and only when something is going off. It names the actual food, because a notification saying you have items expiring is one nobody acts on. An honest limitation: the nudge carries the text it was scheduled with, and that text is rebuilt when you open the app. If the app goes unopened for a week, it can name food you have already eaten.

Your data

In a browser there is no account: everything stays in that browser, as above. In the iPhone app there is no password; you sign in with a code sent to your email address. The app stores your items, the suggestions made, and any receipt you photographed, and deleting your account removes all of it, immediately and unrecoverably.

A food list can imply a religion, a health condition or a household size. It is not analyzed for any of that, not used to profile you, and never sold or shared. The full policy is at carrierpress.com/pantry/privacy, and it names the services involved for the browser and for the iPhone app.
