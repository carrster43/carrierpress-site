Threshold privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026.

Threshold walks you through every parental control across a phone, a console, a tablet and the apps on them, for one child's age, in one sitting. This policy explains what is kept.

It does not monitor your child, and it cannot

This is the most important thing on the page. Threshold tells you which settings to change and where they are. It has no connection to your child's device, no connection to any account of theirs, and no access to anything they do.

It does not see their messages, their browsing, their screen time, their location or their friends. It cannot, because it is never connected to any of it. What you get is a guide and a record of what you have set up.

What we hold about the child

As little as the app can work with: a name to tell one child's setup from another's if you have more than one, and an age, because the age is what determines which settings are recommended.

No date of birth, no photograph, no school, no contact detail, no device identifier and no account name for any platform. The child has no account here, we never contact them, and nothing about them is sold or used for advertising in any form.

What is stored

  • Your email address, and whether you have paid for this child.
  • Each child: the name and age you entered.
  • Which platforms you said they use, and your progress through each guide.
  • Alerts for when a platform changes its settings, so a guide you completed can be revisited.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to your email address. No credentials for any platform. Threshold never asks for your child's logins or your own, because it changes nothing on your behalf; you make each change yourself, on the device.

Who else is involved

Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes. RevenueCat sits between the app and the App Store and tracks whether your purchase is active; it receives the purchase events and an identifier for your account. Apple handles the payment itself, and card details go to Apple and never to us. Nothing is sent to a language model or to any platform named in a guide.

The web version

Threshold in a browser has no account and no database. Everything above about accounts, sign in codes and our database is about the phone app.

Your record stays in your browser. The children you add (a first name and an age range), the devices and apps you pick for each, what you marked done, and which change notices you dismissed are kept in that browser's storage for carrierpress.com, on that device. They are not sent to us and not stored on any server. The guides are part of the app itself, so reading one sends nothing. Another browser or device cannot see your record, and clearing the site's data in your browser erases it, so Account offers Download a copy of my record. Restore from a copy reads a downloaded copy in your browser and adds what is not already there; it never replaces anything, and the file is not sent anywhere.

The Agreement. For a child of 13 or over, the Agreement section keeps the rules you and your teenager suggest and agree to, the check-ins either of you writes, a history of who did what and when, and a short fingerprint of the code each of you picked (not the code). Your teenager says they are 13 or over; we do not ask for or keep a date of birth. All of it stays in the same browser storage as the rest of your record, is never sent to us, and goes into Download a copy of my record with everything else. Threshold collects nothing from your teenager's devices: no messages, browsing, location, app list or screen time.

Your license key. The key checks are the only requests the web version makes, and they carry nothing about your children. The web version opens only with a license key, from your Gumroad receipt or a Carrier Press Access key. When you paste it, on this app's page or on a child's unlock card, it goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and how many children it pays for, and stores nothing. If it is good, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that access ends when a purchase is refunded. The browser's own record keeps only a short fingerprint of each key (not the key) and how many children it pays for, so a key entered twice counts once. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors. Remove my key from this browser, on the app's page, deletes the cookie.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. Threshold never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

To delete it, open Account, then Erase this record from this browser. Every child, every mark and every agreement go at once; the key fingerprints stay, so the children you paid for are still paid for if you add them again. Nothing about your record is held anywhere else to delete. Lock Threshold in this browser forgets your key here and keeps the record for when you enter it again.

Deleting it

Deleting your account removes your profile, every child and all progress, immediately and unrecoverably. The settings you changed on real devices stay changed; they were never ours to undo.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
