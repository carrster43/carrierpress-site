Titrate support

Last updated 15 September 2026.

Titrate is a record. It keeps the dose your prescriber set, what you felt at it and when, your weight over time, and general protein and fibre targets sized to that weight, so the next appointment starts from something written down.

Why it will not tell me what to do about my dose

Titrate never suggests a dose. It does not tell you to go up, come down, hold, split or skip, and it does not say whether a side effect is a reason to change one. Those are decisions for you and your prescriber, and the app has no column anywhere that could hold a recommendation.

Why the same line appears after every symptom I log

After every entry, at every severity, Titrate says the same thing: your prescriber wants to know about this, and they are the only one who can say what it means for you. It is identical every time on purpose. The app does not decide whether what you logged is serious. A version that used stronger words as the severity went up would be triage with a slider, and deciding how urgent a symptom is belongs to a clinician. Wording that never changes is not an opinion about any particular entry. Mild, moderate and severe are recorded exactly as you choose them, and nothing acts on that choice.

How the patterns are counted

The patterns screen compares the week after a dose increase with the fortnight after it, per symptom. Three things about how it counts are worth knowing, because they change the numbers:

  • It counts days, not entries. Logging nausea three times before lunch is one day, so one rough morning cannot weigh triple.
  • The denominators are days that have actually elapsed. If you stepped up three days ago, the second week has not happened yet and is not divided by as though it had.
  • It needs at least two increases. One step up is what happened once, not a shape, and the screen says so rather than drawing a line through a single event.

Only increases are counted. A reduction is a real and common event, and mixing the days after one in with the days after a step up would put them backwards. Everything on that screen is arithmetic about entries you typed, and carries no judgement about whether any of it is normal or concerning.

Why my weight trend has no comment on it

The trend states the change, over the period, in the unit you chose. No goal, no target rate, no adjective, and up is stated as plainly as down. What a flat few weeks means about your treatment is a question for your prescriber.

Why my weight was refused

Weights outside a plausible adult range are rejected rather than used: pounds typed into a kilograms field produce a protein target roughly twice what it should be, and a number that specific gets believed. Check which unit the field is in and re-enter. You can switch between pounds and kilograms in Account, and switching redraws the history rather than changing it.

The protein and fibre figures are the general published adult targets scaled to a weight, a starting point for a conversation rather than a plan made for you. A dietitian or your prescriber sets a real one.

What Titrate does not do

  • No dose guidance, no interpretation of a plateau, no decision about whether a symptom is urgent.
  • No reminders or notifications, including for injection day.
  • No connection to Apple Health or any other health store. Weights are the ones you enter.
  • No clinician view and no sharing. Nothing you record is sent to a language model.

Your data

There is no password; signing in is a code sent to your email address. Stored: that address, each dose and when it started, each symptom log with its severity and date, and your weights. No prescriber, no pharmacy, no prescription number and no insurance details.

Deleting your account from the Account tab removes your profile and every dose, symptom and weight, immediately and permanently. If any of it is going to an appointment, write it down first: there is no export. The full policy is at carrierpress.com/titrate/privacy.
