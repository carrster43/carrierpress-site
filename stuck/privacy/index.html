Stuck privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026.

Stuck helps with homework by asking questions rather than giving answers. It cannot output a finished answer, which is a property of how it is built. This policy explains what is kept and who reads it.

A parent can read the whole transcript, and the child should know that

This is the part that matters most, so it is here rather than at the bottom. Every session is kept in full and the parent who owns the account can read it. That is intentional, because a homework helper a parent cannot inspect is not one they can trust.

Tell the child. A young person who does not know their work is being read is being watched rather than helped, and the app is meant to be the second thing.

The question is sent to Anthropic

The tutoring is done by a large language model run by Anthropic. What the child types is sent so a reply can come back. Whatever they write goes, including anything about themselves they put in a message.

This is information about a child

We ask for as little as the app can work with: a name to tell one learner from another, and a year or level so questions are pitched right. There is no date of birth, no photograph, no school, no address and no contact detail for the child.

The child has no account of their own; the account belongs to the parent. We never contact a child, and nothing about one is used for advertising in any form or sold to anybody.

What is stored

  • The parent's email address, and whether the subscription is active.
  • Each learner, by the name and level given.
  • Each session, and every turn in it, as text.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to the parent's email address. No grades, no school records and no payment card details.

Who else is involved

  • Anthropic receives the text of each turn.
  • Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes.
  • RevenueCat sits between the app and the app store and tracks whether your subscription is active. It receives the purchase events and an identifier for your account.
  • Apple handles the payment itself. Card details go to Apple and never to us.

Deleting it

Deleting the account removes the profile, every learner and every session and turn, immediately and unrecoverably.

The web version

Stuck in a browser, at carrierpress.com/stuck/app, has no account and no database. Everything above about your account, our storage provider, payments through Apple and deleting an account is about the phone app.

No account, and the child's name stays in your browser. There is no sign in and no email address. Each child you add is a first name and a grade range, kept in that browser's storage for carrierpress.com, on that device. The name is never sent anywhere, including with a question to the tutor.

Sessions stay in your browser. What the child was stuck on in their own words, every message in the conversation from both sides, what unlocked it, the number of turns, and how many times the child asked for the answer are kept in that browser's storage, on that device. They are not stored on any server of ours. Another browser or device cannot see them, and clearing the site's data in your browser erases them, so Account offers Download a copy. Restore from a copy reads a downloaded copy in your browser and adds what is not already there; it never replaces anything, and the file is not sent anywhere.

What is sent for each turn. The child's grade range, the subject if one was given, the problem in the child's own words and the conversation so far (up to the last sixty turns) go through a small server of ours on Cloudflare to Anthropic, which writes the next question. Nothing about your family is sent with it. Our server stores none of it. Anthropic does not use data submitted through its API to train its models.

Your license key. The web version opens only with a license key, from your Gumroad receipt or a Carrier Press Access key. When you paste it on this app's page, it goes to our server on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. If it is, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that access ends when a purchase is refunded or a membership ends. The cookie also counts the turns taken that day, so that one key cannot be used without limit. When access ends, everything in your browser stays readable and can still be downloaded, but new problems are paused. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. Stuck never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

To erase it, open Account, then Erase Stuck from this browser. That deletes every child and every session Stuck kept in that browser, at once. We cannot recover it, because we never had it. It does not cancel your Gumroad purchase; manage that from your Gumroad receipt. Lock Stuck in this browser only forgets the key here, and leaves everything else in place.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
