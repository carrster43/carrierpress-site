Conception Zodiac privacy policy

Last updated 2 October 2026.

Conception Zodiac works out the zodiac sign at conception rather than at birth, from a birth date and, if you know it, how many weeks the pregnancy lasted. It is date arithmetic for fun, not a medical tool.

Nothing you type leaves your device

There is no account, no sign in, no database and no server of ours. The app does the arithmetic on your device, and anything it keeps is kept there. The app uploads nothing, because there is nowhere for it to go.

What is stored, on your device

  • The people you choose to save, a feature of the unlock: for each one, the name you typed, their birth date and, if you entered it, the pregnancy length. Nothing is saved unless you tap Save. Removing a person deletes them; deleting the app deletes the list from the phone.
  • Whether the app is unlocked, so it does not have to ask the App Store every time it opens.
  • When the app last asked for a rating, described below.

A weeks value you type but do not save is used for the calculation on screen and is not stored. The conception date the arithmetic passes through is never stored and never shown, only the sign; the reason is in the section below about what this app will not tell you.

Your own backups. Like every app's data, the saved list is part of your phone's own iCloud or computer backups if you use them, and comes back if you restore one. That copy is yours, held by Apple or on your computer under your account; we have no access to it.

Why the pregnancy length matters

A birth date on its own is ordinary. How many weeks a pregnancy lasted is health information, about the birth and about the person who gave birth. It is asked for because it is the only thing that makes the answer specific rather than a fixed shift from the birth sign, and it is optional.

So, exactly: it stays on your device. It is kept only if you save that person, it is deleted when you remove them, and the app never sends it anywhere, including to Apple and to us.

What this app will not tell you

It names a sign. It does not state a conception date. Naming the zodiac sign of a conception season is a toy. Asserting the date on which two named people conceived a child is a claim about their private lives and, if anyone relied on it, a medical claim as well. The app is built so that it does not make one.

Where the pregnancy length is unknown the answer is genuinely uncertain, and the app shows that rather than hiding it behind a single confident word. It is entertainment, not obstetrics, and not a way to establish when anything happened.

Sharing

After the unlock, Share this card makes a picture of the result card on your device and opens your phone's own share sheet. The picture shows the two signs. Its one line of text names the two signs and the app, and says so when the sign is one of several possible. Neither carries a date, a name or the weeks. Where it goes is your choice in that sheet; the app sends nothing by itself.

Children

The birth being described is often a child's. Anything about that child, or anyone else, that you choose to save is stored on this device only and never transmitted by the app. The app collects nothing about anyone.

Who else is involved

Apple, and nobody else. The unlock is a one-time purchase through the App Store. It shows the conception sign for any birthday, the signs it could also be, the share card and the saved list; without it the app shows the birth sign and how many conception signs are possible. Apple handles the payment and tells the app only that the purchase exists. Apple never receives a birth date, a pregnancy length, a name you typed, or anything else from this app.

The app is built from standard components that all run on your device: the App Store purchase library, local storage, the date wheels, icons, drawing and animation, and the picture maker for the share card. None of them sends what you type anywhere. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no crash reporting and no language model. If that ever changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top will tell you so.

Deleting it

There is no account to close. Removing a person deletes them, and removing the app from your device deletes the saved list from it. A copy can remain only in your own device backups, as above. Your purchase is held by Apple rather than by us, so reinstalling and tapping Restore Purchases brings the unlock back without paying again.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
