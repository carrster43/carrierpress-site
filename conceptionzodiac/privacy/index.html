Conception Zodiac privacy policy

Last updated 5 November 2026.

Conception Zodiac works out the zodiac sign at conception rather than at birth, from a birth date and, if you know it, how many weeks the pregnancy lasted. It is date arithmetic for fun, not a medical tool.

Nothing you type leaves your device

There is no account, no sign in and no database. The app does the arithmetic on your device, and anything it keeps is kept there. The app uploads nothing, because there is nowhere for it to go. The one exception is a license key you paste into the web version, which goes to our key check and to Gumroad and nowhere else, as described below.

What is stored, on your device

  • The people you choose to save, a feature of the unlock: for each one, the name you typed, their birth date and, if you entered it, the pregnancy length. Nothing is saved unless you tap Save. Removing a person deletes them; deleting the app deletes the list from the phone.
  • Whether the app is unlocked, so it does not have to ask the App Store every time it opens.
  • When the app last asked for a rating, described below.

The list above describes the iPhone app. The web version keeps its own copy of the list and the unlock in your browser, as described under The web version below.

A weeks value you type but do not save is used for the calculation on screen and is not stored. The conception date the arithmetic passes through is never stored and never shown, only the sign; the reason is in the section below about what this app will not tell you.

Your own backups. Like every app's data, the saved list is part of your phone's own iCloud or computer backups if you use them, and comes back if you restore one. That copy is yours, held by Apple or on your computer under your account; we have no access to it.

Why the pregnancy length matters

A birth date on its own is ordinary. How many weeks a pregnancy lasted is health information, about the birth and about the person who gave birth. It is asked for because it is the only thing that makes the answer specific rather than a fixed shift from the birth sign, and it is optional.

So, exactly: it stays on your device. It is kept only if you save that person, it is deleted when you remove them, and the app never sends it anywhere, including to Apple and to us.

What this app will not tell you

It names a sign. It does not state a conception date. Naming the zodiac sign of a conception season is a toy. Asserting the date on which two named people conceived a child is a claim about their private lives and, if anyone relied on it, a medical claim as well. The app is built so that it does not make one.

Where the pregnancy length is unknown the answer is genuinely uncertain, and the app shows that rather than hiding it behind a single confident word. It is entertainment, not obstetrics, and not a way to establish when anything happened.

Sharing

After the unlock, Share this card makes a picture of the result card on your device and opens your phone's own share sheet. The picture shows the two signs. Its one line of text names the two signs and the app, and says so when the sign is one of several possible. Neither carries a date, a name or the weeks. Where it goes is your choice in that sheet; the app sends nothing by itself.

Children

The birth being described is often a child's. Anything about that child, or anyone else, that you choose to save is stored on this device only and never transmitted by the app. The app collects nothing about anyone.

Who else is involved

On iPhone, Apple and nobody else. The unlock is a one-time purchase through the App Store. It shows the conception sign for any birthday, the signs it could also be, the share card and the saved list; without it the app shows the birth sign and how many conception signs are possible. Apple handles the payment and tells the app only that the purchase exists. Apple never receives a birth date, a pregnancy length, a name you typed, or anything else from this app.

On the web version, at carrierpress.com/conceptionzodiac/app/, a web edition is sold separately by Gumroad, which handles that payment and your receipt on its own pages. Paying happens there, not on this site. Who else is involved on the web, and what is kept, is in the next section.

The app is built from standard components that all run on your device: the App Store purchase library, local storage, the date wheels, icons, drawing and animation, and the picture maker for the share card. None of them sends what you type anywhere. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no crash reporting and no language model. If that ever changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top will tell you so.

The web version

The license key. When you paste the key from your Gumroad receipt, the page sends Gumroad the key and a product number: this app's, and, if Gumroad does not know the key for this app, the Carrier Press Access membership's. No birth date, pregnancy length or name goes with it.

What happens to the key depends on what it is for. A key for this app alone is kept only in the encrypted cookie described under The key check on the web; the app's own storage remembers only that this app is unlocked. A Carrier Press Access key is kept, because a membership can end. It is stored as plain text in this browser's storage for carrierpress.com, in a record the other Carrier Press web apps on this site also read, so one key opens each of them. This app checks that key with Gumroad again when you open it, at most once a day and without you tapping anything, sending the same two things and nothing else. If Gumroad says the membership has ended or does not cover apps, or that the key does not exist, this app erases the key and locks again. If Gumroad cannot be reached, it stays unlocked for up to seven days from the last good check.

What stays in your browser. People you save stay in that browser, on that device. They are not sent anywhere and do not follow a key to another browser. A birth date or weeks value you do not save is gone when you leave the page. The storage belongs to carrierpress.com as a whole and the Carrier Press web apps share it, so clearing the site's data removes the saved people, the unlock and any stored key, for this app and for those apps in that browser.

The page itself. The page's files come from the web host for carrierpress.com, which receives the ordinary details of any page request, such as your internet address and browser. Nothing you typed is in those requests.

Differences from the iPhone app. Share this result passes a single line of text, the one described above, to your browser's share sheet, or copies it to your clipboard if the browser has no share sheet; there is no picture. There is no App Store purchase and no Restore Purchases: pasting the same key again brings the unlock back without paying again. The rating prompt described below is iPhone only, and the web version keeps no such note. Contact support opens whatever your browser uses for email, with the app's name and version in the subject.

Deleting it

There is no account to close. Removing a person deletes them, and removing the app from your device deletes the saved list from it. A copy can remain only in your own device backups, as above. On iPhone your purchase is held by Apple rather than by us, so reinstalling and tapping Restore Purchases brings the unlock back without paying again. On the web, clearing the site's data deletes the saved list and the unlock, and a purchase is held by Gumroad.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.

The key check on the web

The web version opens only with a license key. When you paste your key on this app's page, it goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. Nothing else of yours goes with it.

If the key is good, your browser keeps it in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, with the times Gumroad last answered. The cookie goes to the same server whenever you open the app, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad about the key again, so that access ends when a subscription does. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors. The app may also keep the key in your browser's own storage, as this page describes. Remove my key from this browser, on the app's page, deletes both.

carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.
