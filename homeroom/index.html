Homeroom support

Last updated 24 November 2026.

Homeroom keeps one family's homeschool records: attendance, the work and reading you log through the year, and what your state asks of you, with the statute beside each requirement.

Homeroom in your browser

Homeroom is on sale now in a browser, from the Homeroom page. The iPhone app is not out yet, so this is the version you can buy today. It is bought on Gumroad, not from Apple.

  • Your key. Subscribe on Gumroad, monthly or yearly, or buy the school-year pass once (the prices are on the Homeroom page). The receipt email carries a license key. Paste it on the Homeroom page, under Already have a key?, in each browser you use. Keep that email: you need the key again in any other browser. A Carrier Press Access key on a tier that includes the apps opens Homeroom too, for as long as that membership lasts.
  • There is no account. No password and no sign-in code. Your students, attendance, work samples with their photos and PDFs, reading log, courses and the requirements you ticked are kept in that browser, on that device, and not on our server. Another browser or device cannot see them.
  • Keep a copy. Clearing this site's data in your browser, or a private window once it closes, erases your records, and Safari can clear a site's data by itself after about a week without a visit. We cannot bring them back, because we never had a copy. Under Your copy on Account, Download a copy now and then; it holds everything, photos and PDFs included. Restore from a copy adds what is missing and never replaces what is there, which is also how you move to another browser.
  • Reminders. A browser cannot remind you when it is closed. On The year, Add to my calendar puts the requirements still open this year that have a month into your own calendar, each on the first day of its month. The rules give a month, not a day, so read the statute for the exact deadline.
  • Cancelling. A monthly or yearly membership renews until you cancel. Use Manage membership in your Gumroad receipt email or your Gumroad library, or write to support@carrierpress.com and we will cancel it for you. The period you paid for runs out first. The school-year pass never renews: it ends after the first June 30 that follows the day you bought it. Either way, once it ends everything you recorded stays readable and downloadable in that browser, and new entries pause.
  • Refunds. Apple has nothing to do with a web purchase, so write to support@carrierpress.com about one. A refunded key stops opening Homeroom.
  • Leaving a shared computer. Lock Homeroom in this browser forgets your key here and keeps your records. A key that has ended cannot open Homeroom again after that, so download a copy first. Erase Homeroom from this browser removes every record for good. Neither one cancels your Gumroad purchase.

Where the rules come from, and what they are not

Every requirement Homeroom shows carries two things: the statute it comes from, and the date Homeroom last checked it. A requirement with no citation is a rumor, and an app that hid how old its legal information was would make the staleness invisible, which is worse than being out of date.

These are summaries written for a parent, not legal advice. Homeroom is not a state agency and is not connected to one, and it does not decide whether your family is compliant, because that is a determination a state makes. The citation is there so you can read the source, and your state's department of education or a state homeschool organization is the authority.

Why it asks which state before anything else

A compliance tracker that does not know your state is tracking nothing, so the picker is a wall rather than an empty list. Ten states are covered: Texas, Florida, North Carolina, California, Ohio, Georgia, Pennsylvania, New York, Michigan and Illinois.

You can change your state on the Account screen, and anything you already marked stays recorded under the state it was filed under. Each requirement can be marked Done, or Not us for rules that do not apply to your family, and one with a month attached says when it is coming up until it is marked.

Attendance, days and hours

One tap marks a student in for today, and with more than one student there is a button that marks everybody in at once. Hours are optional, because several states want them and several do not. Tapping again on the same day is a correction rather than a second day, so a year cannot quietly accumulate four hundred days; use Undo to take today back off.

Where days have no hours against them, Homeroom states how many rather than warning you about it. It does not know whether your state accepts days without hours. The rule text on The year screen does.

The school year starts in August, so a day logged in July counts toward the year that began the previous August.

Work, reading and photos

Work is logged with a title and an optional subject, either as a note or with a photograph of it. Reading is its own list, with titles and authors, because some states ask for titles of reading materials specifically.

In a browser, a photo or PDF is kept in that browser with the rest of your records. A photo is made smaller first, and a PDF over 10 MB is not kept: the work is noted without it. In the iPhone app, photographs and uploaded documents go to private storage only your account can read. Either way that matters: in several states a filed affidavit or an evaluator letter carries a home address and a signature.

The transcript

Homeroom can produce a plain text transcript for a student. In a browser it downloads as a text file, and it asks for the name to print as the issuer. It says what it is in its first three lines: a home education transcript, issued by the parent or guardian named on it, and not issued by an accredited institution. It states no grade point average, because a self-issued average is the number an admissions office trusts least.

In a browser, Portfolio also offers Print this school year (or save as PDF) under Work and Reading, which puts one student's attendance, work with its photos, reading and courses on paper.

In the iPhone app

The iPhone app is not out yet. Its subscription will be billed through your Apple ID, and its Account screen shows whether it is active. Managing or cancelling happens at Settings › your name › Subscriptions. Cancelling stops the renewal and leaves the current period running to its end. We cannot cancel an Apple subscription for you. After a reinstall or on a new device, use Restore purchase. Refunds are requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com, since Apple is the merchant of record.

Your records

In a browser there is no account: your records stay in that browser, as above. Homeroom on the web makes no AI calls, because the state rules are part of the app. The only requests it makes are to carrierpress.com, to check your Gumroad key is still current and, when you lock it, to forget the key. The transcript, the calendar file and your copy are made in your browser and saved to your device.

In the iPhone app, signing in is a code sent to your email address; there is no password. It holds your state and school year, each child with the name, date of birth and grade you entered, their attendance, work and reading, and the files you upload. There is no other parent, no teacher and no directory, and nothing you write is sent to an AI model.

In the iPhone app, deleting your account removes the account itself and takes your children, attendance, work, reading log and uploaded files with it. It cannot be undone, and it does not cancel an App Store subscription. The full policy is at carrierpress.com/homeroom/privacy.
