Boat Ready privacy policy

Last updated 2 October 2026.

Boat Ready answers what safety equipment federal rules require a recreational boat to carry, and lets you keep that answer per boat. It works with no signal, which is the point, since the question tends to arrive at a ramp.

There is no account and no server

You do not sign in, because there is nothing to sign in to. This app has no database of ours, no cloud sync and no backend. Everything it knows is written to your phone's own storage and stays there.

What is stored, on your device

  • The boats you save: the name you gave each one, its length, its hull and engine details, and the other answers the requirements depend on.
  • Which items you have ticked off for each boat.
  • The expiry dates you type for a boat's dated gear, such as flares and fire extinguishers.
  • A boat you have described but not yet saved, so it is still there after the unlock screen.
  • Whether the app is unlocked, so the unlock works with no signal.
  • When the app last asked for a rating, described below.

That is the whole list. No name, no email address, no phone number, no location, no registration or hull number is asked for or kept, and a boat's name is whatever you decide to type.

Nothing above is ever uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.

Who else is involved

On iPhone, Apple and nobody else. The unlock is a one-time purchase made through the App Store, so Apple handles the payment and tells the app only that the purchase exists. Apple never receives anything you entered. When the app opens with a connection, it also asks the App Store whether a purchase is waiting for you, such as one a parent approved through Ask to Buy; nothing you entered goes with that question.

On the web version, at carrierpress.com/boatready/app/, the unlock is sold by Gumroad, which handles that payment. When you paste the license key from your Gumroad receipt, the page sends that key, and only that key, to Gumroad to check it. Nothing about your boats goes with it. The key is not kept afterwards; the browser remembers only that it is unlocked, and boats saved on the web stay in that browser.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking software of any kind. There is no language model. There is no server of ours for anything to be sent to.

The links that leave the app

The app has a link to the US Coast Guard's own site, because the official source should always be one tap away. Tapping it opens your browser, and what happens then is between you and the Coast Guard under their policy. The app sends nothing with you and is not told that you went. The Privacy policy link on the About tab opens this page the same way. Contact support, described below, opens your own mail app and sends nothing by itself.

Deleting it

There is no account to close. Deleting the app removes your boats and your checklists with it, and there is no copy anywhere else, so if a boat's details took a while to enter, write them down before you delete. Your purchase is held by Apple rather than by us, so reinstalling and tapping Restore brings the unlock back without paying again.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
