Boat Ready privacy policy

Last updated 19 October 2026.

Boat Ready answers what safety equipment federal rules require a recreational boat to carry, and lets you keep that answer per boat. It also names a vessel from the lights it shows at night and tells you what a buoy or beacon means, with a Practice tab for both. Conditions shows NOAA's advisories, marine forecast, tides and currents for the water you choose. Apart from fetching those, it works with no signal, which is the point, since the question tends to arrive at a ramp or on the water.

There is no account and no server

You do not sign in, because there is nothing to sign in to. This app has no database of ours, no cloud sync and no backend. Everything it knows is written to your phone's own storage and stays there.

What is stored, on your device

  • The boats you save: the name you gave each one, its length, its hull and engine details, and the other answers the requirements depend on.
  • Which items you have ticked off for each boat.
  • The dates you type for a boat's dated gear, such as the expiry on flares, or the manufacture date on a disposable fire extinguisher, which the app turns into its expiry.
  • A boat you have described but not yet saved, so it is still there after the subscription screen.
  • Whether your subscription is active, when the App Store last confirmed it and when the current period ends, so it keeps working with no signal.
  • The NOAA stations you chose for Conditions: a tide station, a current station and the marine forecast zone they fall in.
  • What NOAA last said for them: the advisories, the forecast and up to 30 days of tide and current predictions, each with the time it was fetched, so they still show offshore.
  • Which lights, buoys and sound signals you have answered right in Practice, so it can tell you what you have left to learn. Nothing you type goes into this.
  • Whether night red is on, the red on black palette you turn on with the eye button, on any screen, to keep your night vision.
  • When the app last asked for a rating, described below.

That is the whole list. No name, no email address, no phone number, no registration or hull number is asked for, no location is kept, and a boat's name is whatever you decide to type.

Your location

Only if you tap "Use my location" in Conditions, and only while you are using the app. iOS asks you first. The app takes one reading, uses it on the phone to find the NOAA tide and current stations nearest you from a list of stations built into the app, and then lets it go. Your position is never sent anywhere and never stored. If you say no, you can search for a station by place name instead. There is no background location and no tracking.

Nothing above is ever uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.

Who else is involved

On iPhone: Apple, NOAA when Conditions is open, and YouTube only if you play a video.

Apple. The subscription, monthly or yearly, is sold through the App Store, so Apple handles the payment and the renewal and tells the app only whether you are subscribed and until when. It renews automatically until you cancel it in your Apple Account settings (the About screen links there). Apple never receives anything you entered. When the app opens with a connection, it also asks the App Store whether a purchase is waiting for you, such as one a parent approved through Ask to Buy; nothing you entered goes with that question.

NOAA, while Conditions is open. Conditions asks NOAA's public services (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov and api.weather.gov, a US government agency) for the advisories, forecast, tides and currents. What goes with each request: the station numbers you chose, the dates wanted, the marine zone, and, when you save stations, the published position of a station you chose, to find its zone. Never your own position and nothing you typed. NOAA sees your connection the way any website would, such as your IP address. There is no account and no key.

On the web version, at carrierpress.com/boatready/app/, the unlock is sold by Gumroad, which handles that payment. When you paste the license key from your Gumroad receipt, the page sends that key, and only that key, to Gumroad to check it. Nothing about your boats goes with it. The key is not kept afterwards; the browser remembers only that it is unlocked, and boats saved on the web stay in that browser.

Videos come from YouTube, and only when you tap Play. A few cards, and the Buoys tab, carry a boating safety video from America's Boating Channel, which is sponsored by the U.S. Coast Guard. Until you tap Play, nothing loads and nothing is sent. When you do tap it, the video is served by Google from youtube-nocookie.com, YouTube's privacy-enhanced player, and Google sees what it sees from any web visitor, such as your IP address and device. Nothing you entered in the app goes with it. In night red, videos do not play at all.

Listen, which reads a card or an answer aloud, uses your phone's own voice. The words are spoken on the device; nothing is sent anywhere, and the app never uses the microphone.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking software of any kind. There is no language model. There is no server of ours for anything to be sent to.

The links that leave the app

The app has two links to the US Coast Guard's own sites, its boating safety site and the full Navigation Rules on its Navigation Center, because the official source should always be one tap away. Tapping either opens your browser, and what happens then is between you and the Coast Guard under their policy. The app sends nothing with you and is not told that you went. The Privacy Policy links on the About screen (the i button) and on the subscription screen open this page the same way; Terms of Use opens Apple's standard licence, and Your subscription opens your App Store subscriptions. A link inside a video player opens outside the app. Contact support, described below, opens your own mail app and sends nothing by itself.

Deleting it

There is no account to close. Deleting the app removes your boats and your checklists with it, along with your stations and saved Conditions, and there is no copy anywhere else, so if a boat's details took a while to enter, write them down before you delete. Deleting the app does not cancel the subscription; do that in your Apple Account settings. Your subscription is held by Apple rather than by us, so reinstalling and tapping Restore my subscription brings it back without paying again.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
