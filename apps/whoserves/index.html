Questions

Who makes Who Serves, and does it take a political side?

Jeffrey L. Carrier, one independent developer, under the Carrier Press imprint, with no advertising and no investors. It is not a government service and not affiliated with any agency, party or campaign. There is no rating, score, grade or endorsement of anyone, and none will be added. It shows the record and leaves the judging to you.

Where does each fact come from, and how fresh is it?

Every name shows where it was read and when it was last checked. Districts come from the US Census Bureau, Congress from the Senate and the Clerk of the House, state legislators from Open States, which compiles them from the states, and county offices from the county or a statewide directory. Congress and the state legislatures are read again every week. When a check is too old, or an election has passed since, the app says so rather than vouching for the name.

Can I see it before I pay?

Yes, on this page: part of the record for one example address, with where each line was read, and screens from the app. The app itself opens only with a key, and there is no free trial.

Exactly what is charged, and does it renew?

On the web, $14.99 a month or $143.99 a year, whichever you pick at Gumroad's checkout. Gumroad takes the payment. It renews automatically until you cancel. Your license key is on the Gumroad receipt email. Paste it on this page once in each browser you use. A web subscription opens the app in browsers, not in the iPhone app.

How do I cancel, and what happens after?

Cancel any time from your Gumroad receipt or library. The subscriber tools stay open for the time you already paid for, then close once Gumroad reports the subscription ended, which the page checks about once a day. A failed renewal payment closes them the same way. The browser you used can still read the addresses you saved. A new browser needs an active subscription.

What happens to my address?

It goes to the US Census Bureau's geocoder to find your districts, and nowhere else. Nothing you look up is kept. If you subscribe and save an address, it stays in your own browser until you delete it. There is no account. Your license key goes to a small key-checking server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad about it and stores nothing. The key is then kept in an encrypted cookie in your browser and in the app's own storage there, so the page can check it again about once a day. The privacy policy has the details.
