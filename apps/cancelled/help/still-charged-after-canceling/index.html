Canceled: free guide

Still being charged after you canceled? What to do

A charge after you canceled is not the end of the road. It usually has one of three causes, and each has a fix. Work down the list in order.

First, check that you canceled the right thing

A frequent cause is canceling in the wrong place. A plan bought inside an iPhone or Android app is billed by Apple or Google; deleting the app or canceling inside it does nothing. The same goes for a plan billed through PayPal, through your phone company, or as an add-on to another membership: it is canceled where it is billed.

Another is timing. Many plans run to the end of the period you have paid for, and a charge dated before you canceled is not a new one. Compare the charge date with your confirmation.

Second, put it in writing

When a company has no cancel button, insists on a call, or keeps billing after it said it had stopped, write to it. Say what you are canceling, the date you first asked, and that any charge after that date is not authorized. Email it if the company lists an address for it; otherwise send it by certified mail with a return receipt. Some gyms ask for a letter as their own route. Keep a copy: it is what a dispute rests on.

Canceled does the reading for you. Open a year of statements and it lists every recurring charge, ranked by what it costs a year, on your own device, with nothing uploaded and no bank login. Seeing the list costs nothing. One payment, never a subscription, shows where to cancel each one.

Third, have your card issuer or bank stop it

  • On a credit or debit card: call the number on the back and dispute the charge as canceled. Have your confirmation or your letter ready. Card issuers set time limits on disputes, often counted from the statement date, so do not wait.
  • From a checking account: ask your bank to stop payments to the company, a stop payment on the automatic debit, and dispute any taken after the date you canceled.

Keep the paper trail

A screenshot of the confirmation page, the confirmation email, the letter and its receipt, and a note of every call with its date. With those, a dispute is usually short.
