CDL Doubles and Triples Practice Test

Your state's licensing agency gives the T endorsement knowledge test. Whether triples may run at all is set by each state; check your state's CDL manual and size and weight rules.

These are the first 10 of the 30 questions in Endorsed's Doubles and Triples (T) Endorsement pack, with the first of its 5 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. Which tests does the T endorsement require?

    1. A knowledge test only
    2. A knowledge and a skills test
    3. A skills test only
    Show the answer

    A. A knowledge test only

    Double/triple trailers is a knowledge test only.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.93

  2. Can a learner's permit carry the T endorsement?

    1. Yes
    2. No
    3. Only for doubles
    Show the answer

    B. No

    The only endorsements allowed on a CLP are P, S and N.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.25

  3. Which of these is a topic the T test must cover?

    1. Oscillatory sway
    2. Passenger loading
    3. Cargo tank retesting
    Show the answer

    A. Oscillatory sway

    Handling and stability, including oscillatory sway, is listed.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.115

  4. A semitrailer sits on a converter dolly behind the first trailer. What is it under the rules?

    1. A semitrailer
    2. A full trailer
    3. A pole trailer
    Show the answer

    B. A full trailer

    A semitrailer with a converter dolly is considered a full trailer.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  5. Why must a converter dolly have safety chains or cables?

    1. To hold up the air lines and glad hands
    2. To hold the trailer if the tow-bar fails
    3. To help steer the dolly in turns
    Show the answer

    B. To hold the trailer if the tow-bar fails

    The safety device keeps the towed unit attached if the tow-bar fails or disconnects.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.70

  6. Where may the safety chains not be attached?

    1. To the frame of the towing trailer
    2. To the pintle hook that holds the tow-bar
    3. To the dolly's tow-bar
    Show the answer

    B. To the pintle hook that holds the tow-bar

    The safety device must not be attached to the pintle hook or other device the tow-bar uses.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.70

  7. How strong must the safety chains and their attachments be?

    1. At least half the towed weight
    2. At least the full towed weight
    3. At least twice the dolly's weight
    Show the answer

    B. At least the full towed weight

    Their ultimate strength must be at least the gross weight of the vehicle or vehicles towed.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.70

  8. A trailer breaks away from the combination. What must its brakes do?

    1. Nothing until the driver acts
    2. Apply automatically and immediately
    3. Apply after 30 seconds
    Show the answer

    B. Apply automatically and immediately

    Trailers that must have brakes must have them apply automatically and immediately on breakaway.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.43

  9. Between what pressures must the tractor protection valve close automatically?

    1. 20 and 45 psi
    2. 60 and 80 psi
    3. 90 and 120 psi
    Show the answer

    A. 20 and 45 psi

    Between 20 psi and 45 psi.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.43

  10. On a set of doubles, how should the rear air shut-off valves be set?

    1. All open, front to back
    2. Open, except at the very back
    3. All closed, front to back
    Show the answer

    B. Open, except at the very back

    Air must reach every trailer's brakes (393.48), so state manuals have the valves open at the back of the first trailer and closed at the back of the last.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.48

Study: What the T endorsement covers

Pulling double or triple trailers needs the T endorsement. It takes a knowledge test only, no skills test.

The test must cover how the units are assembled and hooked up; where the heaviest trailer goes; handling and stability, including off-tracking, response to steering, braking, oscillatory sway, rollover in steady turns and yaw stability; and the problems a long combination makes for other drivers, such as slow climbing on grades, long passing times, blocking freeway entrances, splash and spray, buffeting, blocked views and lane position.

A learner's permit may not carry the T endorsement; only P, S and N are allowed on a permit.

The endorsement is issued by your state, and only to a driver who passes its test.

Source: 49 CFR 383.93; 49 CFR 383.115; 49 CFR 383.25
