Limo and Chauffeur Practice Questions

Chauffeur, livery and for-hire licenses are issued by your state and often by your city or airport, each with its own test or none. This pack covers the federal rules that apply on top of them; ask your state licensing agency and local livery authority for theirs.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's Limousine and Chauffeur pack, with the first of its 5 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. A for-hire limousine is designed to carry 10 people counting the driver and makes an interstate trip. Is it a commercial motor vehicle under the safety rules?

    1. No, it needs 16 seats
    2. Yes, it carries over 8 for hire
    3. Only if it weighs over 26,000 pounds
    Show the answer

    B. Yes, it carries over 8 for hire

    More than 8 passengers, including the driver, for compensation makes it a commercial motor vehicle.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  2. What is the passenger threshold for a vehicle not used for compensation?

    1. More than 8 counting the driver
    2. More than 15 counting the driver
    3. 16 or more not counting the driver
    Show the answer

    B. More than 15 counting the driver

    More than 15 passengers, including the driver, when not used to transport passengers for compensation.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  3. Does the federal definition of a bus include a taxicab?

    1. Yes
    2. No
    3. Only with more than 8 seats
    Show the answer

    A. Yes

    Bus means any motor vehicle designed, built or used to carry passengers, including taxicabs.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  4. Which weight alone makes a vehicle a commercial motor vehicle under the safety rules, in interstate commerce?

    1. 8,500 pounds
    2. 10,001 pounds or more
    3. 26,001 pounds or more
    Show the answer

    B. 10,001 pounds or more

    A rating or weight of 10,001 pounds or more, whichever is greater.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  5. Your limo runs only within one state. Whose rules govern it?

    1. Only FMCSA's
    2. Your state's
    3. No one's, if it stays local
    Show the answer

    B. Your state's

    The federal definition covers vehicles used in interstate commerce; intrastate work is the state's to regulate.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  6. A limousine seats 12 counting the driver and weighs 9,000 pounds. Does the driver need a CDL under the federal rule?

    1. Yes, Class C
    2. No
    3. Yes, Class B
    Show the answer

    B. No

    The CDL threshold for a light passenger vehicle is Group C: designed for 16 or more including the driver.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.5

  7. A party bus is designed for 20 people counting the driver and weighs 14,000 pounds. Which CDL group is it?

    1. Group A
    2. Group B
    3. Group C
    Show the answer

    C. Group C

    Under 26,001 pounds but designed for 16 or more: Group C.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.5

  8. What else does that party bus driver need on the CDL?

    1. The passenger endorsement
    2. The tank endorsement
    3. Nothing else
    Show the answer

    A. The passenger endorsement

    Driving a passenger vehicle that needs a CDL takes the P endorsement.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.93

  9. A church van weighs 8,000 pounds, seats 12 counting the driver and is not used for direct compensation. Which federal rule still applies?

    1. The ban on texting and hand-held phones
    2. The 10-hour driving limit
    3. The medical card
    Show the answer

    A. The ban on texting and hand-held phones

    The 9 to 15 passenger exception keeps the texting and phone bans and their disqualifications.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.3T

  10. A van carries 12 workers on one daily round trip to and from their jobs. Does the federal passenger carrier financial responsibility rule reach it?

    1. Yes, like any for-hire van
    2. No, a commute trip for fewer than 16 is excepted
    3. Only if it crosses two state lines
    Show the answer

    B. No, a commute trip for fewer than 16 is excepted

    387.27 excepts a vehicle carrying fewer than 16 people in a single daily round trip to commute to and from work.

    Source: 49 CFR 387.27

Study: When a passenger vehicle is a commercial motor vehicle

Under the federal safety rules, a vehicle used on a highway in interstate commerce is a commercial motor vehicle if it is designed or used to carry more than 8 passengers, counting the driver, for compensation, or more than 15 passengers, counting the driver, not for compensation. Weight also counts: 10,001 pounds or more makes it one regardless of seats.

So a for-hire stretch limousine or van seating 9 or more, counting you, on an interstate trip, is a commercial motor vehicle and the federal carrier rules apply. A sedan or SUV built for 8 or fewer, counting you, and carrying no more, is not, unless it is heavy enough.

The rules' definition of a bus covers any motor vehicle designed, built or used to carry passengers, including taxicabs.

Trips that stay within one state are regulated by that state, which may or may not adopt the federal rules.

The definition says designed or used. A vehicle built for fewer seats still counts once it is used to carry more than 8 people, counting the driver, for compensation. A trip between two places in one state is interstate commerce if the route passes through another state.

Charter transportation means a group with a common purpose that hires the whole vehicle under one contract at a fixed charge for the vehicle, for its exclusive use, on a route set in advance or changed along the way.

Source: 49 CFR 390.5T
