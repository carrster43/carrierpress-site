Federal Fleet Driving Practice Questions

There is no federal fleet driving exam. Your agency authorizes who drives its vehicles and may add its own training; this pack covers the government-wide rules in the Federal Management Regulation.

These are the first 10 of the 29 questions in Endorsed's Federal Fleet Driving pack, with the first of its 4 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

See Endorsed

10 practice questions

  1. Which of these is a Government motor vehicle?

    1. Only a vehicle the Government holds title to
    2. Any it owns or leases, GSA Fleet included
    3. Any vehicle an employee drives while on duty
    Show the answer

    B. Any it owns or leases, GSA Fleet included

    It includes vehicles obtained by purchase, excess, forfeiture, commercial lease or GSA Fleet lease.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.35

  2. An agency gets a car from a dealer for 60 days. What is that?

    1. A commercial lease
    2. A rental
    3. A GSA Fleet lease
    Show the answer

    B. A rental

    Less than 120 continuous days from a commercial source is a rental; 120 or more is a commercial lease.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.35

  3. Which vehicle is outside the motor vehicle management part?

    1. A GSA Fleet sedan
    2. A military design tactical vehicle
    3. A commercially leased pickup
    Show the answer

    B. A military design tactical vehicle

    Military design vehicles and those used for combat or tactical purposes are not covered.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.20

  4. What may a Government motor vehicle be used for?

    1. Anything while you are on the clock
    2. Your agency's missions, as it authorizes
    3. Any trip under 50 miles, on duty or not
    Show the answer

    B. Your agency's missions, as it authorizes

    Permitted use is performing the agency's missions as the agency authorizes.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.200

  5. May you drive the agency car home each night because the bus is slow?

    1. Yes, if your supervisor agrees in writing
    2. No, convenience never justifies it
    3. Yes, on weekdays only
    Show the answer

    B. No, convenience never justifies it

    Home-to-work needs an agency head determination for a listed reason; convenience is not one.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.205

  6. Who may authorize home-to-work transportation?

    1. Your supervisor or team lead
    2. Only the agency head
    3. The agency's fleet manager
    Show the answer

    B. Only the agency head

    The agency head may not delegate the authority to make home-to-work determinations.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.205

  7. How long can a first home-to-work determination for field work last?

    1. No longer than 90 days
    2. No longer than one year
    3. No longer than two years
    Show the answer

    C. No longer than two years

    Initial determinations for field work last no longer than two years; for other reasons, fifteen days.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.205

  8. Which reason can support home-to-work transportation?

    1. Field work
    2. Saving the employee parking costs
    3. The employee lives far away
    Show the answer

    A. Field work

    Field work, clear and present danger, emergency, or a compelling operational consideration.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.205

  9. You are on temporary duty in another city. Does the home-to-work rule stop you driving the government car from your hotel to the work site?

    1. Yes
    2. No
    3. Only on weekends
    Show the answer

    B. No

    The section does not apply to home-to-work use in conjunction with TDY or relocation.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.205

  10. How long is a determination allowing shuttles to mass transit valid?

    1. Six months
    2. One year
    3. Two years
    Show the answer

    B. One year

    A written agency head determination valid for one year.

    Source: 41 CFR 102-34.210

Study: What the rule covers

The part governs motor vehicles the Government owns, leases commercially, or leases through GSA Fleet. Executive agencies must follow all of it, and its home-to-work section also reaches employees of the judicial and legislative branches, except the Senate, the House, the Architect of the Capitol and the District of Columbia government.

A Government motor vehicle is any motor vehicle the Government owns or leases, including vehicles obtained by purchase, excess, forfeiture, commercial lease or GSA Fleet lease.

Not covered: military design vehicles, vehicles used for field training, combat or tactical purposes, vehicles used mainly inside a military post, camp or depot, and investigative, law enforcement or intelligence vehicles under the exclusive control the agency head finds essential. Those last are still bound by the use rules in the part's subpart D, except the home-to-work section, and by its subpart J.

A commercial lease is 120 continuous days or more from a commercial source. Anything shorter is a rental.

A Government-owned motor vehicle is one the Government holds title to, whether it came by purchase, excess, forfeiture or some other way. A commercially leased vehicle is still a Government motor vehicle, but the Government does not hold its title, so it is not Government-owned.

Source: 41 CFR 102-34.5; 41 CFR 102-34.20; 41 CFR 102-34.35
