CDL Air Brakes Practice Test

Your state gives the air brakes knowledge test and checks the air brake inspection in the skills test. Without it, your CDL carries an air brake restriction.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's CDL Air Brakes pack, with the first of its 5 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. What tells the compressor to stop pumping air?

    1. The safety valve
    2. The governor
    3. The brake pedal
    Show the answer

    B. The governor

    The governor controls when the compressor pumps: off at cut-out, on again at cut-in.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

  2. Why are air reservoirs drained?

    1. To lower brake temperature
    2. To remove water and oil that collect in them
    3. To test the governor
    Show the answer

    B. To remove water and oil that collect in them

    Water and oil in the tanks can freeze or damage the brakes.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

  3. What does the slack adjuster do?

    1. Turns the brake camshaft when the pushrod moves
    2. Stores air for the trailer
    3. Warns of low air
    Show the answer

    A. Turns the brake camshaft when the pushrod moves

    The pushrod moves the slack adjuster, which turns the brake cam and spreads the shoes.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

  4. Where must the air pressure gauge be?

    1. Under the hood
    2. Where the driver can see it from the seat
    3. On the trailer
    Show the answer

    B. Where the driver can see it from the seat

    The gauge must be visible to a person seated in the normal driving position.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.51

  5. On a truck built after 1975, the low air warning must come on when pressure is below:

    1. 100 psi
    2. 60 psi
    3. 20 psi
    Show the answer

    B. 60 psi

    FMVSS 121, applied by 49 CFR 393.51, requires the warning below 60 psi. Some state test sheets use 55 psi or the manufacturer's level.

    Source: 49 CFR 571.121

  6. What holds spring brakes off?

    1. Air pressure
    2. Hydraulic fluid
    3. The parking brake cable
    Show the answer

    A. Air pressure

    Air holds the springs back; lose the air and the springs apply the brakes.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

  7. As air pressure falls, spring brakes usually come on by themselves between:

    1. 60 and 80 psi
    2. 20 and 45 psi
    3. 5 and 10 psi
    Show the answer

    B. 20 and 45 psi

    Manuals give 20 to 45 psi, the same range the federal rule sets for the tractor protection valve.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

  8. A trailer breaks away from the tractor. What must happen to its brakes?

    1. Nothing until the driver acts
    2. They apply by themselves
    3. Only the parking brake works
    Show the answer

    B. They apply by themselves

    Trailer brakes must apply automatically on breakaway.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.43

  9. An air-braked truck tractor needs antilock brakes if it was built on or after:

    1. March 1, 1997
    2. January 1, 2005
    3. March 1, 2010
    Show the answer

    A. March 1, 1997

    Tractors from March 1, 1997; other air-braked trucks, buses and trailers from March 1, 1998.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.55

  10. The antilock malfunction lamp stays on. What still works?

    1. Nothing, pull over and wait
    2. Your normal brakes, without the antilock function
    3. Only the parking brake
    Show the answer

    B. Your normal brakes, without the antilock function

    A fault turns off the antilock function; normal braking remains.

    Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section (state test figure)

Study: How the system works

An engine-driven compressor pumps air into storage reservoirs. A governor stops the compressor pumping when the reservoirs reach the cut-out pressure (around 125 psi) and starts it again when pressure falls to the cut-in pressure (around 100 psi).

Pressing the brake pedal sends air to the brake chambers, which push the pushrods, turn the slack adjusters and press the brake shoes against the drums.

Moisture and oil collect in the reservoirs. Every reservoir has a drain valve that can be worked by hand. Manuals say to drain tanks without automatic drains at the end of each working day.

A safety valve, usually set to open at 150 psi, releases air if reservoir pressure climbs too high.

A check valve, or a device that does the same job, must keep the service reservoirs from losing air if something fails between them and the compressor, and you must be able to test it without disconnecting any line. An automatic drain valve is allowed only if it can also be worked by hand or a manual way to drain the tank is kept.

The brake chambers on the two ends of an axle must be the same size, and so must the effective length of the two slack adjusters. Air-braked vehicles built on or after October 20, 1994 must have automatic brake adjusters, and where an external automatic adjuster has an exposed pushrod, a brake adjustment indicator must show when that brake is under-adjusted.

Source: State CDL manual, Air Brakes section; 49 CFR 393.50 (state test figures)
