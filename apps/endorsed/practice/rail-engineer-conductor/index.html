Locomotive Engineer and Conductor Practice Questions

There is no single federal exam. Each railroad certifies its own engineers (49 CFR 240) and conductors (49 CFR 242) under a written certification program it files with the FRA; the program takes effect 30 days after filing unless the FRA tells the railroad it does not conform to those parts. The railroad writes and gives the knowledge test, and for engineers the skills test, on its own operating rules and territory. This pack covers the federal rules every program rests on, not any railroad's rule book.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's Locomotive Engineer and Conductor pack, with the first of its 10 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. An employee moves a locomotive 60 feet on a running track purely so it can be inspected. Is that a locomotive engineer move under Part 240?

    1. Yes, any move of a locomotive counts
    2. No, it is an excepted incidental move
    Show the answer

    B. No, it is an excepted incidental move

    Incidental moves of less than 100 feet for inspection or maintenance purposes are outside the definition of locomotive engineer.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.7

  2. Under Part 242, who is the conductor?

    1. The most senior employee on the crew
    2. Whoever handles the radio for the crew
    3. The crewmember in charge of the crew
    4. The person at the locomotive controls
    Show the answer

    C. The crewmember in charge of the crew

    Part 242 defines the conductor as the crewmember in charge of a train or yard crew.

    Source: 49 CFR 242.7

  3. What may a locomotive servicing engineer not do?

    1. Move locomotives with cars coupled
    2. Move locomotives in multiple
    3. Move a single locomotive
    Show the answer

    A. Move locomotives with cars coupled

    Locomotive servicing engineers may operate locomotives singly or in multiple but not with cars coupled to them.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.107

  4. How may a student engineer operate?

    1. Alone, but only on yard tracks
    2. Under an instructor engineer's direct, immediate supervision
    3. Only with a conductor riding in the cab
    Show the answer

    B. Under an instructor engineer's direct, immediate supervision

    Student engineers may operate only under the direct and immediate supervision of an instructor engineer.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.107

  5. What is the longest interval for which a railroad may certify an engineer?

    1. 12 months
    2. 24 months
    3. 36 months
    4. 60 months
    Show the answer

    C. 36 months

    No railroad may certify a person as an engineer for an interval of more than 36 months.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.217

  6. Which driving record entry may a railroad consider for engineer certification?

    1. A speeding ticket
    2. A DUI conviction within 36 months
    3. A parking violation
    4. A DUI from five years ago
    Show the answer

    B. A DUI conviction within 36 months

    Only impaired driving or test refusal convictions or completed state actions within 36 months count.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.115

  7. How soon must a conductor report a DUI conviction to the employing railroad?

    1. Within 48 hours
    2. Within 7 days
    3. At the next recertification
    Show the answer

    A. Within 48 hours

    Such incidents must be reported within 48 hours of the conviction or completed state action.

    Source: 49 CFR 242.111

  8. What is the minimum horizontal field of vision in each eye?

    1. 50 degrees
    2. 120 degrees
    3. 70 degrees
    Show the answer

    C. 70 degrees

    A field of vision of at least 70 degrees in the horizontal meridian in each eye.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.121

  9. An engineer candidate fails the knowledge test. When may the person work as an engineer?

    1. Right away, under supervision
    2. After 30 days
    3. Only after passing a reexamination
    Show the answer

    C. Only after passing a reexamination

    No railroad may let the person function as an engineer before achieving a passing score on reexamination.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.125

  10. Who conducts an engineer's skills performance test?

    1. A Designated Supervisor of Locomotive Engineers
    2. An FRA operating practices inspector
    3. The train dispatcher on that territory
    Show the answer

    A. A Designated Supervisor of Locomotive Engineers

    The skills test is conducted by a Designated Supervisor of Locomotive Engineers.

    Source: 49 CFR 240.127

Study: Who needs a certificate

Part 240 sets minimum federal standards for the eligibility, training, testing, certification and monitoring of locomotive engineers, so that only qualified persons operate a locomotive or train. Part 242 does the same for conductors.

A locomotive engineer is anyone who moves a locomotive or group of locomotives, coupled to cars or not, whatever the job title. The exceptions are moves inside a locomotive repair or servicing area, and incidental moves of less than 100 feet for inspection or maintenance.

A conductor is the crewmember in charge of a train or yard crew. A passenger conductor is a conductor who has also had the emergency preparedness training of Part 239.

Every railroad must have a written certification program for each, and a new railroad files it with the FRA at least 60 days before it begins operations. The program takes effect 30 days after filing unless the FRA finds it does not conform. A railroad may add stricter requirements of its own, so your railroad's program and operating rules are what you are actually tested on. This pack does not cover or quote any railroad's rule book.

Parts 240 and 242 do not apply to a plant railroad that runs only on track inside an installation outside the general railroad system, to tourist or excursion operations outside that system, or to urban rapid transit not connected to it. A plant railroad moves goods only for the facility's own industrial processes; a plant that switches cars for other entities, even inside its own fence, is not one.

Running specialized roadway maintenance equipment needs no engineer certificate. A dual purpose vehicle needs none only while it is used for maintenance of way work, moves under the rules for roadway equipment, and is run by someone trained under 214.341, 214.343 and 214.355. Any other use needs an engineer who holds a Part 240 certificate.

Source: 49 CFR 240.1; 49 CFR 240.7; 49 CFR 240.101; 49 CFR 240.103; 49 CFR 242.1; 49 CFR 242.7; 49 CFR 242.101; 49 CFR 242.103; 49 CFR 240.3; 49 CFR 242.3; 49 CFR 240.104
