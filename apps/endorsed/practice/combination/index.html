CDL Combination Vehicles Practice Test

Your state's licensing agency gives the combination vehicles knowledge test, and the Class A skills test that follows it. Check your state's CDL manual for how it runs both.

These are the first 10 of the 60 questions in Endorsed's Combination Vehicles pack, with the first of its 8 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. A combination has a gross combination weight rating of 34,000 pounds. For it to need Class A, the towed unit must be rated:

    1. Over 5,000 pounds
    2. Over 10,000 pounds
    3. Over 26,000 pounds
    Show the answer

    B. Over 10,000 pounds

    Group A needs a GCWR of 26,001 pounds or more with the towed vehicle or vehicles rated over 10,000 pounds.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.91(a)

  2. You take the Class A pre-trip inspection test in a tractor-trailer. Besides the parts every vehicle has, what must you be able to explain?

    1. The trailer's bill of lading and the load's weight
    2. The tractor's fuel mileage
    3. The special features of a tractor trailer
    Show the answer

    C. The special features of a tractor trailer

    The pre-trip skills list adds the special features of a tractor trailer when one is the test vehicle.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.113(a)(1)

  3. Which is one of the three knowledge areas the federal rule adds for combination vehicle drivers?

    1. Coupling and uncoupling a tractor and semitrailer
    2. Loading and unloading passengers at stops and terminals
    3. Cargo tank baffles and surge
    Show the answer

    A. Coupling and uncoupling a tractor and semitrailer

    The combination areas are coupling and uncoupling, inspection unique to combinations, and safe operation including air brakes.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.111(c)

  4. You pass the Class A skills test in an air-braked tractor-trailer, but you failed the air brake part of the knowledge test. What goes on your CDL?

    1. Nothing, the skills test outweighs it
    2. A restriction from driving any vehicle with air brakes
    3. A restriction from pulling doubles and triples until you retest
    Show the answer

    B. A restriction from driving any vehicle with air brakes

    Failing the air brake part of the knowledge test brings the air brake restriction, whatever vehicle the skills test was taken in.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.95(a)

  5. You take the Class A skills test in a truck and trailer joined by a pintle hook. What restriction goes on your CDL?

    1. No manual transmission
    2. No air brakes
    3. No tractor-trailers coupled by a fifth wheel
    4. None
    Show the answer

    C. No tractor-trailers coupled by a fifth wheel

    Testing with a pintle hook or other non-fifth-wheel connection restricts you from fifth-wheel tractor-trailers that need Class A.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.95(d)

  6. Which endorsement does a single tractor and semitrailer need on a Class A CDL?

    1. None, the class covers it
    2. The T endorsement
    3. The N endorsement
    Show the answer

    A. None, the class covers it

    Endorsements are listed for doubles and triples, passengers, tanks, hazardous materials and school buses, not for a single trailer.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.93(b)

  7. In an air-braked Class A skills test, what must you show about the connections between tractor and trailer?

    1. That the glad hands are less than a year old and unpainted
    2. That the air connections are properly made and secured
    3. That the lines are colored red and blue
    Show the answer

    B. That the air connections are properly made and secured

    The skills rule requires you to determine that air system connections between the vehicles have been properly made and secured.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.113(a)(2)

  8. Under the federal definitions, what makes a trailer a semitrailer?

    1. It is shorter than 28 feet from its nose to its rear doors
    2. It has no brakes of its own
    3. Some of its weight rests on the towing vehicle
    Show the answer

    C. Some of its weight rests on the towing vehicle

    A semitrailer is built so that some part of its weight rests on the vehicle towing it.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  9. What must a fifth wheel's locking mechanism do?

    1. Keep the halves together until a positive manual release is worked
    2. Release by itself once the tractor comes to a stop
    3. Lock only when the trailer is loaded
    Show the answer

    A. Keep the halves together until a positive manual release is worked

    The lock must prevent separation of the upper and lower halves unless a positive manual release is activated.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.70(b)(2)

  10. May the fifth wheel release be placed so you can work it from the cab?

    1. No, it must be worked at the fifth wheel
    2. Yes, the rule allows it
    3. Only on a converter dolly
    Show the answer

    B. Yes, the rule allows it

    The rule says the release may be located so the driver can operate it from the cab.

    Source: 49 CFR 393.70(b)(2)

Study: What Class A is and what the test covers

Class A (Group A in the federal rule) is any combination of vehicles with a gross combination weight rating of 26,001 pounds or more, when the vehicle or vehicles being towed are rated over 10,000 pounds. A single vehicle rated 26,001 pounds or more that tows a trailer rated 10,000 pounds or less is Class B.

Every driver who runs combination vehicles must know three areas on top of the general knowledge: how to couple and uncouple a tractor and semitrailer; the inspection steps that are unique to combinations; and how to operate a combination safely, including its air brakes. Your state checks these in the combination vehicles knowledge test.

The skills test is taken in a vehicle that fits the group. If you test in a tractor trailer, you must be able to explain its special features during the pre-trip inspection, and, with air brakes, show that the air connections between the vehicles are properly made and secured.

Pass the Class A knowledge and skills tests and you may also drive Class B and Class C vehicles, as long as you hold any endorsement those need.

Class A is a class, not an endorsement: a single tractor and semitrailer needs no endorsement. Pulling doubles or triples does need the T endorsement.

The vehicle you test in matters. Test in a truck and trailer joined by a pintle hook or other connection that is not a fifth wheel, and your CDL is restricted from tractor-trailers coupled by a fifth wheel. Test in a vehicle without air brakes, or fail the air brake part of the knowledge test, and you get the air brake restriction.

Source: 49 CFR 383.91; 49 CFR 383.111(c); 49 CFR 383.113; 49 CFR 383.93; 49 CFR 383.95
