CDL HazMat Endorsement Practice Test

Your state's licensing agency gives the H endorsement knowledge test, and TSA runs the security threat assessment. DOT hazmat employee training is given and recorded by your employer. The federal rules below are the same in every state; check your state's CDL manual for anything it adds.

These are the first 10 of the 90 questions in Endorsed's HazMat Endorsement and DOT Hazmat Training pack, with the first of its 14 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. Which commercial vehicles need a driver with the H endorsement?

    1. Any vehicle placarded for hazmat
    2. Any tank vehicle, loaded or empty
    3. Any vehicle of 26,001 pounds or more
    Show the answer

    A. Any vehicle placarded for hazmat

    The H endorsement is required for a vehicle that must be placarded, or that carries a select agent or toxin.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.93

  2. A 14,000 pound truck must be placarded for its load. Which CDL class covers it?

    1. None, it is under 26,001 pounds
    2. Class C
    3. Class B
    Show the answer

    B. Class C

    A vehicle that is neither A nor B is Class C when it carries hazardous materials that must be placarded.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.91

  3. Which cargo needs the H endorsement in any quantity, placarded or not?

    1. A select agent or toxin
    2. Combustible liquid in small cans
    3. Class 9 lithium batteries
    Show the answer

    A. A select agent or toxin

    For the CDL rules, hazardous materials include any quantity of a listed select agent or toxin.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.5

  4. What does the H endorsement knowledge test cover?

    1. Placard colors and sizes only
    2. The rules, handling, equipment and response
    3. Only TSA background check questions
    Show the answer

    B. The rules, handling, equipment and response

    The test checks knowledge of the hazardous materials rules and how to handle and respond to hazardous materials.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.121

  5. What must happen before a state may issue or renew an H endorsement?

    1. TSA determines the driver is not a security threat
    2. The driver passes a second road test
    3. The carrier signs a letter for the driver
    Show the answer

    A. TSA determines the driver is not a security threat

    The state may not issue, renew, upgrade or transfer the H without TSA's determination, or a valid TWIC, which rests on the same TSA assessment.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.141

  6. Which is part of TSA's security threat assessment?

    1. A road skills test
    2. A check for disqualifying criminal offences
    3. A vision test
    Show the answer

    B. A check for disqualifying criminal offences

    TSA looks at criminal offences, immigration status, mental capacity and intelligence records.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.5

  7. What is a Class 3 material?

    1. A flammable gas
    2. A flammable liquid
    3. A corrosive
    Show the answer

    B. A flammable liquid

    Class 3 is flammable liquids.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.120

  8. A flammable liquid has a flash point of not more than:

    1. 38°C (100°F)
    2. 60°C (140°F)
    3. 93°C (200°F)
    Show the answer

    B. 60°C (140°F)

    Not more than 60°C (140°F) is a flammable liquid.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.120

  9. A liquid has a flash point of 75°C. What is it?

    1. A flammable liquid
    2. A combustible liquid
    3. Not regulated at all
    Show the answer

    B. A combustible liquid

    Above 60°C and below 93°C is a combustible liquid.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.120

  10. What is Division 2.3?

    1. A gas poisonous by inhalation
    2. A non-flammable compressed gas
    3. A flammable gas
    Show the answer

    A. A gas poisonous by inhalation

    2.1 is flammable, 2.2 non-flammable and non-poisonous, 2.3 poisonous by inhalation.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.115

Study: Who needs the H endorsement

You need the hazardous materials (H) endorsement to drive a commercial motor vehicle that must be placarded for hazardous materials under 49 CFR 172 Subpart F, or that carries any quantity of a select agent or toxin listed in 42 CFR part 73.

A vehicle too light for Class A or B still needs a Class C CDL when it carries hazardous materials that must be placarded, or any quantity of a select agent or toxin.

The H knowledge test checks that you know the hazardous materials rules well enough to follow them: recognizing hazardous materials, shipping papers, marking, labeling and placarding, handling and loading, emergency equipment, and what to do in an emergency.

The endorsement is in addition to your CDL class. Hazmat rules for drivers also sit in the motor carrier rules: railroad crossings (49 CFR 392.10), the fire extinguisher (49 CFR 393.95) and driving and parking (49 CFR 397).

The H endorsement takes a knowledge test only; there is no H skills test. It cannot be placed on a commercial learner's permit, and a CLP holder may not drive a vehicle carrying hazardous materials as 49 CFR 383.5 defines them, even with a CDL holder in the next seat.

Source: 49 CFR 383.93; 49 CFR 383.5; 49 CFR 383.91; 49 CFR 383.121
