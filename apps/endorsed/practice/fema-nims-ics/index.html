FEMA IS-100, 200, 700 and 800 Practice Questions

FEMA offers these four courses online at no cost through the Independent Study Program of its National Disaster and Emergency Management University (NDEMU), as part of the Emergency Management Institute's curriculum (current versions IS-100.c, IS-200.c, IS-700.b and IS-800.d). Each course page has its own online final exam. You need a FEMA Student Identification number (SID) to take an exam, the passing mark is 75 percent, and a pass earns a certificate of completion. As of October 2026 the IS-800.d online lessons are marked temporarily unavailable; the course page says to study the National Response Framework itself and then take the online exam. FEMA sets the minimum NIMS training for recipients of its preparedness grants; each agency, employer or volunteer organization decides which courses its own people need, so ask yours. ICS-300 and ICS-400 are not Independent Study courses: they are taught on campus, in the field or by states, FEMA's FAQ points you to your State Training Office for them, and they are not covered here.

10 practice questions

  1. Which list names the five major ICS functional areas?

    1. Command, Safety, Planning, Logistics, Finance/Admin
    2. Command, Operations, Planning, Logistics, Finance/Admin
    3. Command, Operations, Dispatch, Logistics, Public Information
    Show the answer

    B. Command, Operations, Planning, Logistics, Finance/Admin

    Command, Operations, Planning, Logistics and Finance/Administration, staffed as needed.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 24

  2. Which position is NOT part of the Command Staff?

    1. Liaison Officer
    2. Safety Officer
    3. Planning Section Chief
    4. Public Information Officer
    Show the answer

    C. Planning Section Chief

    The Planning Section Chief is General Staff; the Command Staff are the PIO, Safety Officer and Liaison Officer.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 27

  3. Who may alter, suspend or stop an activity that is immediately dangerous to the life and health of personnel?

    1. The Safety Officer
    2. The Liaison Officer
    3. Only the Operations Section Chief
    Show the answer

    A. The Safety Officer

    The Safety Officer alters, suspends or terminates activities immediately dangerous to life and health.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 83

  4. Which two courses does FEMA's NIMS Training Program list for all personnel working in ICS?

    1. IS-200 and IS-800
    2. IS-100 and IS-700
    3. ICS-300 and ICS-400
    Show the answer

    B. IS-100 and IS-700

    IS-100 and IS-700 for all ICS personnel; IS-200 and IS-800 are added for leadership responsibilities.

    Source: FEMA NIMS Training Program 2020 p. 13

  5. What is the title of the person who leads a Branch?

    1. Director
    2. Supervisor
    3. Chief
    4. Leader
    Show the answer

    A. Director

    Branches are led by Directors; divisions and groups by Supervisors; sections by Chiefs.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 82

  6. How do divisions and groups differ?

    1. Divisions are functional; groups are geographic
    2. Both are geographic, but groups are smaller
    3. Divisions are geographic; groups are functional
    Show the answer

    C. Divisions are geographic; groups are functional

    Divisions always refer to geographic assignments and groups always refer to functional ones.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 88

  7. Five dump trucks of the same kind and type working under one leader form a:

    1. Task force
    2. Strike team
    3. Division
    4. Unit
    Show the answer

    B. Strike team

    A strike team is a set number of resources of the same kind and type under a designated leader.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 89

  8. No Logistics Section Chief has been assigned yet. Who is responsible for logistics?

    1. The Incident Commander
    2. The Operations Section Chief
    3. Nobody until a chief arrives
    Show the answer

    A. The Incident Commander

    General Staff functions default to the Incident Commander or Unified Command until a section chief is assigned.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 28

  9. Before an incoming team takes over a county's flood response, the county administrator gives its Incident Commander a written statement of the commander's responsibilities, priorities and limits. What is that document?

    1. A mutual aid agreement
    2. A delegation of authority
    3. The Incident Action Plan
    Show the answer

    B. A delegation of authority

    A delegation of authority is the statement from the jurisdiction that assigns the Incident Commander specific responsibilities and authorities, typically with priorities, expectations and constraints.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 32

  10. Under NIMS, what does the transfer of command process include?

    1. A briefing, and notice to all personnel
    2. Approval from the state EOC director
    3. A new written IAP signed by both commanders
    Show the answer

    A. A briefing, and notice to all personnel

    The transfer includes a briefing that captures essential information and notifying all personnel involved in the incident.

    Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 22

Study: IS-100: What ICS is and who commands

This pack is study preparation, not the exam. FEMA's Independent Study Program gives the real final exam for each course; this pack does not copy those exams.

The Incident Command System (ICS) is a standardized approach to the command, control and coordination of on-scene incident management. It is used by all levels of government and by many NGOs and private sector organizations. NIMS applies to all incidents, from traffic accidents to major disasters, and using ICS on every incident keeps the skills sharp.

ICS has five major functional areas, staffed as needed: Command, Operations, Planning, Logistics and Finance/Administration. A sixth function, Intelligence/Investigations, is used only when an incident needs it, and the Incident Commander decides where it sits: in the Planning Section, in the Operations Section, on the Command Staff, as its own General Staff section, or a mix of these.

The Incident Commander is responsible for on-scene incident activities, including developing incident objectives and ordering and releasing resources. The Command Staff report directly to the Incident Commander: the Public Information Officer (PIO), who handles information for the public, media and other agencies; the Safety Officer, who monitors operations, advises command on the health and safety of incident personnel, and stops or prevents unsafe acts; and the Liaison Officer, the point of contact for agencies and organizations not in the command.

The Incident Commander or Unified Command approves the release of incident information. When several agencies have PIOs, one is designated the lead PIO.

FEMA's NIMS Training Program lists IS-100 and IS-700 for all personnel working in ICS, and adds IS-200 and IS-800 for those with leadership responsibilities.

Source: FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 1; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 4; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 24; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 27; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 31; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 83; FEMA NIMS 2017 p. 99; FEMA ICS Review Document p. 10; FEMA NIMS Training Program 2020 p. 13
