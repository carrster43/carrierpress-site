USCG Captain (OUPV) Practice Questions

The U.S. Coast Guard's National Maritime Center sets the credential requirements, and the exam is given by the Coast Guard at a Regional Exam Center or through a course the Coast Guard accepts. This pack covers the rules: what the endorsement allows, who qualifies, required equipment, the Navigation Rules and the operator's duties. It does not teach seamanship or chart work, and it is not the full exam.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's USCG Captain (OUPV) pack, with the first of its 9 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. How many passengers may an OUPV operator carry?

    1. Six or fewer
    2. Twelve or fewer
    3. Any number under 100 GRT
    Show the answer

    A. Six or fewer

    OUPV covers vessels under 100 GRT carrying six or fewer passengers.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.467

  2. What tonnage limit applies to an OUPV endorsement?

    1. Less than 200 GRT
    2. Less than 50 GRT
    3. Less than 100 GRT
    Show the answer

    C. Less than 100 GRT

    The OUPV endorsement is for vessels of less than 100 GRT.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.467

  3. Friends split the actual fuel and food costs of a day trip equally. Is that consideration?

    1. Yes, any money counts
    2. No, voluntary expense sharing is excluded
    3. Only if fuel is over $100
    Show the answer

    B. No, voluntary expense sharing is excluded

    Consideration excludes a voluntary sharing of the actual expenses of the voyage.

    Source: 46 CFR 24.10-1

  4. Who of these counts as a passenger?

    1. The master
    2. The owner
    3. A paying guest
    Show the answer

    C. A paying guest

    The owner and the master are excluded from the definition of passenger; a paying guest is a passenger for hire.

    Source: 46 CFR 24.10-1

  5. For an OUPV near coastal endorsement, how far offshore does near coastal reach?

    1. 100 miles
    2. 200 miles
    3. 20 miles
    Show the answer

    A. 100 miles

    For OUPV, near coastal is limited to waters not more than 100 miles offshore.

    Source: 46 CFR 10.107

  6. How much experience does a near coastal OUPV need?

    1. 6 months, all inland
    2. 12 months, 3 of them on near coastal or ocean waters
    3. 24 months, 6 of them offshore
    Show the answer

    B. 12 months, 3 of them on near coastal or ocean waters

    At least 12 months operating vessels, including at least 3 months on ocean or near coastal waters.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.467

  7. Does an inland OUPV endorsement include the Great Lakes?

    1. Yes
    2. No
    Show the answer

    B. No

    Inland endorsements cover all inland waters except the Great Lakes.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.467

  8. How recent must at least 3 months of your required service be?

    1. Within the last 3 years
    2. Within the last 10 years
    3. Within the last year
    Show the answer

    A. Within the last 3 years

    At least 3 months of service within the 3 years before applying.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.201

  9. Under the general age rule, how old must you be for an OUPV endorsement?

    1. 16
    2. 18
    3. 21
    Show the answer

    B. 18

    OUPV is among the endorsements that may be granted at 18; the Coast Guard may also lower the age for OUPV applicants, with matching restrictions.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.201

  10. When may a non-citizen hold an OUPV endorsement?

    1. Never
    2. On vessels over 100 GRT
    3. On an undocumented six-pack boat
    Show the answer

    C. On an undocumented six-pack boat

    The citizenship rule excepts operators of uninspected passenger vessels that are not documented under U.S. law.

    Source: 46 CFR 11.201

Study: What OUPV allows

An OUPV endorsement lets you operate an uninspected passenger vessel of less than 100 gross register tons (GRT), with propulsion machinery of any type, carrying six or fewer passengers.

Under 100 gross tons, an uninspected passenger vessel is one carrying not more than six passengers, including at least one passenger for hire, or one chartered with the crew provided or specified by the owner and carrying not more than six passengers.

A passenger for hire is a passenger for whom consideration is contributed as a condition of carriage, whether it flows directly or indirectly to the owner, charterer, operator, agent or anyone else with an interest in the vessel.

Consideration means an economic benefit, inducement, right or profit, including payment. It does not include a voluntary sharing of the actual expenses of the voyage by money or by donating fuel, food, drink or other supplies.

The owner or the owner's representative, an individual charterer, the master, and a paid crewmember engaged in the business of the vessel who has not contributed consideration are not counted as passengers.

Source: 46 CFR 11.467; 46 CFR 24.10-1
