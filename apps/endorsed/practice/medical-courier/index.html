Medical Courier Practice Questions

There is no single federal license or exam for medical couriers. Your training comes through your employer under the federal rules in this pack: knowing the Category B rules for the most common loads, full DOT hazmat training and testing where you handle fully regulated hazmat, and OSHA bloodborne pathogens training where your job brings occupational exposure. Some states and some clients add requirements of their own; ask them which apply to you.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's Medical Specimen and Supply Courier pack, with the first of its 10 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. A specimen is known or reasonably expected to contain a pathogen. Under the HMR it is:

    1. Division 6.1, poisonous material
    2. Division 6.2, infectious substance
    3. Class 9, miscellaneous hazardous material
    Show the answer

    B. Division 6.2, infectious substance

    Division 6.2 is a material known or reasonably expected to contain a pathogen.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(a)(1)

  2. Which identification number is used for Category B?

    1. UN2814
    2. UN3291
    3. UN1845
    4. UN3373
    Show the answer

    D. UN3373

    Category B is Biological substance, Category B, UN3373.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(a)(1)(ii)

  3. What puts an infectious substance in Category A rather than Category B?

    1. It is carried as a liquid rather than a solid
    2. It travels frozen, packed in dry ice in a cooler
    3. Its form can cause fatal or disabling disease
    Show the answer

    C. Its form can cause fatal or disabling disease

    Category A is in a form capable of causing permanent disability or life-threatening or fatal disease in otherwise healthy people or animals when exposure occurs.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(a)(1)(i)

  4. Blood culture bottles collected from a patient are, under the HMR definitions:

    1. A patient specimen
    2. A culture, so Category A
    Show the answer

    A. A patient specimen

    Patient specimens include specimens in transport media such as blood culture bottles, and a culture does not include a patient specimen.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(a)(3)

  5. Which of these is NOT subject to the HMR as Division 6.2?

    1. A swab from a patient with a suspected infection
    2. Dried blood spots on filter paper
    3. A blood tube sent to test for an infection
    Show the answer

    B. Dried blood spots on filter paper

    Dried blood spots and fecal occult blood specimens on absorbent material are excepted.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(b)(9)

  6. A urine sample for a routine workplace drug test, with a low probability of being infectious, is:

    1. Category B, UN3373
    2. Category A, UN2814, because it is human urine
    3. Not subject to the Division 6.2 rules
    Show the answer

    C. Not subject to the Division 6.2 rules

    Samples for routine testing not related to diagnosing an infectious disease, such as drug or alcohol tests, are excepted when the probability of infection is low.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.134(b)(11)

  7. A lab courier will hand-deliver sealed DOT urine specimens from the collection site to the lab. Is a shipping container required?

    1. Yes, the collector must always use one
    2. No, not for a lab courier's hand delivery
    Show the answer

    B. No, not for a lab courier's hand delivery

    The shipping container is not required when a laboratory courier hand-delivers the specimens from the collection site to the laboratory.

    Source: 49 CFR 40.45(b)

  8. Category B triple packaging is made up of:

    1. Two sealed bags inside a padded mailer
    2. A capped tube inside an insulated cooler bag
    3. A primary, a secondary and a rigid outer
    Show the answer

    C. A primary, a secondary and a rigid outer

    Category B goes in a primary receptacle, a secondary packaging and a rigid outer packaging.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.199(a)(1)

  9. For a liquid Category B specimen, how much absorbent goes between the primary and the secondary?

    1. Enough for the entire contents
    2. Enough for half the contents
    3. None if the tube is capped tight
    Show the answer

    A. Enough for the entire contents

    The absorbent must be enough to absorb the entire contents of the primary receptacles.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.199(b)(2)

  10. What goes on the outer packaging of a Category B package?

    1. An INFECTIOUS SUBSTANCE label and a placard
    2. The UN3373 diamond and the shipping name
    3. A biohazard sticker and nothing else at all
    Show the answer

    B. The UN3373 diamond and the shipping name

    The UN3373 square-on-point mark is displayed with "Biological substances, Category B" next to it.

    Source: 49 CFR 173.199(a)(5)

Study: Infectious substances: Category A and Category B

This pack covers a courier driving lab specimens, blood, medical supplies, medicines and medical waste by road. Two sets of federal rules meet in that job: the DOT Hazardous Materials Regulations (HMR) for what is in the package, and the OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard for the person handling it. The hazmat pack covers general hazmat rules.

Division 6.2 (infectious substance) is a material known or reasonably expected to contain a pathogen: a microorganism such as a bacterium, virus, parasite or fungus, or another agent such as a prion, that can cause disease in humans or animals.

Category A is an infectious substance in a form capable of causing permanent disability or life-threatening or fatal disease in otherwise healthy humans or animals when exposure occurs. It is UN2814 (affecting humans), UN2900 (affecting animals only) or UN3549 (medical waste containing Category A, solid). Which one is assigned rests on the known medical history or symptoms of the source patient or animal, local endemic conditions, or professional judgment.

Category B is an infectious substance not in a form generally capable of causing that harm. The rule says it includes Category B substances transported for diagnostic or investigational purposes. It is described as "Biological substance, Category B" (singular, as in the Hazardous Materials Table) and is UN3373. The package marking in 173.199 uses the plural, "Biological substances, Category B".

Regulated medical waste is UN3291, not UN3373. Patient specimens are those collected directly from humans or animals for research, diagnosis, investigation, or disease treatment or prevention, including blood, tissue, swabs, excreta and specimens in transport media such as blood culture bottles. A culture is an infectious substance containing a pathogen that is intentionally propagated, and the rule says a culture does not include a patient specimen.

Source: 49 CFR 173.134(a)(1); 49 CFR 173.134(a)(3); 49 CFR 173.134(a)(4); 49 CFR 172.101
