TWIC Card Practice Questions

There is no TWIC exam. TSA decides each application through its security threat assessment; this pack explains the rules it applies so you know where you stand before you enroll.

These are the first 10 of the 29 questions in Endorsed's TWIC Card pack, with the first of its 5 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. Who must hold a TWIC?

    1. Every CDL holder, at renewal
    2. Anyone needing unescorted access to secure maritime areas
    3. Anyone who drives across a state line
    Show the answer

    B. Anyone needing unescorted access to secure maritime areas

    The TWIC is the credential for unescorted access to secure areas under 33 CFR parts 104 to 106.

    Source: 33 CFR 101.514

  2. A city police officer needs to enter a port's secure area. Is a TWIC required?

    1. Yes
    2. No
    3. Only at night
    Show the answer

    B. No

    State and local law enforcement are not required to obtain a TWIC, though they may.

    Source: 33 CFR 101.514

  3. Does a TWIC give you the hazardous materials endorsement on your CDL?

    1. Yes, automatically
    2. No, it is a separate credential
    3. Only in coastal states
    Show the answer

    B. No, it is a separate credential

    Your state adds the HME after its knowledge test; a valid TWIC can satisfy the TSA threat assessment the state needs for it.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.141

  4. Which is part of the TWIC security threat assessment?

    1. A driving test
    2. A fingerprint-based criminal history check
    3. A credit check
    Show the answer

    B. A fingerprint-based criminal history check

    It includes a fingerprint-based criminal history records check, an intelligence-related check and a final disposition.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.21

  5. TSA serves you an Initial Determination of Threat Assessment. How long do you have to appeal or ask for more time before it becomes final?

    1. 10 days
    2. 30 days
    3. 60 days
    Show the answer

    C. 60 days

    If you do not appeal or request an extension within 60 days, it becomes final.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.21

  6. Which offense disqualifies permanently?

    1. Robbery
    2. Murder
    3. Bribery
    Show the answer

    B. Murder

    Murder is a permanent disqualifying offense; robbery and bribery are interim offenses.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.103

  7. An interim offense disqualifies if you were convicted within how many years of applying?

    1. 5 years
    2. 7 years
    3. 10 years
    Show the answer

    B. 7 years

    Within 7 years of the application, or released from incarceration within 5 years.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.103

  8. You were released from prison for arson 3 years ago, after a conviction 9 years ago. Is it disqualifying?

    1. No, the conviction is over 7 years old
    2. Yes, release from incarceration within 5 years counts
    3. Only with a second offense
    Show the answer

    B. Yes, release from incarceration within 5 years counts

    An interim offense also disqualifies when release from incarceration was within 5 years of applying.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.103

  9. Which does not count as a dishonesty or fraud offense for TWIC?

    1. Identity fraud
    2. Passing bad checks
    3. Money laundering tied to a listed crime
    Show the answer

    B. Passing bad checks

    Welfare fraud and passing bad checks are excluded.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.103

  10. Improper transportation of hazardous materials under federal law is:

    1. Not disqualifying
    2. An interim disqualifying offense
    3. A permanent disqualifying offense
    Show the answer

    C. A permanent disqualifying offense

    It is listed among the permanent disqualifying offenses.

    Source: 49 CFR 1572.103

Study: Who needs a TWIC

The Transportation Worker Identification Credential is required for anyone who needs unescorted access to the secure areas of vessels, port facilities and outer continental shelf facilities regulated under the maritime security rules (33 CFR parts 104, 105 and 106). Truck drivers who pick up and deliver inside a port terminal's secure area are among those who need one.

Federal officials use their own agency credential instead. State and local law enforcement do not need one, and state and local emergency responders do not need one during an emergency; either may get one voluntarily.

A TWIC is not a hazardous materials endorsement: the HME is added by your state after its knowledge test. The two rest on the same TSA threat assessment standard, and each can stand in for the other's assessment. A state may issue, renew, upgrade or transfer an HME to a driver who holds a valid TWIC. Going the other way, TSA treats a current HME threat assessment as comparable for a TWIC: an HME holder still enrolls for the card, and that TWIC expires five years from the date tied to the HME assessment.

If you do not hold a TWIC and are not exempt, you may still enter a secure area under escort. Only a TWIC holder who is authorized to be in that secure area under the facility security plan may serve as the escort, and the escort must accompany you continuously, side by side or by monitoring, closely enough to see whether you are doing anything other than what you were let in for.

You must also show personal identification that resists tampering and shows your full name, a photo that looks like you now, and the name of the issuer. The issuer may be a government authority, or your employer, union or trade association.

Source: 33 CFR 101.514; 49 CFR 1572.5; 49 CFR 1572.23; 49 CFR 383.141
