FAA Part 107 Practice Test

The FAA gives the Remote Pilot knowledge test at a testing center the FAA authorizes. This pack covers the operating rules in 14 CFR 107; the test also asks about charts, weather and aircraft performance, which the FAA's own study guide covers.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot pack, with the first of its 8 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. What is the most a small unmanned aircraft may weigh at takeoff under Part 107?

    1. Less than 55 pounds, everything on board included
    2. Less than 25 pounds
    3. Less than 55 pounds, payload not counted
    Show the answer

    A. Less than 55 pounds, everything on board included

    Under 55 pounds at takeoff, including everything on board or otherwise attached.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.3

  2. How old must you be to hold a remote pilot certificate?

    1. 14
    2. 16
    3. 18
    Show the answer

    B. 16

    At least 16 years old.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.61

  3. How often must a remote pilot complete recurrent training to keep flying under Part 107?

    1. Every 12 calendar months
    2. Every 24 calendar months
    3. Every 36 calendar months
    Show the answer

    B. Every 24 calendar months

    Within the previous 24 calendar months.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.65

  4. What is the highest you may normally fly?

    1. 400 feet above ground level
    2. 500 feet above ground level
    3. 400 feet above mean sea level
    Show the answer

    A. 400 feet above ground level

    400 feet above ground level, unless within 400 feet of a structure.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  5. You are inspecting a 300-foot tower. How high may you fly while within 400 feet of it?

    1. 400 feet above ground level
    2. 700 feet above ground level
    3. No higher than the top of the tower
    Show the answer

    B. 700 feet above ground level

    Up to 400 feet above the structure's top: 300 plus 400 is 700 feet above ground level.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  6. What is the maximum groundspeed?

    1. 87 knots (100 mph)
    2. 100 knots (115 mph)
    3. 60 knots (69 mph)
    Show the answer

    A. 87 knots (100 mph)

    87 knots, which is 100 miles per hour.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  7. What is the least flight visibility allowed from the control station?

    1. 1 statute mile
    2. 3 statute miles
    3. 5 statute miles
    Show the answer

    B. 3 statute miles

    At least 3 statute miles.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  8. How far below a cloud must the aircraft stay?

    1. 250 feet
    2. 500 feet
    3. 1,000 feet
    Show the answer

    B. 500 feet

    At least 500 feet below, and 2,000 feet horizontally from, clouds.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  9. How far horizontally from a cloud must the aircraft stay?

    1. 500 feet
    2. 1,000 feet
    3. 2,000 feet
    Show the answer

    C. 2,000 feet

    2,000 feet horizontally.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.51

  10. Which of these keeps a flight within visual line of sight?

    1. Watching the live camera feed on the controller
    2. A visual observer watching it with unaided eyes
    3. Following it closely through binoculars
    Show the answer

    B. A visual observer watching it with unaided eyes

    Line of sight means unaided eyes (corrective lenses allowed) of the pilot in command, the person at the controls, or a visual observer.

    Source: 14 CFR 107.31

Study: Who needs a remote pilot certificate

Part 107 covers small unmanned aircraft, which means under 55 pounds at takeoff including everything on board or attached.

Anyone flying under Part 107, which includes any flight that is not purely recreational, must hold a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating, or fly under the direct supervision of someone who does and who can take over the controls at once.

To get the certificate you must be at least 16, able to read, speak, write and understand English, in a physical and mental condition to fly safely, and pass the initial knowledge test (a Part 61 pilot certificate holder, other than a student pilot, who is current on the flight review may complete the FAA's online training instead).

To keep flying, you must have passed the initial test or completed recurrent training within the previous 24 calendar months.

Flying only for fun falls under a separate law, 49 U.S.C. 44809, with its own conditions. This pack is about Part 107.

If you fail the knowledge test, you must wait 14 calendar days before you may take it again.

Source: 14 CFR 107.3; 14 CFR 107.12; 14 CFR 107.61; 14 CFR 107.65; 14 CFR 107.71
