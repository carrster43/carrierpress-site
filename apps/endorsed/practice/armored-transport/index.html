Armored Car Guard Practice Questions

There is no federal armored car license or exam. Armed guard rules are set by the states, and they differ widely. This pack covers the federal rules that reach armored trucks and their crews, and the guard rules of five states read from their statutes; ask your own state's agency for its rules and test.

These are the first 10 of the 46 questions in Endorsed's Armored Car and Cash in Transit pack, with the first of its 13 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. An armored truck has a GVWR of 14,000 pounds and runs a route across a state line. Is it a commercial motor vehicle under the federal safety rules?

    1. No, since it carries no passengers
    2. No, not below 26,001 pounds
    3. Yes, at 10,001 pounds or more
    Show the answer

    C. Yes, at 10,001 pounds or more

    A property vehicle in interstate commerce rated or weighing 10,001 pounds or more is a commercial motor vehicle.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  2. A truck is rated at 9,800 pounds but weighs 10,400 pounds loaded on an interstate run. Which figure decides whether it is a commercial motor vehicle?

    1. Only the rating
    2. Whichever is greater
    3. Only the empty weight
    Show the answer

    B. Whichever is greater

    The definition uses the rating or the actual weight, whichever is greater.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  3. A route runs between two branches in one state but passes through a neighboring state on the way. What kind of commerce is it?

    1. Interstate
    2. Intrastate
    3. Neither, since both ends are in one state
    Show the answer

    A. Interstate

    Transportation between two places in a state through another state is interstate commerce.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  4. An armored car company carries other businesses' cash for a fee. What kind of motor carrier is it?

    1. A for-hire motor carrier
    2. A private motor carrier
    3. Not a motor carrier, since it is a security firm
    Show the answer

    A. A for-hire motor carrier

    A for-hire motor carrier is engaged in transporting goods or passengers for compensation, and cash carried for a fee is goods carried for pay.

    Source: 49 CFR 390.5T

  5. A straight armored truck has a GVWR of 22,000 pounds, weighs under 26,001 pounds loaded, tows nothing, carries no placarded hazmat and is not built for 16 passengers. Does the driver need a CDL under the federal rule?

    1. Yes, Group B
    2. Yes, Group C
    3. No
    Show the answer

    C. No

    It meets none of the Group A, B or C definitions, so no CDL is needed federally.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.5

  6. At what GVWR does a single vehicle become Group B?

    1. 26,001 pounds or more
    2. 10,001 pounds or more
    3. 33,001 pounds or more
    Show the answer

    A. 26,001 pounds or more

    Group B is a single vehicle with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.91

  7. Do the federal CDL rules in part 383 reach a driver whose routes never leave the state?

    1. No, part 383 reaches interstate drivers only
    2. Only on federal highways
    3. Yes, they cover intrastate commerce too
    Show the answer

    C. Yes, they cover intrastate commerce too

    Part 383 applies to commercial motor vehicle drivers in interstate, foreign or intrastate commerce.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.3

  8. What is a vehicle's gross vehicle weight rating?

    1. The scale weight on the day of the trip
    2. The carrier's estimate of the heaviest load
    3. The manufacturer's specified loaded weight
    Show the answer

    C. The manufacturer's specified loaded weight

    GVWR is the value the manufacturer specifies as the loaded weight of a single vehicle.

    Source: 49 CFR 383.5

  9. What is the minimum age to drive a commercial motor vehicle under the federal driver qualification rule?

    1. 18
    2. 19
    3. 20
    4. 21
    Show the answer

    D. 21

    A qualified driver is at least 21 years old.

    Source: 49 CFR 391.11

  10. You drive a 15,000-pound armored truck interstate and hold no CDL. Where must your medical examiner's certificate be while you are on duty?

    1. On your person, the original or a copy
    2. With the carrier's driver file at the terminal
    3. Filed with the state only
    Show the answer

    A. On your person, the original or a copy

    A driver without a CDL carries the original or a copy of a current certificate when on duty.

    Source: 49 CFR 391.41

Study: When an armored truck is a commercial motor vehicle

No federal agency licenses armored car drivers, messengers or guards as such. What the federal rules do reach is the truck: if it is a commercial motor vehicle, the driver and the carrier follow the federal motor carrier safety rules. Guard licensing and the right to carry a weapon come from the state, covered at the end of this pack.

Under the federal safety rules, a vehicle used on a highway in interstate commerce to transport property is a commercial motor vehicle if its gross vehicle weight rating or gross combination weight rating, or its gross vehicle weight or gross combination weight, is 10,001 pounds (4,536 kg) or more, whichever is greater. The other tests are passenger seating and placarded hazardous materials.

So check both your truck's GVWR and its loaded weight. This pack does not assume a typical figure: the numbers for your own vehicle decide.

Interstate commerce means trade, traffic or transportation between a place in a state and a place outside it, between two places in a state through another state or a place outside the United States, or between two places in a state as part of a movement that begins or ends outside the state or the country. Anything else is intrastate commerce. Your state sets the rules for it, and they may differ from the federal ones, so ask your state.

A motor carrier is a for-hire motor carrier or a private motor carrier. A for-hire motor carrier is a person engaged in the transportation of goods or passengers for compensation; a private motor carrier transports property or passengers by commercial motor vehicle and is not for-hire. A company paid to carry other businesses' cash is transporting goods for compensation, so it fits the for-hire definition.

Source: 49 CFR 390.5T
