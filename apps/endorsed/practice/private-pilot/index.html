Private Pilot Knowledge Test Practice Questions

The FAA gives the Private Pilot knowledge test at a testing center the FAA authorizes. This pack covers the rules in 14 CFR 61 and 91, airspace and VFR weather minimums; the test also asks about aerodynamics, performance, charts, weather theory and navigation, which the FAA's own handbooks (the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, the Airplane Flying Handbook and the AIM) cover.

These are the first 10 of the 40 questions in Endorsed's Private Pilot Knowledge Test pack, with the first of its 10 study sections. Every answer names the rule it comes from. The full pack, and 18 other credentials, are in Endorsed.

10 practice questions

  1. How old must you be to hold a private pilot certificate with an airplane rating?

    1. 16
    2. 17
    3. 18
    Show the answer

    B. 17

    At least 17 for a rating other than glider or balloon.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.103

  2. Outside a Part 142 training center course, what is the least total flight time for a private pilot certificate, single-engine airplane?

    1. 40 hours
    2. 30 hours
    3. 50 hours
    Show the answer

    A. 40 hours

    At least 40 hours, including 20 hours of training from an instructor and 10 hours of solo training; a Part 142 training center course can lower it to 35.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.109

  3. Which describes the long solo cross-country required for the certificate?

    1. 100 nm total, landings at three points, one leg over 25 nm
    2. 150 nm total, landings at two points, one leg over 50 nm
    3. 150 nm total, landings at three points, one leg over 50 nm
    Show the answer

    C. 150 nm total, landings at three points, one leg over 50 nm

    150 nautical miles total, full-stop landings at three points, and one straight-line leg of more than 50 nautical miles.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.109

  4. How long is a passed knowledge test good for when you take the practical test?

    1. 12 calendar months
    2. 24 calendar months
    3. 36 calendar months
    Show the answer

    B. 24 calendar months

    It must be passed within the 24 calendar months before the month you complete the practical test; 61.40 gives narrow relief only to people serving abroad in support of the Armed Forces.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.39

  5. You were 35 when you took your third-class medical exam. How long does it last for private pilot privileges?

    1. 24 calendar months
    2. 36 calendar months
    3. 60 calendar months
    Show the answer

    C. 60 calendar months

    Under 40 on the exam date, it runs to the end of the 60th month after the month of the exam.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.23

  6. You were 52 when you took your third-class medical exam. How long does it last for private pilot privileges?

    1. 24 calendar months
    2. 12 calendar months
    3. 60 calendar months
    Show the answer

    A. 24 calendar months

    At 40 or older on the exam date, it runs to the end of the 24th month after the month of the exam.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.23

  7. Under BasicMed, how often do you need the comprehensive exam by a State-licensed physician?

    1. Every 24 calendar months
    2. Every 48 calendar months
    3. Every 60 calendar months
    Show the answer

    B. Every 48 calendar months

    The comprehensive exam is within the previous 48 calendar months; the education course is within 24.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.23(c)(3)

  8. Which flight is outside BasicMed limits?

    1. Cruising at 19,000 feet MSL
    2. Carrying 3 passengers
    3. Flying at 140 knots indicated
    Show the answer

    A. Cruising at 19,000 feet MSL

    BasicMed flights may not go above 18,000 feet MSL, faster than 250 knots, or carry more than 6 passengers.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.113(i)

  9. You fly two friends to the coast. How may the costs be shared?

    1. They may pay all of the fuel and rental
    2. You pay at least your pro rata share of the fuel and rental
    3. They pay your share if you add a fee for your time
    Show the answer

    B. You pay at least your pro rata share of the fuel and rental

    A private pilot may not pay less than the pro rata share, and only fuel, oil, airport expenditures or rental fees count.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.113

  10. How often must you complete a flight review to act as pilot in command?

    1. Every 12 calendar months
    2. Every 36 calendar months
    3. Every 24 calendar months
    Show the answer

    C. Every 24 calendar months

    Since the beginning of the 24th calendar month before the month of the flight; a practical test or proficiency check passed in that period also counts.

    Source: 14 CFR 61.56

Study: Getting the certificate

A student pilot certificate needs you to be at least 16 (14 for a glider or balloon) and able to read, speak, write and understand English.

For a private pilot certificate with an airplane rating you must be at least 17, meet the same English requirement, hold a student, sport or recreational pilot certificate, have an instructor's endorsement before the knowledge test, pass the knowledge test, have an instructor's endorsement before the practical test, meet the aeronautical experience, and pass the practical test.

The knowledge test must have been passed within the 24 calendar months before the month you complete the practical test. The only relief from that for a private pilot applicant is narrow: 61.40 lets a person who served outside the United States in support of U.S. Armed Forces operations use an expired test report, if the practical test is completed within 6 calendar months after returning.

Aeronautical experience for a single-engine airplane: at least 40 hours of flight time, including at least 20 hours of flight training from an authorized instructor and 10 hours of solo flight training. Up to 2.5 hours of instructor training in a flight simulator or flight training device may count, and a private pilot course at a Part 142 training center can lower the total to 35 hours.

The training must include 3 hours of cross-country training; 3 hours of night training with one cross-country flight of over 100 nautical miles total distance and 10 takeoffs and 10 landings to a full stop (a person trained in and living in Alaska may instead get a certificate marked "Night flying prohibited" and must finish the night training within 12 calendar months); 3 hours of flight by reference to instruments; and 3 hours of training in preparation for the practical test within the 2 calendar months before the month of the test.

The 10 hours of solo must include 5 hours of solo cross-country time; one solo cross-country of 150 nautical miles total distance, with full-stop landings at three points and one leg of more than 50 nautical miles straight-line distance; and three takeoffs and three landings to a full stop at an airport with an operating control tower.

Source: 14 CFR 61.83; 14 CFR 61.103; 14 CFR 61.39; 14 CFR 61.40; 14 CFR 61.109; 14 CFR 61.110
