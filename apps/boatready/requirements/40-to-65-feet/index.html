Boat Ready: free reference

Required safety equipment for a boat 40 to 65 feet

The US federal carriage requirements for a recreational boat from 40 feet up to, but not including, 65 feet, from Boat Ready's rule list, which is checked against the Code of Federal Regulations (rules as of 2026-10-01). Each item names its regulation.

Every boat is different. Boat Ready checks your own boat, any length and any engine, free.

Check your boat in Boat Ready

The list for the basic boat

Worked out for a motorboat with an outboard motor, on inland waters, in daylight, 4 people aboard, no closed space where fuel vapour can collect, and no installed toilet. The sections after it say what changes.

Registration and numberingRequired

Certificate of number aboard

A boat with propulsion machinery of any type, an electric motor included, must be numbered by your state. Carry the certificate of number aboard, on paper or in digital form. Display the number on each side of the forward half in plain vertical block characters at least 3 inches high, in a contrasting colour, with the validation sticker beside it as your state directs. A Coast Guard documented boat is not state numbered under this rule.

33 CFR 173.11, 173.15, 173.21, 173.27 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-3 to 3-4

Life jacketsRequired

4 wearable life jackets

One Coast Guard approved wearable life jacket for each person aboard, 4 needed. Each must be serviceable, the right size for its wearer, legibly marked with its approval number, and readily accessible, not locked away or buried under gear. A life jacket whose label sets conditions, such as a Type V that must be worn, counts only when used the way its label says. Inflatables are approved only for the ages printed on them.

33 CFR 175.15, 175.19, 175.21 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-5 to 3-12

Throwable flotationRequired

1 throwable device

One throwable device (a Type IV cushion, ring buoy or horseshoe, or a device labelled with Type IV performance) in addition to the wearables, immediately available rather than stowed. Required at 16 ft and over, except canoes and kayaks.

33 CFR 175.15(b), 175.17(a), 175.19(b) · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-5

Children under 13Required in some cases

Worn by any child under 13

If a child under 13 is aboard while underway, the child must wear a Coast Guard approved life jacket unless below decks or in an enclosed cabin. Where your state sets its own age for children, the state rule applies instead on its waters.

33 CFR 175.15(c), 175.25 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-6

Visual distress signalsRequired in some cases

Not federally required here

Not federally required on inland waters. Required on coastal waters: the Great Lakes, the territorial seas, and waters connected to them out to the first point where the shores narrow to 2 nautical miles apart.

33 CFR 175.101, 175.105 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-12

Fire extinguishersRequired

3 x 5-B, or 1 x 20-B and 1 x 5-B

Carry 3 x 5-B, or 1 x 20-B and 1 x 5-B portable extinguishers. Each one must be: • On board and readily accessible, and an approved type • Not expired, and not used • Gauge in the operable range, pin in place, nozzle clear • Free of significant corrosion or damage Model year 2018 and newer: 5-B or 20-B rated units with a date stamp. Model year 2017 and older may keep serviceable B-I (counts as 5-B) or B-II (counts as 20-B) units. The Coast Guard treats a disposable extinguisher as expired 12 years after the manufacture date stamped on it.

33 CFR 175.310, 175.320, 175.380

Sound signalsRequired

Whistle or horn

A whistle (a horn that meets the rule). A bell is not required below 20 m (65.6 ft).

33 CFR 83.33 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-20

Anchor ballRequired in some cases

One black ball, if you anchor by day

At anchor by day, show one black ball forward where it can best be seen. Not required under 20 m (65.6 ft) in a Coast Guard special anchorage area. Under 20 m a ball smaller than the full 0.6 m size is allowed, in keeping with the size of the boat. It is a daytime rule, so it applies even if you never go out after dark. At night the anchor light takes its place.

33 CFR 83.30(a), (e), (g); 84.06

Oil discharge placardRequired

5 x 8 inch oil placard

Required at 26 ft and over. A durable placard at least 5 x 8 inches stating that discharging oil is prohibited, fixed in a conspicuous place in the engine space or at the bilge pump control.

33 CFR 155.450 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-24

Garbage placardRequired

8 x 5 inch garbage placard

Required at 26 ft and over. A durable, legible placard at least 8 inches wide by 5 inches high (20 x 12.5 cm), where crew and passengers can read it, stating the garbage discharge rules: no plastic into any water, other garbage only where allowed, the penalties, and that state and local limits may also apply.

33 CFR 151.51(e), 151.59

Navigation RulesRequired

A copy of the Rules aboard

At 12 m (39.4 ft) and over the operator must carry a copy of the Inland Navigation Rules aboard for ready reference.

33 CFR 83.01(g) · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-27

State and local requirementsSet by your state

Check your state boating agency

Your state may require more than this list: a fire extinguisher where federal rules do not, a different age for children's life jackets, registration of a boat with no engine, or a boating safety certificate. This app covers FEDERAL carriage requirements only.

varies by state

If the boat is used between sunset and sunrise or in poor visibility

Navigation lightsRequired

Sidelights, masthead and stern

From sunset to sunrise and in restricted visibility. Underway, a power-driven boat shows: • Red and green sidelights • A white masthead light (225°) forward • A white sternlight (135°) At anchor: • One all-round white light where it can best be seen • Not required under 20 m (65.6 ft) in a Coast Guard special anchorage area

33 CFR 83.21, 83.23, 83.30 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-21 to 3-24

If the boat is used on coastal waters or the Great Lakes

Visual distress signalsRequired

Day and night signals, e.g. 3 red flares

Day and night signals, at all times. 3 approved hand-held or aerial red flares cover both. Or combine day signals (1 orange distress flag, or 3 orange smoke signals) with a night signal (1 electric distress light). Readily accessible, marked with the approval number, and within the date on them. Expired flares may stay aboard as extras but do not count. Exception: while competing in an organized marine parade, regatta, race, or similar event, only night signals are required, carried between sunset and sunrise.

33 CFR 175.110(a), 175.115(a), 175.125, 175.130 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-12 to 3-14

Waste management planRequired in some cases

Written plan, if you go offshore

Required on a boat of 40 ft or more with a galley and berthing that is documented or state numbered and ever goes beyond the territorial sea (12 nautical miles out). A written plan saying how garbage is collected, stored, processed and discharged, naming the person in charge of it.

33 CFR 151.05, 151.51(d), 151.57

If the boat has a closed space where fuel vapour can collect, or a permanent fuel tank

VentilationRequired in some cases

Vents if a gas tank is built in

For a gasoline outboard: a closed compartment holding a permanently installed gasoline tank needs natural ventilation (cowls and ducts to the lower part of the space) unless it is open to the air. A portable tank in a seat locker does not by itself call for it.

33 CFR 175.201, 183.620 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-18

If the boat has an inboard gasoline engine

VentilationRequired

Working blower and vents

Each compartment with a permanently installed gasoline engine must be open to the air or have an exhaust blower, with the label near the ignition switch: run the blower for 4 minutes before starting. A compartment with a permanently installed fuel tank needs natural ventilation (cowls and ducts) unless it is open. Boats built after 31 July 1980 must keep the system the builder fitted working; older boats need two ducts with cowls to each engine and tank space.

33 CFR 175.201, 183.605, 183.610, 183.620; 46 CFR 25.40-1 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-18

Backfire flame controlRequired

Flame arrestor on each gas engine

Every gasoline engine installed after 25 April 1940, except outboards, needs an acceptable means of backfire flame control, usually a flame arrestor on the carburetor or throttle body. Look for a Coast Guard approval number (162.015, 162.041 or 162.042), or an SAE J-1928 or UL 1111 marking. Keep it clean and secured.

46 CFR 25.35-1 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-19

If the boat has an installed toilet

Marine sanitation deviceRequired

Certified MSD

An installed toilet needs an operable, certified marine sanitation device: Type II or III, or Type I at this length. In a No Discharge Area, secure it so nothing can go overboard, for example seacock closed with the handle removed, or padlocked or wire-tied shut.

33 CFR 159.7 · COMDTINST M16796.8A p. 3-26
