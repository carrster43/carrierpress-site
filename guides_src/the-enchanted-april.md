# A Reader's Guide

## Questions for reflection and discussion
1. The novel argues that a change of place can produce a change of heart. Do you believe beauty and rest can genuinely remake a person, or does the castle only give the women permission to become who they already were?
2. Each of the four women arrives wanting something different: escape, solitude, memory, happiness. Which of them did you identify with most, and did your sympathies shift as the month went on?
3. Lotty Wilkins seems to grow almost clairvoyant about other people's contentment. Is her gift believable to you, and what is von Arnim suggesting about how happiness spreads?
4. Mrs. Fisher begins as the most closed and forbidding of the four. Why do you think von Arnim gives her one of the novel's most moving transformations?
5. The men arrive later and are changed in turn. What is the novel saying about marriage, and about what husbands and wives fail to see in each other at home?
6. San Salvatore is almost a character in the book. How does von Arnim make the setting act on the people, and where did the descriptions most work their spell on you?
7. This is a comedy with a serious argument underneath it. What is that argument, and does the lightness of the telling strengthen it or soften it?
8. The novel was written in the shadow of the First World War, in a tired and grey London. How does that context change the way you read the women's hunger for color and warmth?
9. "The Enchanted April" has been prescribed for a century as a comfort read. What makes a book comforting, and what does this one do that a lesser feel-good story would not?
10. If you could take your own enchanted month, where would you go, who would you take, and what in your ordinary life are you hoping it would change?

## Elizabeth von Arnim: a brief timeline
- **1866** Born Mary Annette Beauchamp in Sydney, Australia; taken to London as a small child.
- **1880s** Studies organ at the Royal College of Music.
- **1891** Marries Count Henning von Arnim-Schlagenthin; becomes Countess von Arnim, living on his Prussian estate.
- **1898** Publishes *Elizabeth and Her German Garden* anonymously; it is an instant bestseller and gives her the name "Elizabeth."
- **1910s** Widowed; has a three-year affair with H. G. Wells.
- **1916** Marries Frank Russell, second Earl Russell (brother of Bertrand Russell); the marriage is unhappy and they separate.
- **1921** Rents Castello Brown above Portofino and writes *The Enchanted April* there.
- **1922** Publishes *The Enchanted April* to wide success; it is credited with making Portofino fashionable.
- **1930** Settles in the south of France, at a house she calls Mas des Roses.
- **1941** Dies of influenza in Charleston, South Carolina, aged 74.
