# A Reader's Guide

*These questions are spoiler-light and safe to read after finishing the novel. The frankest discussion of the ending is reserved for the Afterword.*

## Questions for reflection and discussion
1. *The Murder of Roger Ackroyd* is famous for a single audacious stroke. Setting the ending aside, what made the book absorbing before you reached it? How much of its power is the trick, and how much is simply good storytelling?
2. Dr. Sheppard narrates the story in a mild, ironic, companionable voice. How did you feel about him as a guide through the mystery? Did your sense of him change by the end, and again on reflection afterward?
3. Caroline Sheppard runs an intelligence network that rivals any detective's. Christie later called her a forerunner of Miss Marple. What makes the observant village spinster such a powerful figure in this kind of fiction?
4. Poirot is introduced here in retirement, growing vegetable marrows and reluctant to return to work. How does that framing change your sense of him? Is the great detective more appealing when he is a little diminished?
5. King's Abbot is a small, closed, gossiping community. How does the village setting shape the mystery, and why has this kind of place become the natural home of the English detective novel?
6. The novel plays scrupulously by the rules of "fair play," giving the reader every clue. Did you feel you had a genuine chance to solve it? On reflection, were you treated fairly, or did you feel misled?
7. The floor plans are presented as evidence, not decoration. Did you study them as you read? What does it say about the golden-age mystery that it asks the reader to think like an investigator, diagram and all?
8. When the solution was revealed, what was your immediate reaction, delight, outrage, disbelief, admiration? The book's first readers split sharply on exactly this point. Where do you land, and why?
9. *Ackroyd* provoked a lasting argument about what a detective story is allowed to do. Do you think a mystery has an unwritten contract with its reader? If so, did Christie honor it or break it?
10. Having finished the book, would you reread it knowing the secret? What do you expect a second reading would reveal that the first could not?

## Agatha Christie: a brief timeline
- **1890** Born Agatha Mary Clarissa Miller on 15 September in Torquay, Devon, England.
- **1914** Marries Archibald Christie; works as a hospital dispenser during the First World War, learning the pharmacology that informs her fiction.
- **1920** Publishes *The Mysterious Affair at Styles*, introducing Hercule Poirot.
- **1923–24** *The Murder on the Links*; *The Man in the Brown Suit*; *Poirot Investigates*.
- **1925** *The Secret of Chimneys*, introducing Superintendent Battle.
- **1926** Publishes *The Murder of Roger Ackroyd*, which makes her internationally famous; in December, amid the breakdown of her first marriage, she disappears for eleven days in a case that becomes a national sensation.
- **1928** Divorces Archibald Christie; travels on the Orient Express to the Middle East.
- **1930** Marries the archaeologist Max Mallowan; introduces Miss Marple in *The Murder at the Vicarage*.
- **1934** *Murder on the Orient Express*, another landmark Poirot novel.
- **1952** *The Mousetrap* opens in London and becomes the longest-running play in the world.
- **1971** Created a Dame of the British Empire.
- **1976** Dies on 12 January at Wallingford, Oxfordshire, the best-selling novelist in history.

## If you enjoyed The Murder of Roger Ackroyd
Read on into the great run of Poirot novels that followed, above all *Murder on the Orient Express* (1934) and *And Then There Were None* (1939), another Christie landmark built on a single unforgettable idea. To meet the village sleuth Caroline Sheppard helped inspire, begin with *The Murder at the Vicarage* (1930), the first Miss Marple novel.
