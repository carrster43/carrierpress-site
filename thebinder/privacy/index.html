The Binder privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026.

The Binder is the if-something-happens file: the things the people you leave behind will need to find. This policy explains what is kept.

What this version does, and what it does not

Stated plainly because the alternative would be misleading about the most important thing in the product. This version is a vault and a checklist that you keep. It does not yet release anything to anybody automatically, and nothing is sent to a named person on your death in this version.

Automatic staged release to named people, on verified death, is planned for a later version. Until it ships, somebody has to know this exists and how to get into it, or the file will not be found. Tell at least one person.

What is stored

  • Your email address, and whether you have paid.
  • Your vault, and the sections in it, with whatever you have written into each.

What goes in the sections is entirely your choice, and people put serious things in a file like this. Treat it as one of the most sensitive records you keep, because it probably is.

Please do not put passwords in it

Worth saying directly. The Binder is designed for the things a person needs to know: where documents are, who to call, what you wanted. It is not a password manager, is not built to the standard one is built to, and passwords stored here would sit in ordinary database rows. Use a password manager for those, and use The Binder to say which one you use.

What is not stored

No password for the app itself, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to your email address. We do not ask for a Social Security number, a will, an account number or a payment card, and none of those are fields.

Who can see it

You, and nobody else using the app. Every table checks the signed in account against the owner of the row before returning anything. There is no executor view and no sharing feature in this version. We do not sell any of it, we do not send it to advertisers, and nothing in it is sent to a language model.

Who else is involved

Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes. This app has no in-app purchase in this version, so nothing about a payment or a subscription reaches Apple or anyone else.

The web version

The Binder in a browser has no account and no database. Everything above about your email address, sign in codes, our database host and deleting an account is about the phone app.

Your binder stays in your browser, encrypted. Before anything you write is saved, it is encrypted in your browser with a key worked out from your passphrase. What is kept, in that browser's storage for carrierpress.com on that device, is only the encrypted binder, the values needed to check a passphrase against it, and the date you last downloaded the encrypted copy, so the app can remind you when it is old. Your passphrase and its key are never saved anywhere: they are held in the page's memory while it is open, and reloading the page, or leaving it hidden for two minutes, forgets them. Nothing you write is sent to us or to anyone else, and no part of it reaches a server. Another browser or device cannot see it, and clearing the site's data in your browser erases it. Browsers also delete what websites save on their own: Safari does it after about a week of use without visiting the page. That is why the encrypted copy you download is the real backup, and why the app offers it when you finish a section. Once your binder is open, the page asks your browser to keep its storage; the browser may say no.

Nothing is released to anyone. The web version does not watch for a death, holds no list of people to send anything to, and sends nothing to anybody, automatically or otherwise. Tell one person you trust where the Binder is and where the passphrase is written down.

Copies. On Account, Download the encrypted copy saves the encrypted binder as a file on your device; it opens only in The Binder, with your passphrase. Restore from an encrypted copy reads such a file in your browser and adds the sections that are not already there; it never replaces one, and the file is not sent anywhere. Download a printable copy saves a plain-text file of what you wrote, which is not encrypted: anybody who finds that file or its printout can read it.

No recovery. If you forget your passphrase, the binder cannot be opened, by you or by us, because we never have it or the key.

Your license key. The web version opens only with a license key, from your Gumroad receipt or a Carrier Press Access key. When you paste it on this app's page, it goes to a small server of ours on Cloudflare, which asks Gumroad whether the key is good and stores nothing. If it is, your browser keeps the key in an encrypted cookie that scripts on the page cannot read, and about once a day the server asks Gumroad again, so that access ends if a purchase is refunded. The key never expires otherwise. The server keeps no list of keys or visitors. Remove my key from this browser, on the app's page, or Forget my Gumroad key in this browser in the app, deletes it.

Payments are processed by Gumroad. The Binder never receives or stores your payment details. carrierpress.com is served through Cloudflare, which handles your IP address and each request the way any web host does.

To delete it, open Account, then Erase the binder from this browser. Nothing in your binder is held anywhere else to delete.

Deleting it

Deleting your account removes your profile, the vault and everything written in it, immediately and unrecoverably. There is no copy anywhere else, which is the point of it and also the risk.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
