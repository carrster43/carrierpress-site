The conventional direction of buoyage

“Returning from sea” means travelling this way, roughly clockwise around the country:
Southerly along the Atlantic Coast
Florida to Texas along the Gulf Coast
Northerly along the Pacific Coast
Westerly and northerly in the Great Lakes. Lake Michigan runs southerly down its eastern side and northerly up its western side
Numbers on red and green marks increase as you go in the conventional direction, and on a river that is usually upstream. Where you are unsure, the chart settles it.
33 CFR 62.21; Boat Crew Training Handbook A.2 and A.6; USCG Light List preface.
A buoy marks a charted position, and it can drag, sink or go off station, the handbook's phrase is that it may not be 'watching properly'. Aids to navigation should never be relied on exclusively. This app tells you what a mark MEANS; it cannot see your chart, your position, or which way you are heading, so it cannot tell you where the channel is.
A reference tool, not an aid to navigation and not a substitute for a chart. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the United States Coast Guard or the Coast Guard Auxiliary. Covers the US Aids to Navigation System (IALA-B), also used across the Americas, Japan, South Korea and the Philippines. Most of the rest of the world uses IALA-A, where the red and green sides are REVERSED. One US exception: US possessions in the Pacific west of the International Date Line and south of 10 degrees north follow IALA-A (33 CFR 62.21(a)).
