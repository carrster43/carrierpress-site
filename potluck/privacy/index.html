Potluck privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026.

Potluck is neighbourhood-scale organising and lending, geofenced to a few blocks. This policy explains what is kept and who sees it.

Your neighbours see your name and what you offer

This is the part that matters most, so it is here rather than at the bottom. Everyone in your neighbourhood sees the display name you chose, anything you offer to lend, and anything you organise or say you are coming to. That is the app working as intended.

They do not see your email address, and they do not see your street address. Where you live is used to decide which neighbourhood you belong to and is not shown to other members.

Why an address is needed at all

Membership is address verified, because a neighbourhood tool that anyone could join from anywhere is not a neighbourhood tool. We never receive your address and never store it. The address lives on the invite, issued by the neighbourhood association or municipal office that already holds it: they posted the code to that address, and claiming the code is what proves you receive post there. Your membership records the date it was verified and nothing else. There is no address column in this app's database, which is deliberate, because a list of names, faces and street addresses for a few blocks is exactly the database this app's users should be most afraid of.

You can choose the display name your neighbours see. If you would rather they did not see your full legal name, use something else.

Your door on the October map, and the one time we ask where you are

For October only, a household can add its own door to a map of doors handing out treats on Halloween, with what it offers (allergy-safe treats, a bowl on the step, no steps, no jump scares), the hours, and a description if it wants one. That point, placed by the household itself, is the only map position anywhere in Potluck.

  • Only verified neighbours in your neighbourhood can see it, the same people who can see your posts. Somebody you have blocked cannot see it.
  • Location is asked for once, when you save your door, to check you are standing within 60 metres of it. That is what stops somebody putting a pin on a neighbour's house. Looking at the map never asks. Nothing runs in the background and nothing keeps a trail of where you have been.
  • We keep the point of your door, not where your phone was. It is deleted two days after your hours end. There is no map in November.
  • The map is drawn by the app itself. No map company (Apple, Google or anyone else) is sent the location of any door.
  • There is no way to mark a house to avoid. Only doors that added themselves appear.

Reporting and blocking

Moderation shipped with the first version rather than being added later. If you report someone, what you wrote is visible to whoever reviews it, not to the person reported. If you block someone, they are not told. A report about a door on the October map hides it straight away, whatever the reason, until the neighbourhood's steward looks.

What is stored

  • Your email address, display name and address, and the neighbourhood you belong to.
  • Things you organise, who has said they are coming, and what people are bringing. For a gathering that repeats, which weeks you said you are coming.
  • Items you offer to lend, and the loans made.
  • Reports and blocks, so moderation works.
  • In October, your door on the map, if you add it, as above.

What is not stored

No password, because there is not one. Signing in is by a code sent to your email address. No phone number, no location tracking of any kind, and no payment details. The one location check, when a household saves its October door, keeps nothing about where the phone was. Potluck is free to neighbours.

Who else is involved

Supabase hosts the database and sends the sign in codes. Nothing is sent to a language model or to any other company, and we do not sell anything to anyone.

Deleting it

Deleting your account removes your profile, your address, your membership and your door on the October map if you added one, immediately. Things you organised that other people came to remain in the neighbourhood's record, without your name attached, because they were shared events rather than only yours.

On your phone

The app keeps your sign in on your phone, in the phone's secure keychain, so you stay signed in between launches. It also keeps the small rating note described below.

The rating prompt and Contact support

After something in the app has gone well, it may now and then ask Apple to show the App Store's own rating sheet. So that it asks rarely, it keeps one small note on your device: how many times things have gone well, the last two days they did, and the app version it last asked on. Nothing you entered is in that note, and it never leaves the device. Whatever you choose in the rating sheet is handled by Apple.

Contact support opens your own mail app with a message addressed to support@carrierpress.com. The subject line carries the app's name, its version and your phone's system version, so we know which build you mean. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app is not told whether you did.
