Weird fiction and cosmic horror

Classic weird fiction and cosmic horror, restored

Weird fiction is the horror of the thing you cannot quite see. The shelf below is where that tradition was built: a play that ruins its readers, a city that should not exist, an island that has noticed two campers. Most of these books are over a century old and still do their work in a few quiet pages.

Start with The King in Yellow, Robert W. Chambers's 1895 book of linked tales and the most-read title in the Carrier Press catalogue. Lovecraft built on it, and the Yellow Sign and Carcosa reached a new audience when True Detective went looking for them. Every edition here is newly set and annotated, with an introduction, headnotes and a glossary, so the old language and the old references stop being a barrier.

Questions about weird fiction and these editions

Where should I start with cosmic horror classics?

Start with The King in Yellow, then read Carcosa, which gathers the Bierce tales Chambers borrowed his names from beside his own Yellow Sign tales. After those, the shelf runs through Chambers's The Mystery of Choice, Machen's The Great God Pan, Blackwood's The Willows, M. R. James's ghost stories and Meyrink's The Golem.

What is in the Carrier Press edition of The King in Yellow?

It is an annotated edition of Robert W. Chambers's book. It has an Introduction with a map of how to read the collection, headnotes for the stories, a glossary of the Yellow Mythos with 17 entries, and 11 new illustrations. On Amazon it is $14.99 in paperback and $23.99 in hardcover (prices as of November 1, 2026).

What is Carcosa, and where does it come from?

Carcosa is the lost city at the heart of The King in Yellow. Chambers took the name, and Hali, from Ambrose Bierce's tales. The Carrier Press Carcosa gathers seven Bierce and Chambers pieces in publication order, with a dated headnote for each piece and Lovecraft's essay on Bierce and Chambers reprinted.

Are these editions just the free public domain texts?

No. The works are in the public domain, but each edition here is newly set with its own cover and new apparatus written for it, such as an introduction, headnotes, a glossary and notes. The page for each title lists what that edition adds.
