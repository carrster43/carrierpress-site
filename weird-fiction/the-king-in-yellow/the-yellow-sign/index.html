The King in Yellow

What is the Yellow Sign?

In The King in Yellow (1895), the Yellow Sign is a symbol tied to the forbidden play. In the story “The Yellow Sign” it is inlaid in gold on a clasp of black onyx. Chambers never describes its shape. To have it, or perhaps only to have seen it, is to be found.

What the book actually says

The narrator of “The Yellow Sign” opens a small box: “On the pink cotton inside lay a clasp of black onyx, on which was inlaid a curious symbol or letter in gold. It was neither Arabic nor Chinese, nor, as I found afterwards, did it belong to any human script.” That is nearly all Chambers gives you. He names the Sign and never draws it.

The story itself is the strongest in the book: an artist, his model, a watchman in the churchyard opposite, and a dream of a hearse that both of them keep having. Lovecraft counted it among the greatest weird tales ever written.

The spiral is not Chambers's

The three armed spiral most people now call the Yellow Sign was designed by Kevin Ross for a 1989 role-playing game. It is a fine design, and it is not in the book. Neither is Hastur as a tentacled Great Old One (that came from August Derleth), Yhtill as a city (James Blish), or Carcosa as a ruin in Louisiana (True Detective). Each one shows how far the book has travelled. None of them is in it.

The Yellow Mythos, A to Z

Aldebaran
The red star in Taurus that follows the Hyades. Cassilda's song sets the play beneath it.
Camilla
A character in the play, who asks the Stranger to unmask and gets the book's most quoted answer.
Carcosa
The lost city on the Lake of Hali, under twin suns and black stars, where the King reigns. Chambers took the name from Ambrose Bierce, for whom it was a ruin. See where the names come from.
Cassilda
A character in the play and the singer of Cassilda's song, the largest piece of the play the book lets you read. It opens the book.
Hali
The lake of cloud waves beside Carcosa. In Bierce, Hali was a sage quoted in an epigraph; Chambers made the sage into a lake.
Hastur
In Bierce, a kindly god of shepherds. In Chambers, a word that will not hold still: a place, a person or a power in one story, a falconer's name in another.
The Hyades
A star cluster in Taurus, the rainy stars of Greek poetry. In the play, their songs die unheard in Carcosa.
The King in Yellow (the play)
A drama in two acts, banned and burned. The first act is harmless bait; no one who starts the second can stop. Chambers quotes perhaps a dozen lines of it.
The King in Yellow (the figure)
The tattered King himself, whose scalloped robes must never be lifted. No one in the book who sees him clearly survives to describe him.
The Pallid Mask
From the play: the mask that is no mask.
The Phantom of Truth
A figure from the play, named in “The Repairer of Reputations” and in the shared dream of “The Yellow Sign.”
The Stranger
The unmasked visitor at the play's masquerade.
Yhtill
Named once, in “The Repairer of Reputations,” as the thing the King's tatters must hide forever. Chambers never explains it.
