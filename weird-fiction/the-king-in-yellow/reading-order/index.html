The King in Yellow

The King in Yellow reading order

Read all ten stories in the order Chambers set them, starting with “The Repairer of Reputations.” If you only want the weird tales, they are the first four. If you read only one, make it “The Yellow Sign.”

The ten stories, in order

  1. The Repairer of ReputationsNew York in 1920, which was the future when Chambers wrote it: government suicide chambers in the parks, and a narrator just out of an asylum who is sure he is heir to the Imperial Dynasty of America. Trust nothing he tells you.
  2. The MaskParis. A sculptor has found a solution that turns living things into flawless marble. Around it, three friends and a tender love triangle, and the play's most famous line.
  3. In the Court of the DragonThe shortest of the weird tales. A man who has spent the night reading the play sits in a Paris church and notices the organist looking at him with hatred. Read it in one sitting.
  4. The Yellow SignAn artist, his model, a church watchman with a face like a grave worm, a recurring dream of a hearse, and a small black clasp. The closest Chambers lets you come to the King.
  5. The Demoiselle d'YsA sudden change of air: an American lost on the moors of Brittany is rescued by a young woman whose French is three centuries old. The gentlest story in the book.
  6. The Prophets' ParadiseEight short prose poems, the hinge of the book, where the fever of the weird tales starts to dissolve into the Paris stories.
  7. The Street of the Four WindsA poor artist in a Latin Quarter garret follows a battered cat home. The last time the King's shadow falls across the book; watch for it.
  8. The Street of the First ShellThe longest story, and straight historical fiction: art students starving through the Prussian siege of Paris in the winter of 1870 to 1871.
  9. The Street of Our Lady of the FieldsThe most autobiographical story: American art students in Paris and a love affair Victorian reviewers found too frank. Read it beside the next one.
  10. Rue BarréeThe book closes on unrequited love, and on the discovery that some doors stay shut.

Two books under one cover

First time readers deserve fair warning: the book changes key halfway. The first four stories circle the play at closer and closer range. Then comes a gentle Breton ghost story, then the prose poems, and then four stories of art students in Paris with almost no King at all.

Read them anyway, and in order. The Paris stories share the book's real subject, young people at a threshold they cannot step back across, and the last glimpse of yellow sits exactly where the two halves meet.

What to read after it

Next, Carcosa, which prints the two Ambrose Bierce stories Chambers took his names from, then the four Chambers stories that used them, in the order they were published, so you can watch the mythology being put together. Then The Mystery of Choice, Chambers back in weird country two years later. The weird fiction shelf carries the reading order on from there.
